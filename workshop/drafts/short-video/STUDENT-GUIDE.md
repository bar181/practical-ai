# Short video guide: student guide

By the end you will have one finished short video and a repeatable way to make the next one.

## Before you start

You need an idea and about 30 minutes. Which accounts you need depends on the route you choose in
step 2. Tool limits and prices change often. The facts below were checked on 2026-11-14.

## Step 1: Write the script first

Every route starts from words. Fix the words before you generate anything, because renders cost
time or credits.

Use the four-beat format in [`level-simple/script-template.md`](level-simple/script-template.md).
A 20-second video is about 50 spoken words. Read it aloud and time it.

Rules that hold for every route:

1. **One idea per video.** A short that makes two points makes none.
2. **Earn the first two seconds.** Open on the hook, not a logo or a greeting.
3. **Assume the sound is off.** Put captions on screen.
4. **A person watches it all the way through before it is posted.** Every time.

## Step 2: Choose your route

| Route | Use when | Length | Made with | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Generated clip | You need an eye-catching moment: a hook, a cutaway, a product shot | Under 8 seconds | A text-to-video generator such as Google Gemini | Striking and short. Hard to keep consistent across clips |
| B. Free explainer | You have a document, notes or a topic to explain | About 1 to 5 minutes | A notebook tool that turns sources into a narrated video, such as NotebookLM | Fast and free within a daily cap. You steer it, you do not direct it. The narrator is not you |
| C. Avatar short | You want yourself on screen, in your own voice, without filming | About 20 seconds | A still image of yourself, an avatar tool, a clone of your own voice | Most personal. Needs a one-time setup and a careful review each time |
| D. Motion graphics | You want exact layouts, numbers and text, and you will reuse the design | Any | Code-driven video, built by a coding assistant | Precise and repeatable. No realistic footage |

Two limits worth planning around:

- Generated clips stop at about 8 seconds each. Anything longer is several clips cut together.
- Photo-based avatars hold up best under 60 seconds.

## Step 3: Follow the steps for your route

**Route A, generated clip**

1. Write one sentence describing the shot: subject, action, setting, camera move, mood.
2. Generate. Change one thing per retry, so you learn what made the difference.
3. Save the prompt that worked. That collection becomes your template.
4. Need more than 8 seconds? Plan separate shots and cut between them.

**Route B, free explainer**

1. Put the source material in one notebook: documents, notes, links.
2. Choose the format by where it will be watched: a full explainer, or a short vertical version.
3. Add a steering note: who it is for, the one takeaway, what to skip.
4. Generate, then watch it through. Tighten the note and regenerate if it wandered.

**Route C, avatar short**

1. Write the script and time it.
2. Create a still image of yourself. Keep the framing, clothing, background and lighting settings so every video matches.
3. One-time setup: create your avatar and your voice clone from your own footage and recordings.
4. Generate a few seconds as a test. Check the voice and the lip sync.
5. Render the full piece, add captions, watch it through.

**Route D, motion graphics**

1. Write the script and mark what appears on screen at each beat.
2. Ask a coding assistant to build it as code-driven video in your colours and font.
3. Review the preview, adjust the timing, render.
4. Keep the project. It is your reusable layout.

## Step 4: Check before you post

- [ ] One idea, stated in the first two seconds
- [ ] Captions readable on a phone
- [ ] Every fact in it is true and is yours to state
- [ ] Watched from start to end by a person
- [ ] Marked as AI-generated where the platform or the audience expects it

## Guardrails

- **Your own face and voice only**, or someone who has agreed in writing. Never clone another person.
- **Nothing posts itself.** The process ends at a file that a person approves.
- **Watch for invented facts.** A generated explainer can say things your sources do not.

## Try it

1. Write a 20-second script on something you explained to someone this week.
2. Make it by route B and by one other route.
3. Note which took longer and which you would actually post.
