# Example: one full loop

Synthetic example. Everything here is invented for illustration.

## 1. The voice file (excerpt)

**Rules**

- Never open with a question.
- No exclamation marks.
- Never use "journey" or "unlock".

**Examples**

> I ruined three boards before I learned to measure twice. Nobody tells you the first skill in
> woodworking is patience. The second is a sharp pencil.

> Week 6 of running three times a week. I am not faster. I am just less surprised by hills.
> That counts.

> A rule I borrowed from a better writer: write the ugly version first. You cannot edit a blank
> page. You can absolutely edit a bad one.

**Facts I may state**

- I have been learning woodworking for one year.
- I run three times a week.

## 2. The request

> Point: beginners should buy fewer tools, not more. For people starting a hobby.

## 3. The draft

> I bought eleven tools in my first month of woodworking. I use four.
>
> The other seven taught me something, though. A new tool feels like progress. It is not. It is
> shopping.
>
> Buy the fifth tool when the fourth one stops you. Not before.

**Voice traits used**

- A specific number in the first line (as in the "three boards" example)
- Short sentences that end on a dry turn (as in "That counts.")
- Plain advice in the last line (as in "write the ugly version first")

**Unsure about**

- "Eleven tools" and "I use four" are not in your facts. Are these numbers true? If not, give me the real ones or I will rewrite without numbers.

## 4. What happened next

The numbers were not true, so the drafter was told the real ones and rewrote the first line. This
is the rule about invented facts doing its job: the draft flagged its own guess.

## 5. The feedback

> I like this. But never tell people what to buy.

**Line to add to the Rules section**

- Never tell people what to buy. Describe what I did and let them decide.
