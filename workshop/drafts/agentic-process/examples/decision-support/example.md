# Example: should an invented bakery start office catering?

This is what a good result looks like. Made on 16 November 2026 in Claude by a new chat that had
only [`SKILL.md`](SKILL.md) and the message below, with web search, and no way to ask questions.
The bakery is invented; the sources are real and were read that day. Nothing below the line is
edited. It was made with the current `SKILL.md`.

**The message** (after the pasted `SKILL.md`):

> Here is my business profile, then my question.
>
> Business profile: Harbour Lane Bakery. We sell bread, pastries and coffee to walk-in customers. Three shops in Hamilton, Ontario, 22 staff, about 1.4 million dollars in sales a year. Goals for the next twelve months: grow sales 10 percent, raise weekday afternoon sales, keep the team from burning out. Limits: up to 25,000 dollars to invest, no new building, the head baker already works six days. Risk: medium. My business partner has a say in big decisions.
>
> My question: should we start a weekday catering service for offices near our shops?

---

**Short answer:** Don't launch a full weekday catering service yet. Run a small, capped 8-week test of an afternoon office box from one shop. I couldn't find any evidence of how much office demand there is near your shops, and a full service would add early-morning and delivery work to a team you're trying to protect. Your first step: list the offices within about 1 km of each shop and ask 15 to 20 office managers or regular customers whether they'd order an afternoon box at about $8 a person.

# The brief

**Decision:** Should Harbour Lane Bakery start a weekday catering service for offices near its three Hamilton shops?

**Recommendation:** Don't launch a full service now. First, check demand with a few conversations. Then run a capped 8-week afternoon office-box pilot from one shop, with spending capped at $3,000 (proposal).

**Why:**
- I found no Hamilton office vacancy figure and no count of offices near your shops. A recent report says [downtown Hamilton office space has continued to struggle](https://blog.remax.ca/hamilton-commercial-real-estate-report/), so demand needs checking before you commit to a full service.
- Catering on its own is unlikely to deliver your 10% growth goal. That goal is about $2,700 more in sales every week (estimate). A capped pilot brings in at most about $1,000 a week (estimate).
- A full service adds early prep, packing and delivery work. That conflicts with your goal of protecting the team, and your head baker already works six days.

**SWOT (for the pilot):**

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|
| Existing products and three shops (profile). Spending capped at $3,000 (proposal). Afternoon orders use quieter hours (assumed). | Small sales, not enough for the 10% goal on its own (estimate). Adds coordination work (assumed). No delivery vehicle (option). |
| **Opportunities** | **Threats** |
| 84% of new Canadian job postings are fully on-site (source). National office vacancy is falling (source). Suburban Hamilton is in demand, Ancaster especially (source). Your bakery prices are in line with other local caterers (source). | Weak downtown office market (source). Grocery platters and about 20 local caterers compete (source). A courier costs about 11% of a $96 order (estimate). Food must stay at safe temperatures while it travels (source). |

**Risks and how to reduce them:**
1. *Staff strain:* cap the pilot at 2 orders a day, use only existing products, and stop if overtime appears (proposal).
2. *Thin margin after delivery:* set a minimum order and a delivery fee, or walk orders over within 1 km (proposal).
3. *Food safety in transit:* stick to baked goods and coffee, and keep anything with dairy or meat at 4°C or colder. Confirm the setup with Hamilton Public Health.

**Next steps (next 30 days):**
1. Agree the pilot cap and success test with your partner.
2. List offices near each shop and ask 15 to 20 office managers or regulars about interest. Pick the shop with the most interest.
3. Build a 3-item box menu and an order form. Call Hamilton Public Health and your insurance broker, then take the first orders.

**What would change this recommendation:**
- Fewer than 5 interested offices: don't run the pilot, and look for other ways to lift afternoon sales.
- At least 8 offices ordering and 4 reordering by week 8: expand to a second shop or a full service.
- Hiring a dedicated part-time catering person would lower the burnout risk of a full service.

**Assumptions:** offices are within walking or short-courier distance of at least one shop; there is spare staff time in weekday afternoons; pastries and coffee travel well; your partner must approve any spending.

# How I got there

## Step 1: Frame the decision

Should Harbour Lane start selling catering to offices near its shops on weekdays?

- **A. Do nothing:** stay with walk-in sales only.
- **B. Full weekday catering service (your idea, plainest form):** breakfast, lunch and meeting platters for offices near all three shops, ordered ahead and delivered by courier or your own staff, Monday to Friday.
- **C. Small test:** an 8-week pilot from one shop (proposal):
  - an "afternoon office box" of existing pastries, loaf slices or cookies, plus coffee carafes;
  - ordered by noon the day before, delivered or picked up between 2 and 4 pm;
  - at most 2 orders a day, priced at about $8 a person, with spending capped at $3,000;
  - made from the normal bake or by other staff, never as extra work for the head baker.

  The riskiest part of B is whether enough nearby offices will reorder. The pilot tests that cheaply.

**Estimates** (working shown):
- Weekly sales now: $1,400,000 ÷ 52 ≈ $26,900. A 10% goal is $140,000 a year ≈ $2,700 a week.
- One pilot box: 12 people × $8 = $96. The pilot cap is 2 a day × 5 days = 10 orders a week, so at most about $960 a week, or about $7,700 over 8 weeks.
- Courier for 3 km: $6.99 + 3 × $1.10 = $10.29, about 11% of a $96 order.
- Comparable prices: Pane Fresco croissants work out to $22.99 ÷ 12 ≈ $1.92 each, against $3.25 from the hospital caterer. So $8 a person for 2 to 3 items plus coffee sits within the range found.

**Scores against doing nothing, over 12 months:**

| Goal | B | C |
|---|---|---|

| Raise weekday afternoon sales | 0. Plain catering is mostly breakfast and lunch, and demand is **not found** | 0. Built for afternoons, but demand is **not found** |

**Leading option:** nothing scores above 0, so doing nothing leads. The missing fact (office demand near your shops) could change that, so I recommend the small test (C) as the check.

**Check first:** a few conversations before spending anything, with offices near each shop and with regulars who work in offices. Then the capped pilot. **Proposed success test:** by week 8, at least 8 offices have ordered and at least 4 have reordered, the average order is $80 or more, and no extra overtime has appeared.

**Judgement calls:**
1. *"Weekday afternoon sales"*
   - Reading taken: sales delivered between noon and 5 pm, Monday to Friday.
   - Other reading: any weekday sales.
   - Would it change the leading option? No, because demand is still unknown.
2. *"Up to $25,000 to invest"*
   - Reading taken: setup costs plus early losses.
   - Other reading: one-time equipment only.
   - Would it change the leading option? No.
3. *"Head baker already works six days"*
   - Reading taken: no added hours for the head baker.
   - Other reading: no added work for them at all.
   - Would it change the leading option? No, because C uses the existing bake and other staff.
4. *Demand scored 0 because it is not found.* If the check shows strong demand, B and C would score +1 on sales and lead. **This would change the answer**, which is why the check comes first.
5. *Burnout scored −1 for B.* If you hired a dedicated part-time catering person, B could score 0 here. It still wouldn't lead without demand evidence.
6. *Longer view:* if the shift back to the office holds, office catering could become a steady business-to-business sales channel and even out weekday production beyond 12 months. This supports running the test now. It doesn't change the 12-month answer.

**Professional advice needed:**
- Food safety setup and any notice for catering: Hamilton Public Health inspector.
- Liability and vehicle insurance if staff deliver in their own cars: insurance broker.
- HST on catering sales: your accountant.
