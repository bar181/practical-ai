# Training explainer: landscape review

What research says about teaching with video. Checked on 2026-11-15. The tool was drafted before
this review. This is the one tool in the repository whose practice rests on published studies,
and the review found both support and a caution.

## Summary

- **Break the lesson into pieces the learner controls.** This is the segmenting principle from
  Richard Mayer's work on multimedia learning. The tool's beat sheet already works this way.
- **Shorter holds attention better.** A large study of online course videos found the median time
  people kept watching topped out at about six minutes, however long the video was.
- **The caution: staying to the end is not the same as learning.** One critique points out that
  few studies connect video length to what people learned. One research group reports failing to
  repeat the segmenting result. The tool now says which claim it rests on.

## What the research says

| Finding | Detail | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Segmenting helps | People learn better from material delivered in learner-paced pieces than as one continuous block. A review of ten studies found the effect in all ten, with a median effect size of 0.79 | [Springer, 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10758-024-09745-2), [Forasoft](https://www.forasoft.com/learn/elearning-video/articles-elearning/pedagogy-of-video-attention-retention-chunking) |
| Length matters less than pieces | Segmented videos gave higher learning gains than the same video unsegmented. Length was not crucial as long as the video came in meaningful segments. This was a small study of 22 people | [Springer, 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10758-024-09745-2) |
| Watching time tops out near six minutes | Median engagement was at most about six minutes, regardless of total length, in a study of online course sessions | [University at Buffalo](https://www.buffalo.edu/catt/develop/tools/instructional-videos/cognitive-technical.html), [Forasoft](https://www.forasoft.com/learn/elearning-video/articles-elearning/pedagogy-of-video-attention-retention-chunking) |
| Point at what matters, cut what does not | On-screen cues that highlight the key point help. Interesting but irrelevant material hurts | [University at Buffalo](https://www.buffalo.edu/catt/develop/tools/instructional-videos/cognitive-technical.html), [eLearning Industry](https://elearningindustry.com/creating-better-video-for-learning-design-principles-for-processing-videos) |
| Teach the terms first | Complex material is easier when the names and parts are taught before the process | [eLearning Industry](https://elearningindustry.com/creating-better-video-for-learning-design-principles-for-processing-videos) |
| Speak plainly | People learn better from a conversational style than a formal one | [University at Buffalo](https://www.buffalo.edu/catt/develop/tools/instructional-videos/cognitive-technical.html) |
| Principles from other media may not all carry over to video | A systematic review found some hold for video, notably coherence, segmenting and learner control, and asks designers to test the rest | [Australasian Journal of Educational Technology](https://ajet.org.au/index.php/AJET/article/download/7296/1915) |

## The cautions

| Caution | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Much of the "short is better" evidence is about attention. Few studies tie length to learning, and where they did the results were largely null | [Education Week, 2014](https://www.edweek.org/leadership/opinion-short-videos-are-better-for-learning-right-maybe-not/2014/03) |
| One university group reports unpublished work that did not repeat the benefit of segmenting. This is a student thesis, not a published study | [UC San Diego honors thesis, 2020](https://cogsci.ucsd.edu/undergraduates/honors-program/Sheel,-Shreya_Segmenting-in-Multimedia-Learning_Final.pdf) |

## How this tool follows it

| Finding | In this tool |
| --- | --- |
| Segmenting | The lesson is planned as beats. The demo lets the viewer tap any beat and step through |
| Near six minutes | The demo lesson is three minutes. **Added after this review:** the skill splits any lesson planned at more than six minutes into lessons that each stand alone |
| Point at what matters, cut the rest | Each beat shows at most one image or one line of text |
| Teach the terms first | The teaching voices open with an analogy before the detail. Naming the terms first is **added** to the brief template as a question |
| Speak plainly | The voices are written to be spoken |
| The cautions | **Added after this review.** The student guide now says the six-minute figure is about how long people watch, and asks for two or three questions at the end of each lesson as the check on learning |

## Build or license

| Level | Lean | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple | Build nothing. A brief, the style cards and the skill | The plan is the valuable part |
| Working | License a narrated-explainer or avatar tool by route | Rendering is a solved problem |
| Automated | Review before building. A person approves every lesson | A wrong lesson teaches the wrong thing to everyone who watches it |
