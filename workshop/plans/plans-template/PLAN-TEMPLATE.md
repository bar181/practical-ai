# Tool name

One-line promise: what it does for the person using it.

| Complexity | Research | Level 1 | Draft |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, or a range | Not started, Partly, or Done with the date | Not started, Drafted, or Published | Link to `workshop/drafts/<slug>/`, or "None yet" |

**Think of it as:** one everyday analogy, in one or two sentences.

## Overview

Two to four sentences. What problem it solves and how. No jargon.

## Who it is for

One sentence.

## Scope

- **In:** what level 1 does.
- **Out:** what it deliberately does not do.
- **It works when:** one specific input and what a good result looks like.

## What might be involved at each level

Level 1 is described in most detail, because it is built first. The later levels are
outlines and will change.

A tool has a base, which holds everything, and up to three levels, each holding only what it adds.
The layout and the stages are in the [project method](../../../projects/agentic-process/).

| Level | What it involves | Complexity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base | One line: what a person can open and use with nothing set up | |
| Level 1, Beginner: the simplest version that works | Three or four sentences anyone can follow in any chat assistant: what they prepare, what they paste, what they get back, what they check | Beginner |
| Level 2, Advanced: repeatable | One line: the skill or saved project, and the connectors to where the work lives | Intermediate |
| Level 3, Expert: connected and automated | One line, or "None": a database, an MCP server, a schedule, agents doing the work with a person approving | Advanced |
| Explainer | One line: the page, written last, that helps people use the finished tool | Written after the tool works |
| Commercial grade | One line: what a commercial build adds | Explained, not built here |
