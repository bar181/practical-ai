# Friction

1. **BLOCKING.** "Recommend the option with the highest total." versus "If a fact that would decide between the options is "not found", recommend a short, capped trial". The instructions do not say whether the trial must be one of the options framed in step 1, or whether it overrides the scored winner. If step 1 frames only "full service" and "not now", the not-found rule recommends something that was never scored or limit-tested. I put the trial in as option B so both rules agree. A run that framed different options (for example "breakfast only" versus "full service") could recommend a different thing.

2. **MINOR.** "Drop any option that fails a limit." There is no rule for a limit you cannot test because the fact is not found (for example, whether full service fits in $25,000 when vehicle and staff costs were not found). I marked it "Unclear" and dropped option A on another limit. Here the result does not change: even kept, A ties B at +1 at best and the tie-break ("the smaller, easier step") and the not-found rule both pick B.

3. **MINOR.** "If you had to interpret the person's words, mark that line "assumed"". Almost every limit needs some reading: does "up to 25,000 dollars to invest" include ongoing wages for a catering cook or driver? Does "no new building" rule out a rented commissary kitchen? I marked the two limits I had to read as "assumed", but the line between reading and restating is not drawn.

4. **MINOR.** "a short trial", "a short, capped trial". No guidance on length, cap or success measure, so every trial number in the brief is a proposal I chose (8 weeks, $2,500, 5 orders a week). Another run would pick other numbers.

5. **MINOR.** "medium, a trial unless the facts are strong". "Strong" is not defined. It did not matter here because the not-found rule forces a trial anyway.

6. **MINOR.** "score each remaining option against each goal as better (+1), same (0) or worse (-1) than doing nothing". Doing nothing is itself one of the options, so it always scores 0; the instructions do not say whether to show it in the table. I showed it.

7. **MINOR.** The rule to mark interpreted words "assumed" covers only limits. Goals need interpreting too: does catering delivered at 2 pm count as "weekday afternoon sales"? I marked it "assumed" in the score reason anyway.

8. **MINOR.** "Strengths and weaknesses come from the profile or the option itself" versus "Each point says where it comes from: the profile, a source, an estimate, a proposal, or "assumed"". "The option itself" is not one of the allowed labels. I labelled such points "proposal".

9. **MINOR.** "opportunities and threats come from the research". A threat such as staff strain comes from the profile, not the research, so it had to go under weaknesses.

10. **MINOR.** "as a one-page brief they can check and share" and "Show the work for each step under its own heading". Showing six steps plus the brief makes the full answer several pages, and the brief "repeats the key points from the steps above", so the person reads most points twice. It is unclear whether the steps should be shown in full or briefly.

11. **MINOR.** "No legal or tax advice: say when a question needs a professional, and who." The research step also asks for "rules that apply". Quoting the food premises regulation sits near that line. I quoted the rule as a fact and sent the person to Hamilton Public Health, an insurance broker and an accountant for what it means for them.

12. **MINOR.** "tell them to save it as a file in this project". It assumes the person is working in a chat project with files; a plain chat has nowhere to save it.

Blocking: 1. Minor: 11.
