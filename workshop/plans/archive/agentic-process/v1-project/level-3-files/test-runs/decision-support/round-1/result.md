## Step 5. The brief

**Decision:** Should Harbour Lane Bakery start a weekday catering service for offices near its shops?

**Recommendation:** Run a capped 12-week pilot selling pre-ordered breakfast and afternoon meeting trays from one shop to nearby offices, before deciding on a full service.

**Why:**
- Office demand is improving: Ontario government workers are back five days a week (F2), and large workplace orders are growing faster than regular ones (F4).
- Afternoon meeting trays go straight at your goal of more weekday afternoon sales, using products you already make.
- A full launch puts more work on a head baker who already works six days, and one part-time helper could use about 75% of your $25,000 (E3).

|---|---|

**Risks and how to reduce them:**
1. **Head baker overload.** Build trays only from the normal daily bake, set a daily order cap and a 24 to 48 hour order deadline, and stop the pilot if the cap is broken.
2. **Too little demand near your shops.** Ask nearby offices before spending anything, and choose the shop with the most offices around it.
3. **Food safety, transport and insurance.** Before the first delivery, ask Hamilton Public Health what applies to moving trays to offices, and ask your insurance broker whether delivery by staff is covered. These need those professionals' answers, not mine.

**Next steps (this month):**
1. Agree the pilot rules with your business partner and head baker: which shop, which items, the daily cap, and a spending cap.
2. Ask Hamilton Public Health about catering and transporting food, and ask your insurance broker about delivery.
3. List the offices within a short walk of your chosen shop and ask five office managers whether they would order trays, how often and at what price.

**What would change this recommendation:**
- No firm daily cap agreed with the head baker or partner: choose not now.
- Few offices near any shop say they would order: choose not now.
- The pilot regularly fills its cap and a second baker fits the budget: plan a full launch.
- Public Health or insurance requirements cost more than the pilot can carry: choose not now.
