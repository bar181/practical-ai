# Friction with AI-INSTRUCTIONS.md

1. **BLOCKING.** "Score each option against each criterion as better, same or worse than doing nothing, with one line of reason. Recommend one option."
   - The instructions give no rule for turning the scores into a recommendation. There is no weighting and no tally rule, and nothing says what to do when the scores split.
   - Option C scores "better" on sales growth, the first goal. A reader who weights that goal most could pick C from the same table.
   - The three-point scale also hides size. Any spending scores "worse" on the budget criterion, whether it is $500 or $25,000.

2. **BLOCKING.** "Find the facts that would change the decision: demand, costs, prices, competitors, rules that apply. [...] A fact you could not find is written 'not found'."
   - The fact that matters most is how many offices near the shops would order, and it cannot be found on the web.
   - The instructions do not say whether to still recommend when a deciding fact is "not found", or to make finding it the recommendation.
   - I recommended a capped pilot, with "ask five offices" as the first step. Another reader might recommend "not now until demand is known", which would change the result.

3. **MINOR.** "Look for a business profile in this project's files or earlier in this chat."
   - The profile came in the same message as the question, not earlier in the chat. I treated it as found.

4. **MINOR.** "If there is none, ask these six questions [...] then write the answers as a profile under the heading 'Business profile' and tell the person to save it as a file in this project."
   - There is no instruction for a profile that arrives pasted in the message. It is unclear whether to write it up and tell the person to save it, so they are not asked again. I did neither.

5. **MINOR.** "SWOT for the leading option." (step 3) comes before "Compare and recommend." (step 4)
   - The leading option has to be picked before the scoring that is meant to justify it. Nothing says how to pick it.

6. **MINOR.** "Show the work for each step under its own heading."
   - Step 5 then asks for a SWOT again inside the brief, and step 4's "the condition under which you would recommend a different one" repeats in the brief's "What would change this recommendation".
   - It is unclear whether the brief should repeat this work or refer back to it. I repeated it in a compact form, which makes the answer long.

7. **MINOR.** "**The brief.** One page"
   - "One page" is not defined for a chat answer, especially with a sources list.
   - It is also unclear whether only the brief must be one page, or the whole reply.

8. **MINOR.** "Strengths and weaknesses come from the profile"
   - The profile describes the business, not the option. Some weaknesses of the option (no catering experience) are not in the profile, so I had to mark them "assumed". The rule allows "assumed" but says S and W "come from the profile".

9. **MINOR.** "Every fact gets a source and a date."
   - It does not say which date: publication, last updated, or accessed. One source (Ontario.ca) shows no page date. I used the publication date, or "no page date (accessed 16 Nov 2026)".

10. **MINOR.** "Never invent a figure. A number you work out yourself is labelled 'estimate' with the working shown."
    - It does not cover design choices for an option, such as the pilot length (12 weeks) and spending cap ($5,000). These are neither facts nor estimates. I labelled them as my proposals under Assumptions.

11. **MINOR.** "Name the criteria you will judge them by, taken from the goals and limits in the profile."
    - Risk appetite is a separate profile item, not a goal or a limit. I included it anyway.

12. **MINOR.** "No legal or tax advice: say when a question needs a professional, and who."
    - Step 2 asks for "rules that apply". The line between stating a researched rule (O. Reg. 493/17) and giving legal advice is not drawn. I stated the rule as a sourced fact and sent the transport and insurance questions to Hamilton Public Health and an insurance broker.

13. **MINOR.** "Next steps: the first three, each small enough to do this month."
    - On 16 November, "this month" leaves two weeks. It is unclear whether this means the calendar month or the next 30 days.

14. **MINOR.** "the person who should see this before it is decided."
    - This is singular, but the profile names a partner, and the head baker is directly affected. I named both.

15. **MINOR.** The research step sets no limit on how much to search, and no rule on sources from outside the country or currency.
    - Several of the best demand figures came from US or global sources (DoorDash, Bloomberg). I used them, labelled them as US sources and treated the price as US dollars.

Blocking: 2. Minor: 13.
