# Friction

1. **BLOCKING.** The person table says, for "An idea in a sentence, and they are new to this": "All nine steps in plain words." The short-path section says: "If the job is one person turning one kind of input into one result inside a chat (an email from notes, a summary of a document), offer the short path before step 1." This person fits both. One says nine steps, the other says offer the short path. I read the short path as the more specific rule and took it. This choice decides the shape of the whole result.

2. **BLOCKING.** "Use all nine steps if they say no, or if the tool needs files, other people or anything paid." The email goes to six staff. It is unclear whether "other people" means people who use the tool or anyone involved, including readers of the output. I read it as users of the tool (the staff only receive the email), so the short path held. The other reading forces all nine steps.

3. **MINOR.** "offer the short path before step 1" assumes the person answers. The list of what I may decide when I cannot ask ("which assistant, the tone, the length, a proposed wow") does not say whether the choice of path is one of them. I decided it and recorded it under Assumed.

4. **MINOR.** "End every step by writing down what was decided, in two or three lines, and asking the gate question" conflicts with the short path's "Steps 2 to 5 in one line each." I kept the one-line rule and put each gate answer under Assumed.

5. **MINOR.** "Step 7 is skipped. Step 8 does its job." It is unclear whether "its job" means step 8 does step 7's job (review) or simply does its own. I read it as: the fresh-chat test stands in for the review.

6. **MINOR.** RULES: "Use only facts from the person's message. Invent nothing." COACH: "Examples use invented data and say so", and step 1 requires "a specific input". A worked example cannot exist without invented notes. I followed the coach and labelled every invented line.

7. **MINOR.** Short path: "Steps 3 and 4: nothing to buy, the chatbot they already have." Step 4 asks for "every dependency of the first version with its price and its limit". Whether the person's ChatGPT is free or paid is not known, so its price and limit are unknown. I listed it as not checked and passed the gate on "buys nothing new".

8. **MINOR.** "In a chat with no access to files, write each file out in full under its path." The short path keeps the prompt "in a note they can copy from", which has no path. I named the phone note instead of giving a path.

9. **MINOR.** "One worked example ... Keep it beside the prompt, not inside it." The fresh-chat test needs the example notes pasted into the prompt's "My notes:" slot. I kept them as a separate block that is pasted in only at test time.

10. **MINOR.** The note heading "Decided" is not defined for a session where the person could not decide anything. I put the facts from their message under Decided and every choice I made under Assumed.

11. **MINOR.** Plan heading "The steps" could mean the nine coach steps or the steps the user follows (step 6). I used the user's steps.

12. **MINOR.** Step 1: "Ask what would make people stop and look." It does not say which people: the user, the team reading the email, or others. I wrote the wow for the user, since the user is the one who keeps using the tool.

13. **MINOR.** Step 2 short path: "if the result is good enough to send unchanged, stop", and the table says to hand over "a short guide to that thing and the note, in place of the plan". Because the plain-sentence try cannot be run here, the hand-over had to be the plan. If the person's try works, the plan should have been a guide instead. The instructions do not say how to hand over both possibilities.

Blocking: 2. Minor: 11.
