# Note: Monday sales email

Who has tried the first version: nobody. It is built, not used. I could not ask questions or search the web in this session.

## Decided

- One user: you. One job: last week's sales notes in, a draft Monday team email out.
- ChatGPT does the drafting, because you already use it.
- The first version is a block of instructions you paste. No code, nothing to install.
- You read every draft and send the email yourself. Nothing sends by itself.
- Three notes in one folder called "Monday email" on your phone.
- Unclear lines are left out of the email and listed as questions underneath.
- Names, prices and dates are kept exactly as typed.
- Step 9 was not run. The next three additions are listed in the plan anyway.

## Assumed

Every item here is something I would have asked you.

- Step 1 gate: you can say the job in one sentence, and my sentence is right: "On Monday I paste last week's sales notes into ChatGPT and get back a draft email for my team, which I check and send."
- The "wow": a ready-to-read email with nothing made up, plus a list of anything unreadable. This is my guess at what would make your team stop and look.
- The team wants a short, plain, friendly email that reads in about a minute, with things to act on first.
- Every clear line of your notes belongs in the email.
- You sign off with your first name.
- You write your notes and the email in English.
- Step 2 gate: nothing existing does the whole job. This is the biggest assumption. If ChatGPT can store these instructions for you, the answer leans towards yes.
- Step 3 gate: you build the instructions and buy nothing.
- Step 4 gate: you already have ChatGPT, a notes app and an email app, so there is nothing new to pay for and you accept that.
- Step 5 gate: a beginner could draw the design. Your notes app can hold a folder.
- Step 6 gate: the beginner version is fully described.
- Step 7: nobody else is available to read the plan, so I reviewed it myself.
- Step 8 gate: not assumed. It is open until someone runs the test.
