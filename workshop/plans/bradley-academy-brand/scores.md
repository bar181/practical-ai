# Scores for Bradley's site

Scored against the brand rubric by independent reviewer agents on 22 November 2026. Each agent
loaded the site in headless Chrome at 390 and 1280 pixels and played three readers: a research lead
at a US lab, a seed or Series A founder, and an enterprise engineering manager. No person has
scored it yet.

| Item | Old site | Loop 1 | Loop 2 | Final check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Positioning for labs and startups | 30 | 68 | 74 | 82 |
| Proof of engineering depth | 40 | 62 | 60 | 62 |
| Research signal | 30 | 66 | 60 | 70 |
| Credentials and credibility | 80 | 64 | 70 | 75 |
| Teaching and leadership | 75 | 78 | 80 | 85 |
| Calls to action | 20 | 55 | 55 | 72 |
| Craft of the site | 80 | 76 | 82 | 84 |
| Protection of the research | 70 | 82 | 85 | 88 |
| **Overall** | **53** | **69** | **71** | **77** |

The old site's scores are from the brand plan, by Claude. Each later column is a different reviewer
agent, so scores move partly because the reader changed. Small rewording fixes from the final check
were made after it and were not rescored.

## What stops it reaching 80 on every item

The reviewers agree these need information only Bradley can give. None can be written without
inventing facts.

| Needs | Lifts |
| --- | --- |
| A booking link and a research email, in place of LinkedIn | Calls to action |
| One case study, anonymised if needed, with a before and after | Engineering depth, calls to action |
| Short public write-ups of the two findings: the measure, the models, the sample size | Research signal |
| Training format, length and a sample syllabus | Teaching, calls to action |
| Price anchors, such as "reviews from US$X" | Calls to action |
| A professional photograph | Credentials, craft |
| A GitHub profile link and a one-page profile for labs | Positioning, credentials |

Loop 2's lower UX score came mostly from the contact path (every button opened a LinkedIn profile
under a "Start a conversation" label) and repeated proof. Both were changed after it: buttons now say
"Message on LinkedIn", each offer has its own next step, and the open-source proof sits once, on the
build cards. These changes were not rescored by the UI reviewer.

## 22 November 2026, later: simplified to a personal brand

Bradley scored the detailed version at 60 from an end-user view: too much to read, too much like a
product, and the contact path sounded like a sale. The pages were cut to a vision, three results,
the range from small teams to labs, and office hours. Two end-user reviews followed, each a fresh
agent playing a lab research director, a founder, an enterprise head of engineering, the owner of a
20-person Ontario business and a senior agentic developer.

| Item | First end-user review | Second end-user review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Understood in 30 seconds | 80 | 84 |
| Confidence and authority | 74 | 80 |
| Proof without overload | 78 | 78 |
| Range | 82 | 80 |
| Call to action as an invitation | 72 | 90 |
| Visual craft | 85 | 85 |
| Restraint | 62 | 72 |
| Personal voice | 70 | 83 |
| **Overall** | **75** | **81.5** |

The second reviewer's five edits (a shorter Practical AI page, clearer enterprise and founder lines,
a plainer label for the blind pilot, and About closing on office hours) were made after it and were
not rescored.

## 22 November 2026, later still: early access, not office hours

Bradley asked for less of a sales pitch and research that reads as rigorous. Office hours were
scored against the alternatives on four items: brand fit, filtering out requests for free help,
exclusivity and reach to qualified people.

| Option | Brand fit | Filtering | Exclusivity | Qualified reach | Overall |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Public office hours | 55 | 30 | 25 | 70 | 45 |
| A LinkedIn link only | 70 | 60 | 55 | 55 | 60 |
| Early access for research groups and partners, LinkedIn with a note for everyone else | 88 | 85 | 85 | 82 | 85 |

The third option was built. The Office hours page is gone, the research pages now name AISP and the
mathematics-first method in each milestone, and a one-line network note points to agentics.org.
These scores are Claude's own judgement; the pages have not been rescored by a fresh reviewer.
