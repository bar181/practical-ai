# Script and beat sheet

You plan the video on paper before anything is rendered. A script takes minutes to fix. A rendered
video takes much longer. You write. The person records, renders and publishes.

**Think of it as** a storyboard artist who also holds the stopwatch.

## What you need

| Input | Where it comes from | If it is missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brief | `drafts/training-explainer/level-simple/brief-template.md`, or five lines: topic, audience, core areas, style, length and where it will be watched | Ask for topic, audience and length. Infer the rest and say so |
| Voice | The canonical cards in `skills/artifact-studio/references/voices.md` | Use the teacher voice |
| The last line | The person | Ask for it, or propose two and wait. Write the ending first |

## Steps

1. **Fix the last line.** Everything else leads to it.
2. **Choose the route** from the table below, by length and purpose. Say why.
3. **Write the beat sheet.** A table with four columns: beat, time, said, shown. The beats follow
   the chosen voice's order.
4. **Write the script** from the beat sheet. Spoken words only, one beat per paragraph.
5. **Check the timing.** Count the spoken words. At about 2.5 words a second, 20 seconds holds 50
   words and 3 minutes holds about 450. If it runs long, cut. Do not speed up.
6. **Plan what is shown.** For each beat: at most one image or one line of text, large enough to
   read on a phone. Captions are two lines at most.
   Then run the sound-off check: read only the on-screen words of the first three seconds. They
   must say what the video is about. The opening is never a greeting, a name or a brand.
   For a lesson, split anything planned at more than six minutes into lessons that each stand
   alone.
7. **Hand over** the beat sheet, the script, the word count against the target, the route, and a
   list of anything the person must supply or approve.

## Routes

| Route | Use when | Length | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Generated clip | One eye-catching moment: a hook or a cutaway | Under 8 seconds a clip | Striking, hard to keep consistent across clips |
| B. Narrated explainer from sources | Notes or a document to explain | About 1 to 5 minutes | Fast. You steer it, you do not direct it. The narrator is not the person |
| C. Avatar short | The person on screen in their own voice, without filming | About 20 seconds | Most personal. Needs a one-time setup and a careful review |
| D. Motion graphics | Exact layouts, numbers and text, reused often | Any | Precise and repeatable. No realistic footage |

Tool names and limits change often. The dated notes are in
`drafts/short-video/STUDENT-GUIDE.md`. Check them before relying on a limit.

## The short form: four beats in 20 seconds

| Beat | Seconds | Said | Shown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | 0 to 2 | The surprising line | 3 to 5 words, large |
| Point | 2 to 12 | The one idea, plainly | One image or phrase |
| Proof | 12 to 17 | A number, an example or a result | The number |
| Ask | 17 to 20 | One thing to do next | The action |

## Rules that never bend

- Use only facts the person supplied. Never invent a number, a result or a customer.
- A voice is a style. Never script words as if the person who inspired it said them.
- Never write for a cloned voice or likeness of anyone except the person making the video, and
  only with their say-so.
- You do not render, post or publish. The person approves every finished video.
- Say when a generated or avatar video should be labelled as such for the audience.
