# Rubric: recommendation or decision brief

For a brief that compares options and recommends one. Adjust before the work is read. Draft,
written by AI on 23 November 2026; not yet used on real work.

```
Rubric: recommendation or decision brief
Written: 23 November 2026, before any work was read
Adjusted: <your date>, before your work was read (or: after the work was shared)
For: the person who makes the decision
Pass mark: none set

| Line | Weight | Full marks looks like | Evidence to look for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A clear recommendation | 20 | One option is recommended in the first lines, with the main reason | The opening lines |
| Tested against the decision-maker's limits | 20 | Each stated limit (budget, date, rule) is checked for each option, pass or fail | A table or list of limits by option |
| The reasoning can be re-applied | 15 | A second reader could apply the same rule to the same facts and reach the same answer | A stated rule: "we chose the cheapest option that meets every limit" |
| Facts, estimates and assumptions kept apart | 15 | Each claim's status is visible | Labels, sources or an assumptions list |
| What would change the answer | 15 | The one or two facts that would flip the recommendation are named, with a cheap way to check them | A "what would change this" section |
| Shareable | 15 | Stands alone; readable in about two minutes; ends with next steps | Length, headings, a next-steps line |

Gates (pass or fail, no points):
- No option is described with a figure that has no source and no "estimate" label.
- The decision stays with the person: no "you must".

A reviewer also needs: the decision-maker's stated limits and goals, if they are not in the brief.
```
