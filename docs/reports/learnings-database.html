Practical AI field guide

Stop making the same mistake twice.

Tell Claude, ChatGPT or any AI tool "log that" when something goes wrong (tested so far in Claude only). It keeps the lesson as one rule in a file you own, and gives the rules back before you start similar work.

Start in two minutesOpen the project

What it is

Think of it as a ship's log.One line each time the crew hits a rock or finds a faster channel, read by the next crew before they sail. Your AI writes the line. You decide which lines become the rules.

You probably fix the same things again and again: the email with last year's dates, the quote with no expiry date, the price list that drops a line. Chat memory remembers facts about you; it does not keep rules for the work, and nobody checks what it keeps. This does both.

The step-by-step guide

Six steps. The first four take about two minutes.

1

Copy the starter prompt 30 seconds

Change the last line to something that went wrong for you this week: what happened, and what you expected.

Read the instructions at this link and follow them for the rest of this chat:
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bar181/practical-ai/main/projects/learnings-database/base/SKILL.md

log that: [what happened]. I expected [what you expected].
2

Paste it into your AI tool 10 seconds

ChatGPT, Claude, or any chatbot that can open links. If you already keep a log, attach it with the paperclip or + button, so it can spot repeats. No log yet? It gives you the starter spreadsheet.

Your AI can't open links? Open base/SKILL.md on the project page, press Raw, and paste the whole text instead of the link.

3

Check the lesson it wrote 1 minute

You get one row: the kind of mistake, the area, and the lesson as an instruction, "When ..., do ...". Check it says what happened in your words, and that nothing was added.

4

Keep it 30 seconds

Say "yes, give me the updated file" and save it over your old one. That is your log: a normal spreadsheet you can open, sort and share.

5

Before similar work, ask for the rules 30 seconds

Start a chat with the same prompt, attach your log, and say "before I start [the work]". You get up to five rules you accepted, in a block to paste into your next chat or an agent's instructions.

6

Once a week, review 5 minutes

Say "weekly review". Each new lesson comes with a suggestion: accept it, merge it with another, or retire it. You decide. Only accepted lessons become rules.

  1. Create a Supabase project just for the log.
  2. Paste one file into its SQL editor and press Run.
  3. Connect it to Claude or ChatGPT with Supabase's own connector, limited to that project.
  4. Use the same prompt. Rows are saved after your yes, with a history of every change.

Common mistakes

  • Logging opinions. "We should post more" is a plan, not a lesson. It declines those in one line.
  • Forgetting the log. Without your file attached it cannot spot a repeat, and it says so.
  • Accepting everything. The five-minute review is the point: accept the rules you will follow, retire the rest.
  • Putting names in rules. Rules name no one, so they can be shared. Names in the details become [removed], and those rows stay private.

Questions

Where is my data?

In your own spreadsheet, folder or Supabase project. Nothing is stored by Practical AI. When you attach the log to a chat, the chatbot's company handles it like anything else you send there.

How is it different from my chatbot's memory?

Memory keeps facts about you, automatically. This keeps rules for the work, counted so you can see what repeats, and used only after you accept them.

What does it cost?

Nothing beyond the AI tool you already use. Supabase has a free plan for level 3.

Has anyone used it?

It was built and tested by its author with fresh Claude sessions on invented data, 19 November 2026: 40 of 40 lessons filed in the right area, 11 of 11 behaviour checks. Since then it has come into use by people from business owners to agentic developers (reported by Bradley, 22 November 2026). It has not been tried in ChatGPT.
