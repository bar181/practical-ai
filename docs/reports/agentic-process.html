Agentic Process · an explainer for builders

How do you turn a job you repeat into a tool someone else can use?

Agentic Process is for people who build tools or workflows with a coding agent such as Claude Code or Codex. Give it a job you repeat. It hands back a small package someone can start in five minutes, and it tests the package in a fresh session before you get it.

See the build request

A recipe card that works in any kitchen.

You do not print a card for each brand of oven. And before you get the card, someone who has never seen your kitchen cooks from it.

What comes back

A folder of three or four files. These three are always there:

FileWhat it is
README.mdHow to use the tool: a five-minute start, what to give it, what to check
SKILL.mdThe tool. Paste it into any chatbot with a notice
example.mdA worked example, and practice notices to try it on

In the pilot, a fresh chat had only that tool's SKILL.md and a notice it had never seen. Its reply opened with a short summary and a warning that the notice never gives its Effective Date and leaves out the form it says is attached. Then came tables of what you must do, the dates and the open questions, each line with the exact words and where they are, and a column to tick each one against the page.

Inside the builder

Four steps, with one loop back to fix

Between your request and the three files, the builder does four things in order. The bar below stays on screen and marks the step you are reading.

  1. 1Name
  2. 2Pick
  3. 3Build
  4. 4Test

fails at 4? fix at 3, run 4 again

1Name the usable result

Who
The builder, in your coding agent. If the job, the user or an input is missing, it asks you once, then gets on with it.
What
Before building anything it writes five lines:
  • U1 who uses the result
  • U2 what they give it
  • U3 what they need to receive
  • U4 what makes it usable for them
  • U5 one catch it must spot
Here
U1 is the café owner, in the chatbot they already use. U5 is a notice that leaves out a date or an attachment it mentions.

In the pilot, U5 and U4 are what produced a practice notice with no date and a warning block on the first screen of the result.

2Pick the simplest build

Who
The builder. It checks what already exists: Anthropic's skills and plugins first, then the platform you use, then open source, for about ten minutes.
What
It takes the first row that fits:
  • Something existing does the whole job: a quick-start README only
  • Repeatable instructions and local rules: a thin SKILL.md, the default
  • A step needs exact behaviour, such as arithmetic, dates or matching: that step as a formula or short script, called from the SKILL.md
  • A connection removes recurring manual work: a connection, with a person approving consequential steps

In the pilot, the Agentic Process package had 4 files. The direct build had 12, including test scripts and a check sheet.

3Build one SKILL.md

Who
The builder, starting from the project's template.
What
SKILL.md is the one source of instructions. It works pasted on its own, asks for missing input in one reply, and puts what the person will use first. The builder also writes the README and the inputs for three test cases.

External skills and packages are research material only: read as text, never installed or run.

4Test it in a fresh session

Who
A separate session that has only the README and what it says to paste. If the builder cannot start one, you: it hands you the exact messages.
What
Three cases: an input missing something, the normal case, and the catch. Each result is read twice:
  • Does it hold up? Rules followed, nothing invented, the catch caught.
  • Would the person use it as it is? Read only the first screen. If they would have to dig, rewrite or ask, it fails.
Loop
Fix what failed and rerun that case, at most twice. The normal case's unedited result becomes example.md.

In the pilot, neither builder could start a separate session, so the runs were started from outside. Since then the builder puts the exact message for each case in the hand-back.

Then it hands back

A note with U1 to U5, each with its check, its result and who ran it; what it chose to build and why; what it assumed; and what you decide next. It merges, publishes and sends nothing.

The harder case

A notice that never gives its Effective Date

The catch is where a tool earns its keep. In the pilot, two fresh sessions got the same build brief. One followed the builder. The other built however it judged best. Each package then ran in a fresh chat on an unseen, invented supplier notice whose Effective Date is never given, and a separate session reviewed both blind.

In one blind pilot, a tool built with Agentic Process scored 93/100. The direct-build version scored 73/100 on the same brief.

From the pilot result, unedited. The supplier and notice were invented for the test.

Warning: Three things are missing. (1) The "Effective Date" is never stated, so I cannot tell you when the new prices and 30-day payment terms start. (2) The Account Update Form is described as "attached" but was not in what you sent, so I cannot tell you what it asks for. (3) ...

The scope: one build task, one unseen notice and one blind reviewer. A signal, not a study.

As of 16 November 2026 it has not been tried in Codex or any other coding agent, and nobody but its author has used it. The pilot is its only head-to-head comparison.

Use cases

Jobs it has been pointed at

  • Supplier notices to actions

    Paste a supplier notice or a change of terms. Get what you must do, the dates and what is unclear, each line with the exact words it came from.

    Status: built by the builder in the pilot, run once on an unseen notice and reviewed blind.

  • A business decision brief

    Ask a business question. Get a one-page brief: the facts with sources, the options compared by a written rule, a SWOT, and a recommendation you check. You decide.

    Status: converted by hand. Passed its three-input check in fresh Claude chats.

  • A learning log

    Say "log that" when something goes wrong or works better than expected, and get one row for your log. Paste the log back before similar work and get the rules that apply.

    Status: built by the builder on its own. Passed three fresh-session checks.

  • Meeting notes to actions

    Turn a client's meeting notes into actions with owners and dates. The catch: notes that never say who owns an action.

    Status: the README's example of a job to describe. No tool built for it yet.

Use it with your own job

Keep the first three lines of the build request. Replace the rest with four answers:

  1. The job: what you do by hand, every week or month. "Turn a client's meeting notes into actions with owners and dates."
  2. Who uses the result, and where. "Me, in ChatGPT."
  3. An example input: a real one with private details removed, or ask for an invented one.
  4. The catch: what goes wrong when people do it by hand. "Notes that never say who owns an action." The builder tests for it.
Check yourself

Is it ready?

Open the hand-back and the new folder. Tick each one as you check it. It is ready when all five hold.

0 of 5 ticked

The hand-back says "not yet run" against the checks. Is the tool ready?

No. It is built, not ready. Paste the messages from the hand-back into a fresh chat yourself and read what comes back.

With no fresh session, may the builder write example.md itself?

No. It writes "not yet run" against each check and leaves the inputs waiting, never a result it wrote itself.

Recap

Write one card, let a stranger cook from it, then hand it over.

  1. Name the usable result
  2. Pick the simplest build
  3. Build one SKILL.md
  4. Test it in a fresh session
