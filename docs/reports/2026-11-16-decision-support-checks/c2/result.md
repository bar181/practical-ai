## 1. Frame the decision

**Decision:** Should Harbour Lane start a weekday catering service for offices near its three shops?

**Options:**
- **A. Do nothing.** Stay walk-in only.
- **B. Full weekday office catering.** Breakfast and lunch menus, delivery from all three shops, and active selling to offices.
- **C. Small trial.** This tests the two riskiest parts cheaply: whether nearby offices will order, and whether you can fill orders without the head baker working more.
  - One shop only, for 8 weeks.
  - Two boxes made only from products you already bake: a morning meeting box (pastries and coffee) and an afternoon coffee-break box.
  - Orders are due by noon the day before. Customers pick up, or you deliver within walking or short driving distance.
  - Spending is capped at $3,000.
  - The trial length, menu, cap and order cutoff are all **proposals**.

## 5. The brief

**Decision:** Should Harbour Lane Bakery start a weekday catering service for offices near our shops?

**Recommendation:** Run an 8-week, one-shop office-order trial capped at $3,000, but first spend two weeks calling 15 nearby offices, and launch only if at least 3 say they would order.

**Why:**
- More Canadian employers now require on-site work, and Ontario public servants went back full time in January 2026. Whether that demand exists near our shops is unknown.
- A full service would add early starts and deliveries to a team we are trying to protect, and to a head baker who already works six days. Its costs are unknown.
- A trial-sized line might add about $21,600 a year (estimate). Catering alone would need about 19 to 20 orders a week to reach our 10% growth goal, so we should learn cheaply before building.

**SWOT:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Strengths:** existing products and kitchens; three locations; small cost | **Weaknesses:** small sales impact at trial scale; no lunch items; little help to in-shop afternoons |
| **Opportunities:** more on-site work; growing breakfast catering; suburban offices filling | **Threats:** weak downtown office market; reliability expectations; competing caterers and platforms |

**Risks and how to reduce them:**
1. **Orders pull staff off the counter or onto the head baker.**
   - Cap the trial at 3 orders a day.
   - Use only products we already bake.
   - Pack boxes in quiet afternoon hours.
2. **A late or wrong morning order.**
   - Orders close at noon the day before.
   - Deliver within a short radius only.
   - One named person owns each delivery.
3. **Food safety or insurance gaps during delivery.**
   - Ask Hamilton Public Health and our insurance broker before the first order.

**Next steps (next 30 days):**
1. **This week:** agree the trial rules with my partner: one shop, 8 weeks, $3,000 cap, stop rules.
2. **Weeks 1 to 2:**
   - Call or visit 15 offices near the chosen shop.
   - Ask Public Health about delivery from our premises.
   - Ask our broker about delivery and liability cover.
3. **Weeks 3 to 4:** if at least 3 offices say yes, launch two boxes with a simple order form. Price them against the Hamilton benchmarks after checking current local prices.

**What would change this recommendation:**
- **Go bigger:** strong call results (many offices ordering weekly), or trial weeks 6 to 8 averaging more than 3 paid orders a week with no extra head-baker hours.
- **Stop:** fewer than 3 interested offices.
- **Judgement call:** reading "afternoon sales" as any afternoon sale, including deliveries, would make an afternoon break box count toward that goal. It does not change the recommendation.

**Assumptions:**
- Existing kitchens have room for trial volume.
- Staff other than the head baker can pack orders.
- An average order is about $150.
- Some of our shops are near enough offices to deliver to.
