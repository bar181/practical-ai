I had to interpret several things in the instructions.

- **Scoring rule versus "not found" facts.** The rule says a score that rests on a not-found fact gets 0. But "does catering add sales" doesn't depend on how much local demand there is, only on its direction, so I scored it +1. I listed the stricter 0 reading as a judgement call. Under that reading the rule's fallback still points to the smaller option as the check.
- **Unclear limits.** "Head baker already works six days" and "risk: medium" aren't written as pass-or-fail limits. I turned them into tests ("adds no head-baker hours"; "worst case is bounded and easy to stop") and marked both as assumed.
- **"Who else has a say."** I treated this as a process point for the "who should see this" line, not as a limit.
- **Check first when the leading option is already the small trial.** The skill doesn't say what to do here. I used a still-cheaper step (calling 15 offices) as the check before the trial.
- **Brief length.** The brief repeats the facts compactly, so the earlier work sections and the brief overlap. I kept the brief near 400 words, not counting the SWOT and sources.
- **Sources.** Nearly all the demand and price evidence is national, US or undated. I found no Hamilton-specific office-catering data, so I flagged those items as not found and labelled the weaker sources instead of leaving them out.
