# Action brief: Kestrel Fold Wholesale, changes to terms of supply

**What this is:** A letter from your supplier changing how you order, when you get deliveries, and how fast you must pay. The changes start on **1 December 2026**. Prices are not changing.

**The one thing to know:** You have **14 days to pay invoices instead of 30**. That is the change most likely to squeeze your cash. They also want a signed form back by **Friday 20 November 2026**, which is two days from today.

## Things to watch out for

- **The pay window halves.** You pay for stock about two weeks sooner. If you buy on credit and pay from the takings, check you can cover the first bills due in mid-December.
- **Late fees are per invoice.** With two deliveries a week, one missed payment run could mean several $35 fees at once, plus a hold on orders.
- **The form signs you up to "terms of supply" in general,** not just the changes in this letter. The full terms are not attached. Ask for a copy before you sign.
- **"Invoices are now due 14 days"** is unclear. It does not say whether invoices dated before 1 December keep the 30-day terms. Ask.
- **"Standard plan"** is not explained. The letter does not say which plan you are on or what other plans exist.
- **The letter does not say what happens if you do not sign** by 20 November.
- **There is room to talk.** The letter says the accounts team "is always happy to talk" about cash flow. That is your opening to ask for longer terms.

## Questions for your account manager

1. Can I send you the full terms of supply before I sign?
2. Do invoices dated before 1 December stay on 30-day terms?
3. Is my account on the standard plan? What other plans are there?
4. Can we keep 30-day terms, or agree 21 days, given how long we have bought from you?
5. What happens if I do not return the form by 20 November?

## Your next steps

1. **Today or tomorrow:** email or call your account manager with the questions above.
2. **By 20 November:** sign and return the form, or send a written note that you are still waiting on answers.
3. **Before 1 December:** set order reminders for Wednesday and Friday at noon, and set up a payment run that pays this supplier within 14 days.
4. **Mid-December:** check that your first invoices on the new terms were paid on time.

This brief is not legal advice. If this supplier is a large share of your costs, it is worth a quick word with your bookkeeper about the cash impact.
