# Rubric: is this decision brief ready to send?

Score a brief or a decision record out of 100 before it goes to the decider. Ask a second chat to
score it, with only this rubric and the brief, so the work is not grading itself. Below 80: fix the
lowest item and score again.

| # | Item | Points | Full marks when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The decision is framed | 10 | One question, who decides, decision needed by, and what would change the answer |
| 2 | The options are real | 10 | Two to four options, including "do nothing", compared on the same criteria |
| 3 | Every fact is traceable | 20 | Every number and claim has a source number, the exact words and a link or "their figures"; every quote is word for word; nothing filled from memory |
| 4 | Problems are visible | 10 | Unchecked, partly checked, mismatched and unopened sources are marked ⚠, and none of them carries the recommendation on its own |
| 5 | The answer comes first | 10 | The recommendation is in the first five lines, with three reasons that point to sources |
| 6 | The evidence against is honest | 10 | The strongest facts against are stated with sources, or what was looked for is named |
| 7 | Risks have controls | 10 | A premortem of three to five reasons it could fail, each with a control and an owner |
| 8 | It can be acted on | 10 | Next steps with owners and dates; whether it can be undone is stated |
| 9 | It reads in two minutes | 5 | One page above the Sources appendix, plain words, sums shown with their working, no adjective doing the work of a number |
| 10 | The gaps are named | 5 | What is not known, and how to find it out |

**Hard fails, whatever the score:** an invented source, quote, number or name; a recommendation that
rests only on facts marked ⚠ without saying so; a brief that contacts, buys, signs or sends anything.

**The score is advice to the writer.** A high score means the brief is well made, not that the
recommendation is right. The decider decides.
