Today's lesson

How do you get an AI to make a page you are proud to send?

What a default AI page does to a room.

Think of it as

A smart intern.

A new hire who has read every book in the world, and has never met you. Fast and capable. No idea what you like, who you are writing to, or what good looks like in your business.

You would not hand an intern a pile of notes and say "make it nice". You would hand over a brief. The studio is that brief, written once.

Three questions before you go

Why paste the instructions into a Project, and not into every chat?

A brief you give once gets used every time. A brief you have to retype gets skipped by the second week.

The page shows a number that is not in your notes. What now?

Have it removed. The instructions tell the assistant to use only what you gave it and to mark the gaps. A number from nowhere is a mistake, not a bonus.

Which is the better ask: "make it pop", or "make the three numbers a bar chart"?

The second. Name the part and say what it should become. The intern cannot see inside your head.

So

An AI is a smart intern. Give it the brief once. Then ask in one sentence: the job, the reader, the shape.

Behind this page

The explainer is a lesson you can teach from. It uses the teacher voice, which makes a hard idea feel obvious by starting somewhere familiar. Inspired by the great professors at Harvard. This one teaches how to use the studio itself, so you can run it in a class.

How it is built

  1. One question, stated plainly, over a picture with a line of text on it
  2. An everyday analogy in very large type, with a sketch that draws itself
  3. Steps you scroll through while a diagram keeps your place, each with one example
  4. A small moving picture of the before and after, and a second picture that answers the first
  5. A worked example, stepped through line by line
  6. An exercise for the learner, with a time limit
  7. Questions to check understanding
  8. A one-sentence recap that reuses the analogy

Design rules

  • Black and white, with one colour that means "look here"
  • The analogy gets the biggest type on the page
  • Complexity rises one step at a time, never two
  • Every section is tagged with what kind of thing it is
  • Motion teaches or it goes: the sketch draws in the order you would explain it
  • The reader does something: scrolls, steps through, tries it, then answers
