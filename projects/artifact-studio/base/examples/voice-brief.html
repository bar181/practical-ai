What the assistant said about its page
# Delivery note

Page: `out.html`. One self-contained file, system fonts, no scripts, no network requests. It prints on one sheet (checked on A4 and US Letter).

## Chose

- **Kind of thing:** a page to be read, because you asked for a one-page brief.
- **Recipe:** one-page report. No recipe is a decision brief, and the guide says to use the one-page report when none fits. The proposal recipe is for a customer saying yes, which this is not.
- **Voice:** executive brief. You asked for "a one-page decision brief for busy executives" in the order decision, background, options, recommendation, risks. That is this voice's order. The voice adds a memo header and next steps.
- **Look:** the voice's default, a paper memo. Serif system type, one accent (blue, used only on the deadline and the recommended option), no images, no motion.
- **Parts:** three numbers with what they were before, one thin bar chart of site visits booked, one options table. No moving parts.
- **Colours and contrast:** main text on paper 15.3 to 1. Secondary text on paper 6.7 to 1. Accent on paper 8.0 to 1. Main text on the highlighted row 13.9 to 1. Accent on the highlighted row 7.3 to 1. Chart bars on paper 5.5 to 1.
- **Preferences applied:** no preferences file came with the request. The only standing writing preference I had was "no em-dashes", and the page and these notes follow it.

## Assumed

- **The reader is the owners.** Your notes say the owners decide, so the brief is addressed to them.
- **The decision is whether to keep drafting first replies after the trial ends on 31 May.** Your notes give the deadline but never state the question.
- **The three options are mine.** Your notes do not list options. I took A (keep it) from the trial, B (a part-time coordinator) from your cost comparison, and C (do nothing) because the voice requires it.
- **"Do nothing" means the trial stops on 31 May and replies go back to how they were before:** 26 hours, 12% within the hour, 6 hours a week. Your notes do not say what happens if nobody decides.
- **"No tool cost" under option C.** I assumed the $150 a month stops when the trial stops.
- **Option B is "not tried".** Your notes give a cost for a coordinator and nothing else.
- **The recommendation (option A) is mine.** Your notes do not say what you recommend. The numbers point that way, but it is your call.
- **Two of the three controls are mine** and are labelled "proposed" on the page: keep recording visits after spring, and count jobs won. The control labelled "in place" is your own rules.
- **The risk "a drafted reply goes out wrong" is my wording.** Your notes give the rules but do not name the risk they guard against.
- **22, 28 and 31 are February, March and April in that order.** Your notes say "then 22, 28 and 31" and that the trial ran February to April.
- **The chart source reads "the company's trial notes".** You did not say where the counts come from.
- **Derived numbers, with working shown on the page:** 69 points (81 − 12), 3.5 hours a week (6 − 2.5), $1,800 a year ($150 × 12), $24,200 more for a coordinator (26,000 − 1,800).
- **"One page" means one printed sheet.**
- **I left Priya's story out.** The executive brief voice has no place for a story, and your notes say only that you "may" tell it.

## Missing

- **Who the brief is from.** Marked on the page as `[needs: who is sending this]`.
- **The date it is sent.** Marked on the page as `[needs: date sent]`.
- **The year.** "26 May" and "31 May" appear as you wrote them.
- **Jobs won during the trial.** Not counted yet. The page says so in the summary and in the risks.
- **Who will count jobs won, and from when.** The page says the owners name the person.
- **Anything about option B beyond its cost.** Hours, what the coordinator would do, how fast replies would be.
- **Whether Priya is a real customer, whether she has agreed to be named, and whether we were one of the three companies.**
- **Which enquiries came by which route.** You name web form, email and telephone but give no split.

## Check before sharing

- **The dates do not agree.** Your notes say the trial "ran from February to April, 90 days" and also that "the trial ends on 31 May". The page treats February to April as the period measured and 31 May as the end of the trial. Confirm that is right. February to April is also 89 days in most years and 90 only in a leap year.
- **Read the recommendation as if you had to defend it.** It is my reading of your numbers, and it will go out under your name.
- **Confirm options B and C are options the owners would recognise.**
- **Confirm or delete the two "proposed" controls.**
- **Fill in From and Date,** then remove the two `[needs: ...]` marks.
- **The visit numbers do not prove the cause.** The page says so under Risks. Do not let the chart be quoted without that line.
- **"About one visit in four becomes a job"** sits under the chart. I did not multiply it out into jobs, because jobs won are not counted yet.
- **Open it as a page, not as code.** On many phones a bare HTML file opens as text. Print it or send a PDF if in doubt.

The decision

Do we keep drafting a first reply to every enquiry after the trial ends on 31 May?

During the trial our median first reply fell from 26 hours to 38 minutes, and site visits booked rose from 18 a month to 31. It costs about $1,800 a year, and jobs won are not counted yet, so the trial does not show whether it brings in more work.

Background

  1. We are a home renovation company. Enquiries arrive by web form, email and telephone. Before the trial, 12% were answered within the hour.
  2. What changed: a reply is drafted the moment a message arrives. A member of staff reads it, fixes it and sends it.
  3. Our rules: a person approves every reply. The first reply confirms and offers a visit. It never states a price.
  4. Setting it up took about an hour. We collected three replies we are proud of, wrote our rules on half a page, saved both in a chat project, tested it on five old enquiries, then used it on the next real one.
  • Median first reply 38 minutes Was 26 hours.
  • Answered within the hour 81% Was 12%. Up 69 points (81 − 12).
  • Office manager's time on replies 2.5 hours a week Was 6. Frees 3.5 hours a week (6 − 2.5).

Options

Option Cost a year What we know
A. Keep drafting first replies Recommended About $1,800 ($150 a month × 12) The trial results: median first reply 38 minutes, 81% answered within the hour, 2.5 hours a week of the office manager's time.
B. Hire a part-time coordinator About $26,000, which is about $24,200 more than A (26,000 − 1,800) Not tried. We have a cost for it and no results.
C. Do nothing: the trial ends on 31 May Weak point: the slowest replies No tool cost How things were before the trial: median first reply 26 hours, 12% answered within the hour, 6 hours a week of the office manager's time.

Recommendation

Choose option A. Keep drafting first replies after 31 May, keep the three rules as they are, and start counting jobs won. In the trial the median first reply went from 26 hours to 38 minutes, the office manager got back 3.5 hours a week, and the cost is about $1,800 a year against about $26,000 for a coordinator.

Risks and controls

  • A drafted reply goes out wrong. Control, in place: a person approves every reply, and the first reply never states a price.
  • The rise in visits may not come from faster replies. This is one company, there was no comparison group, and spring is the busy season. Control, proposed: keep recording site visits booked each month after spring and compare.
  • We do not know the effect on jobs won. They are not counted yet. Control, proposed: count jobs won from now on.

Next steps

  • By 26 May: the owners choose A, B or C.
  • 31 May: the trial ends. Under A, drafting carries on with the same three rules. Under C, it stops.
  • If A is chosen: the owners name who counts jobs won.
