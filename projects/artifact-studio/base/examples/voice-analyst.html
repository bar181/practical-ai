What the assistant said about its page
# Delivery note

Page: `out.html`. One self-contained file, system fonts, no script, no network requests.

## Chose

- **Kind of thing:** a page to be read, because you asked for a one-pager.
- **Recipe:** one-page report. "Evidence-first one-pager" is not a recipe name in the guide, and this is the closest one.
- **Voice:** analyst. It is the first voice of that recipe, and its order is the order you described: the conclusion as the title, the answer in two sentences, three numbers, three findings each with a full-sentence heading, one chart and a "so what", then method, limits and next steps.
- **Look:** the analyst default. Quiet page, one data colour (blue) for the trial, grey for the comparison, unit and source under each chart. You gave no design spec and no picture, so there is no picture on the page.
- **Layout:** one column, built at 390 pixels wide first. In print it folds into columns and fits one sheet (see Check before sharing).
- **Parts:** three plain bar charts drawn in HTML so every label is real text, one results table with the weak row marked, three numbers set as a ruled list. No moving parts and no buttons.
- **Three numbers at the top:** 38 minutes (was 26 hours), 31 visits in the third trial month (was 18 a month), 2.5 hours a week (was 6).
- **Three findings and their charts:** Speed (81% answered within the hour against 12%), Visits (18, then 22, 28, 31), Cost (about $1,800 a year against about $26,000).
- **Preferences applied:** none. No preferences file came with the message.
- **Colours:** seven tokens plus the data colour. Contrast against the page background: main text 15.1 to 1, secondary text 5.9 to 1, accent 7.0 to 1, data colour 5.9 to 1, comparison grey 3.4 to 1 (bars only, never text).

## Assumed

- **The reader is the owners and the job is the decision due by 26 May.** I could not ask, so I decided.
- **The answer is mine, not yours.** Your notes give the evidence but never say "keep it". I wrote: "That evidence favours keeping the drafted replies after 31 May", with the limits in the same sentence. If you want the page neutral, change that one sentence.
- **I call the thing "drafted replies".** Your notes do not name the tool or say what drafts the reply, so the page does not either.
- **22, 28 and 31 are the three trial months in order.** The chart labels them "Trial month 1, 2, 3" and the caption says February to April. I did not write February = 22 and so on, because the notes do not say it.
- **The figures cover February to April, and the arrangement itself ends on 31 May.** Your notes say the trial "ran from February to April" and also "ends on 31 May". I read those as the measured period and the end of the arrangement.
- **The part-time coordinator is the alternative the owners are weighing.** The notes give the cost but do not say so outright.
- **"One visit in four becomes a job" applies to the trial's visits.** I used it for an estimate, labelled as an estimate on the page.
- **Sums I worked from your figures, all shown with their working on the page:**
  - 22 + 28 + 31 = 81 visits in the trial months
  - 18 × 3 = 54 visits at the earlier rate, so 81 − 54 = 27 more
  - 27 ÷ 4 = 6.75, written as "about 7 more jobs"
  - 150 × 12 = 1,800 a year
  - 26,000 − 1,800 = 24,200 a year less than a coordinator
  - 6 − 2.5 = 3.5 hours a week given back
  - "more than a day" for 26 hours
- **Every source line says "the company's trial notes".** The notes do not say where the figures were recorded, so I named the only source I have.
- **Priya's story is left out.** The analyst voice has no place for it, it is not evidence from the trial, and the notes say only that you "may" tell it. I do not know whether Priya is a real customer or has agreed to be named.
- **The limits paragraph adds one reading of your limits:** some of the rise in visits may be seasonal, and the extra jobs cannot be stated yet. That follows from your four limits. It is not a new fact.
- **The breakdown table the recipe requires** is a before and during table of the measures. The "weak row" is jobs won, because it has no figure.

## Missing

- **The period and number of enquiries behind the two before figures (26 hours and 12%).** This is marked on the page as `[needs: the dates and the number of enquiries those two before figures cover]`, in the Method section. It is the one mark on the page.
- Jobs won during the trial. The page says they are not counted yet.
- Company name, author and the date of the report. The year is not in the notes either, so "26 May" and "31 May" carry no year.
- An owner and a date for any step other than the decision. The analyst voice wants an owner and date on every next step. The notes give one: the owners, by 26 May. Nobody is named for acting on the decision or for counting jobs won, so the page lists only the decision and the end date.
- The name of the tool, and what the $150 a month covers.
- How reply times and the office manager's hours were measured.
- Whether the first-reply figures include telephone enquiries.
- A comparison group, and any figures from a quieter season.

## Check before sharing

- **Read the answer sentence and agree with it**, or change it. It is my reading of your evidence.
- **Fill or remove the `[needs: ...]` line** in Method before the owners see it.
- **Check the estimate of about 7 more jobs.** It is the number most likely to be quoted and the least supported: it leans on "about one in four" and on visits that rose in the busy season.
- **Check that 22, 28 and 31 are February, March and April in that order.**
- **Check the coordinator comparison is fair** to show as a bar beside the tool. The page treats the two as alternatives.
- **Print it once.** In Chrome it fits one sheet at both A4 and US Letter, but the print text is small (about 8 point). Other browsers and printer margins may push it to a second sheet.
- **Open it as a rendered page.** A bare HTML file opens as code on many phones. Send it through something that shows it as a page.
- What I checked by rendering it: no sideways scrolling at 320, 390 and 768 pixels wide, the page is complete with scripts off (there are none), no network requests, every number on the page traced to your notes or to a sum shown on the page, body text contrast above 4.5 to 1.
- What I did not check: a real phone, Safari or Firefox, a screen reader.
- Nothing has been sent, posted or published.
