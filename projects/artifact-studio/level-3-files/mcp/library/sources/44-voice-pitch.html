My father kept a hardware shop. There was a bell over the door. When it rang, he looked up. Every time.

He did not always have what you came for. But you knew you had been seen.

Somewhere along the way, we swapped the bell for a form. A form does not look up.

26 hours.That is how long someone stands at our door now.

You know that silence. You have stood in it yourself.

So this is not a faster reply. It is not software. It is not a process.

It is the bell.The First Hour. Every enquiry, answered by a person.

We are going to look up again.

What they will remember

In the trial, 81 of every 100 people heard from us within the hour. They will not remember the number.

They will remember that on an ordinary evening, they rang the bell, and somebody looked up.

Behind this page

The pitch voice sells a feeling and lets the product arrive last. Inspired by Don Draper's carousel pitch in Mad Men. The father, the shop and the figures are invented for the demo.

How it is built

  1. A personal memory, in the first person
  2. The feeling inside that memory
  3. What was lost, in one image
  4. The single hard fact
  5. What this is not, three times
  6. What it is: the feeling, renamed as the product
  7. A quiet close that returns to the memory

Design rules

  • A dark room and one lit rectangle, like a slide projector
  • One slide at a time, advanced by hand
  • Photographs look remembered: faded, warm, soft at the edges
  • Never more than three short sentences under a slide
  • The product name appears once, on the last slide
  • No bullet points, no charts, no feature list
