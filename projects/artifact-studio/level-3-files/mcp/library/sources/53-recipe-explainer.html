How a quote gets made

Five stages between your first message and a price you can trust. Scroll and the diagram keeps your place.

  1. Stage 1. You, then the office

    You tell us what you want

    A few sentences and a photo are enough. We reply within the hour to book a visit. No price yet, because nobody has seen the room.

    Takes you about five minutes.

  2. Stage 2. The estimator

    Someone comes to look

    The estimator walks the room with you and asks what annoys you about it now. That answer shapes the design more than any measurement.

    About 45 minutes at your home.

  3. Stage 3. The estimator

    Everything is measured and listed

    Walls, windows, pipes and sockets go on a drawing. Then every item the job needs goes on a list: 41 lines for a typical kitchen.

    This list is what makes the price reliable.

  4. Stage 4. The office

    Each line gets a price

    Materials come from this month's supplier prices. Labour comes from how long the same task took on our last ten jobs. Then an allowance for the unknown.

    A typical allowance is 8% of the total.

  5. Stage 5. A second person

    Someone else checks it

    A person who did not write the quote reads it against the drawing. Only then does it go to you, with three options and a date it is valid until.

    You have it within three working days of the visit.

Five stages, two people, three days. That is why the number does not change later.

Behind this page

A scrolling explainer teaches a process one stage at a time while a diagram keeps the reader's place. This one uses the teacher voice and no photographs at all.

Recipe: the order of the page

  1. A title that names the process, and one line on why it matters
  2. A diagram of every stage that stays on screen
  3. One block of text per stage: who does it, what happens, one fact
  4. A closing line that adds the stages up

Components used

  • A pinned diagram, the same pattern as the scrolling photo tour
  • A line that fills as you move through the stages
  • Small drawn icons, one per stage

Goes well with

  • Voices: the teacher, the practical instructor, the keynote
  • Looks: dark with one bright colour, or black and white
