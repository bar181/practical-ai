The story deck

Seven slides that follow a story: a person who wants something, what stands in the way, a guide, a plan, and how it ends. Tap the right side of a slide to move on.

The person

She wants a kitchen she is glad to come home to.

Start with her, not with us. Use the words a customer would use.

The problem

She asked three companies. Nobody wrote back for a day.

It is a small thing, and it tells her how the whole job will go.

Name what happened, then how it felt. The feeling is the real problem.

The guide

We have been the company that wrote back late.

Our replies took 26 hours. We changed one rule, and now they take 38 minutes.

Now we may enter, as the guide. One line of empathy, one proof.

The plan

Three steps, and the first is free.

  1. Tell us about the room. We answer within the hour.
  2. We visit, measure and listen.
  3. You get one clear price with three options.
Three steps, and make the first one cost nothing.

The ask

Send us two photos of your kitchen today.

Start with two photos
Ask for one thing only. Say it the same way the button says it.

If nothing changes

Another winter of the drawer that sticks and the light you never liked.

Say what stays the same if she does nothing. Keep it gentle and true.

How it ends

Friends in the kitchen, and you not apologising for it.

Close on the picture of life afterwards, and return to the opening image.

Behind this deck

The story deck is for winning one customer or one room of them. The customer is the hero and the company is the guide. It follows Donald Miller's story framework: a character, a problem, a guide, a plan, a call to action, what failure looks like and what success looks like. The company and figures are invented.

How it is built

  1. The person and what they want, in their words
  2. The problem, outside and inside: what happened and how it felt
  3. The guide: we understand, and here is our proof
  4. A plan of three steps, with an easy first one
  5. One call to action
  6. What it costs to do nothing
  7. What life looks like afterwards

Design rules

  • Warm paper, a serif headline, one deep red for labels and the button
  • A photograph on the first and last slides only, so the story opens and closes on a place
  • A small label on each slide naming its part in the story
  • Sentences the customer could have said, never the company's own terms
  • Left-aligned, like a page being read aloud
  • Each slide has a note for the speaker: tap Notes or press N
