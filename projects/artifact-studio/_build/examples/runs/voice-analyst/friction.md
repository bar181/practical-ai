# Friction with the guide

Ordered by how much guessing each one cost. Each item quotes the line.

## 1. The recipe and voice merge rule does not say where the recipe-only sections go

> "Where the recipe and the voice disagree, the voice sets the order, the counts and the headings. The recipe sets which sections the page must have."

The one-page report requires "One trend chart with a full-sentence heading", "One breakdown table, weak row marked" and "What changed". The analyst order is "Three numbers. Three findings: full-sentence heading, one chart, a 'so what'. Method. Limits. Next steps with owner and date." The rule does not answer any of these:

- Is the recipe's one trend chart one of the analyst's three charts, or a fourth? I made it one of the three.
- Where does the breakdown table go in an order that has no slot for it? I put it after Method.
- Is "What changed" a section, or is it absorbed by the findings or by Method? I folded it into Method as one sentence.
- "The voice sets the headings", but the table and "What changed" have no voice heading at all.

This is the core of the build and I guessed at every step of it. The result is a page that states the same four numbers three times (top numbers, charts, table) because both lists had to be satisfied.

## 2. The guide never says whether I may write the conclusion

> "The conclusion as the title. The answer in two sentences."
> "You add structure and craft. You never add facts."
> "No invented facts, numbers, names, customers or quotations."

The notes contain evidence and a decision date. They contain no conclusion. The analyst voice demands one, twice. Is a recommendation ("keep it") a fact I am adding, or craft? The guide is silent. I wrote a hedged one and flagged it in the note, but a stricter reader of "never add facts" would have left the title as a bare result, and a looser one would have written "Keep it". Same problem with next steps: may I propose "count the jobs won", which the notes imply but do not say? I left it off.

## 3. "Next steps with owner and date" collides with the gap rule

> "Next steps with owner and date."
> "A gap is never filled with a guess. If the page would mislead without it, a number, a name, a date or a price the reader will act on, mark it on the page as `[needs: ...]`. Otherwise leave the page clean and list the gap under Missing."

The notes hold one step with an owner. A missing owner on a second step does not "mislead", so by the gap rule the page stays clean and the voice's requirement goes unmet. Or it is "a name the reader will act on", so it gets a mark. Both readings fit the sentence. The test for "would mislead" is left to taste, and it decides how many marks end up on a page going to the owners.

## 4. "One-pager" is undefined and the required sections do not fit one

> "One-page report"
> Executive brief, Avoid: "More than one page"

The guide never says what a page is: one screen, one scroll, one printed sheet. The build rules say nothing about print at all. The recipe plus the analyst voice add up to a title, an answer, three numbers, three charts with captions and so-whats, a table, method, limits and next steps. That is about 4,400 pixels tall on a phone, and it fits one printed sheet only with 8 point text and a three-column print layout I invented. If "one page" means a sheet, the recipe needs a length budget. If it does not, the name is misleading.

## 5. The recipe's "Use when" does not match the request, and "if named" is vague

> "One-page report | Someone needs to know how a period went"
> "Pick one from the table. If named, use it. If none fits, use the one-page report."

The person wrote "evidence-first one-pager". That is not a recipe name. It half-names a recipe and fully describes the analyst voice. Does "one-pager" count as naming the one-page report? And the actual job is a decision by a date, which is the executive brief's territory ("decision needed by"), while the structure the person asked for is the analyst's. The intake line "If the request names two jobs, the page does the one its reader will act on" did not settle it, because the person's own structure pointed the other way.

## 6. Derived numbers: the guide invites them and forbids them

> "Totals add up and derived numbers show their working"
> "Charts: ... Only numbers from the content."
> "No invented facts, numbers..."

Where is the line between a derived number and an invented one? "27 ÷ 4 = 6.75, about 7 more jobs" is arithmetic on the notes, and it is also the least supported and most quotable number on the page. The guide gives no test. I kept derived numbers out of the charts and put them in the so-what text with working, but that split is mine.

## 7. "Load the front-end design skill" pulls against the guide and has no precedence rule

> "If a front-end design skill is available (for example `frontend-design`), load it as well."

I had one and loaded it. It says to take aesthetic risk, choose distinctive typefaces, and treat "a big number with a small label" as a default to avoid. The guide says "Analyst: quiet, one data colour" and requires "Three numbers". The guide does not say which wins. I let the guide win everywhere it gave a rule and used the skill only where the guide was silent (typeface pairing, no all-caps labels, no dotted meta strings). A plain chat assistant has no skills, so this line also does nothing for most readers.

## 8. The analyst look is six words

> "Quiet, one data colour, grey for comparison, source under each chart. One picture at most, at the top."
> "Analyst: quiet, one data colour."

No hue, no background, no type, no density. "Quiet" is a mood, not a palette. The only detailed colour method in the guide is for a photo or a single brand colour, and I had neither. Two assistants will produce two unrelated pages from this.

## 9. Does the comparison grey count as one of the seven colours?

> "Seven named colour tokens at most, plus one data colour if the page has a chart."
> "one data colour, grey for comparison"

The grey bar is a chart colour that is not the data colour. I counted it inside the seven. The guide does not say.

## 10. What gets the accent on a page with no button?

> "One accent, used only for what to act on."

The analyst page has nothing to press. I spent the accent on the decision date. A reader could equally conclude the page should have no accent at all.

## 11. "Source under each chart" when the only source is the person's notes

> "Unit and source underneath."

The notes do not say where the figures were recorded. I wrote "Source: the company's trial notes" three times. It satisfies the rule and tells the reader almost nothing. The guide should say what to write when the source is the request itself.

## 12. Is the chart's "sentence" the finding's heading or a second sentence?

> "Charts: one point per chart, stated as a sentence."
> "full-sentence heading, one chart"

I treated the heading as the sentence. If the guide wants both, each finding states its point twice.

## 13. "Avoid: topic headings" against a voice order made of topic words

> "Method. Limits. Next steps with owner and date."
> Avoid: "Topic headings, hidden limits"
> "A heading on every section, key word first. A full sentence where the voice asks for one"

The voice asks for a full sentence only on the three findings. So are "Method" and "Limits" allowed as bare headings, which are topic headings, which the same row says to avoid? I wrote "Method: sentence" for all of them.

## 14. No rule for supplied content that the chosen voice has no place for

> "Map the content onto the recipe's sections in the voice's order."

The notes include a customer story ("we may tell"). The analyst order has no slot for it. The guide says how to map content, not whether I may drop some, and not whether dropped content must be reported. I dropped it and reported it under Assumed.

## 15. "Weak row marked" assumes a table with a worst performer

> "One breakdown table, weak row marked."

In a before and after trial every measured row improved. I marked the row with no data. Whether "weak" means worst result or weakest evidence is not stated.

## 16. The note length rule has three tiers and this request fits none

> "One sentence (beginner)", "Names recipe, voice, look, parts (advanced)", "'Use the artifact-studio skill' (expert)"

The request was three sentences plus notes. It named a structure but no recipe, voice or look by the guide's names. I wrote the full note.

## 17. Checklist items with nothing to check, and no "skip if none"

> "Every button does something, and it works by keyboard"
> "Tap targets 44 pixels. Use `svh` with a pixel cap, not bare `vh`."
> "At most three moving parts on a page."

The page has no buttons, no viewport heights and no motion. The guide gives a ceiling for moving parts and no word on whether zero is fine. "Big numbers that count up" is the first part listed, the analyst voice needs three numbers, and the look says "quiet". I chose static.

## 18. "60 to 75 characters wide" cannot be met at the width the guide says to design for

> "Design at 390 pixels wide first."
> "Read something through | One column, 60 to 75 characters wide"

At 390 pixels and a readable size a line holds about 44 characters. The rule only applies on wider screens and should say so.

## 19. "A section headed Preferences in your instructions" is ambiguous

> "If there is a preferences file (a file named `preferences.md`, or a section headed Preferences in your instructions), read it before you choose anything. It sets the defaults for recipe, voice, look, layout and format."

My session carried general writing-style preferences from the account, nothing about recipe, voice, look, layout or format. The guide does not say whether those count as the preferences file. I treated them as not the guide's preferences file and reported "none applied" in the note.

## 20. "Show the page as a rendered page if you can" has no fallback

> "Show the page as a rendered page if you can: an artifact, a canvas or a preview. A bare HTML file opens as code on many phones."

Here I could only write a file. The guide names the problem and gives no instruction for the case where I cannot render, other than implying the delivery is second-rate. I passed the warning on in the note.

## Not friction

The analyst order itself was clear and matched the request. The chart rules, the `[needs: ...]` syntax, the four note headings and "Rules that never bend" were easy to follow.
