# Friction with the guide

Every point quotes the line. BLOCKING means two careful readers would have built visibly different pages.

## BLOCKING

1. **What "three lines" means.**
   > "the text cut after three lines, a blank line counting as a line, with a control to read the rest"

   Lines of what? Three lines as typed in the draft (paragraph breaks), or three lines as rendered in the frame at 15 pixels? The two give different cuts. Draft 1 typed is "Good answer. Grey page." / blank / a full second paragraph, which renders as four lines. I cut at three rendered lines with CSS line clamp. A reader who counts typed lines shows more text and a taller frame. The guide also does not say whether the cut shows a mark (an ellipsis), or what to do when the third line is the blank one.

## MINOR

2. **The frame width and "side by side" pull against each other.**
   > "a frame at most 360 pixels wide with 15 pixel text on every screen"
   > "'Side by side' means side by side on a wide screen, from about 1000 pixels"

   Three frames of 360 pixels plus gaps need about 1130 pixels. At 1000 they cannot be 360. I let them shrink to 307, which "at most" allows, but then the three-line cut falls at a different word than at 360. The guide should say whether the frame is a fixed 360 or whether side by side starts later.

3. **"Pictures in one row".**
   > "pictures in one row cropped to one shape, four by three, with the subject in view"

   One row where? With one picture per draft, I read it as: the three pictures line up across the three frames. That only holds on a wide screen and only while all three drafts are closed. Opening one pushes its picture down. On a phone there is no row at all.

4. **"When there are several".**
   > "one picture for each draft when there are several"

   Several drafts or several pictures? It reads either way. It also does not say what happens with three drafts and two pictures, or four pictures.

5. **The picture order rule has a loose exit.**
   > "given out in the order the person attached them unless one plainly fits a draft's way in"

   The order of the drafts is mine to choose, so "the order attached" only binds after I have ordered the drafts. And "plainly fits" is a judgement call: the lightbulb full of paint fits the grey page problem about as well as the bored call does. I kept the attached order.

6. **Where the picture sits in the frame is not said.** The sentence lists "the name, the text cut after three lines ... one picture ... and the actions" but never says the picture goes under the text. I followed the list order.

7. **What the "control to read the rest" should be.** Same sentence as point 1. No wording, no place (at the end of the third line, or on its own line), and no word on whether it must work with scripts off. The checklist line only covers tabs:
   > "With scripts off, the first screen shows content and any tabs still switch"

   Next to "never ... copy its interface", the feed's own wording felt off limits, so I wrote "Read the rest" on its own line and built it from a checkbox and a label.

8. **How much of the feed post to draw.**
   > "the name ... and the actions as plain text, not buttons: Like, Comment, Share"
   > "Never draw a platform's logo or copy its interface"

   A feed post normally has a profile picture, a job line and a time. The guide lists only the name. I drew only the name. Another reader could add a blank circle for the profile picture and still claim to follow the guide.

9. **The look step has no entry for a post.**
   > "4. **Look.** In order: the person's design spec; colours taken from a picture ...; the voice's default look."

   For a post "the voice is the person's own", which has no default look. The answer is 130 lines later ("Frames are plain: a white frame on a pale grey page with dark text, whatever the voice"). Step 4 should point to it.

10. **The outline step has no entry for a post.**
    > "5. **Outline.** For a page, the voice gives the order (a deck follows its manner instead ...)"

    Nothing gives the order of a post. "A different way in" is not defined: is it only the opening line, or the whole order of the post? I changed the opening and the order that follows from it.

11. **Two crop rules, and no rule for a subject that does not fit.**
    > "Images: alt text that says what is shown, cropped to the subject at phone width"
    > "cropped to one shape, four by three, with the subject in view"

    The lightbulb picture is square and the bulb fills its height. Four by three cannot hold all of it. The guide does not say whether to cut the subject or to leave the shape. I cut the base of the bulb.

12. **How long the note should be.**
    > "Anything in between | Treat it as a one-sentence request and follow whatever it names"
    > "After a one-sentence request, one line under each is enough."
    > "If you cannot ask at all, decide, and say what you decided under Assumed."

    This request sits in between, so one line under each heading. But I had more than one assumption to record, and one line cannot hold them. I wrote more than one line.

13. **Is the link in a post a live link?** The guide says "opens with no network" and "Stop at the preview: nothing is posted or sent" but is silent on a web address inside the post text. I showed it as plain text.

14. **No word on getting the text out.**
    > "never hand back a list of text"

    The person still has to paste one draft into the platform. The guide does not say whether the page should offer a way to copy it, or whether the note may carry the plain text. I did neither. The person selects the text in the opened frame.

15. **The post preview rule is one sentence of more than a hundred words with a bracket that runs most of its length.** I had to read it four times to pull out the eight separate rules in it. It should be a list.

16. **Contrast list.**
    > "List the contrast ratio of each text and background pair in your note."

    This sits in the paragraph about building colours from one brand colour. It is not clear whether it applies to every page. I listed the ratios anyway.

Blocking: 1. Minor: 15.
