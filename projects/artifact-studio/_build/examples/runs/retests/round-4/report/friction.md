# Friction with the guide

Request: a one-page report from eight lines of notes, reader on a phone, answer first, colour "green", one open decision. Recipe one-page report, analyst voice.

I read "visibly different pages" as different sections, charts or facts on the page, not different wording inside the same structure.

## BLOCKING

**B1. Does the decision also appear in the next steps?**
> "make the report and give the decision a section, placed directly before the next steps and named in the answer at the top."

> "Next steps with owner and date."

The notes give two upcoming items: hire a worker, decide on a van. The guide gives the decision its own section and also asks for next steps with owner and date. It never says whether the decision is then repeated as a next-step row. One reader ends with a two-row list and the date "15 November" three times on the page. Another ends with a one-row list and puts owner and date inside the decision section. I repeated it. Say which.

**B2. A finding with two measures, and how facts group into findings.**
> "Up to three findings, each with its own full-sentence heading, its own chart and its own 'so what'."

> "Charts: one point per chart, stated as a sentence."

The quotes finding holds two comparisons: days to send (six to two) and share won (38% to 46%). One chart per finding and one point per chart means one of them goes uncharted. The guide does not say which, or whether to split it into two findings, which would push the van finding to a fourth and over the limit. With five facts and a cap of three findings, the grouping decides which charts exist. I charted the share and put the days in text. Another careful reader charts the days, or splits and drops the van finding into "what changed" prose.

**B3. Can "Method" and "Limits" be filled from notes that never mention either?**
> "A beat in the voice's own order that the content cannot fill is left out too, and listed under Missing."

> "Totals add up and derived numbers show their working"

> Avoid: "Topic headings, hidden limits"

The notes say nothing about method or limits. I filled Method with the working for the three derived changes and Limits with statements about what the notes do not hold (two months only, period of the 38% unknown, no proof of cause). A stricter reader says the content cannot fill either beat, leaves both out, and lists them under Missing. That is two whole sections present or absent. The guide needs one sentence on whether "what the notes do not say" counts as content for Limits, and where working is shown when there is no Method.

## MINOR

**M1. The decision lands seventh for a reader who wants the answer first.** "placed directly before the next steps" is clear, and I followed it. With analyst order that puts it after Method and Limits, a long scroll on a phone. I added a jump link from the answer. The guide does not mention one.

**M2. Which three numbers.** "Where the two disagree on a count or a heading, the voice wins" settles three, not four. It does not say which to drop when the notes hold four candidates. I dropped quote speed to a finding.

**M3. Note length contradicts itself.**
> "Anything in between | Treat it as a one-sentence request" and "one line under each of the four headings"

> "List the contrast ratio of each text and background pair in your note." / "Every gap, marked or not, is listed under Missing." / "give its hex code under Assumed"

A word-only brand colour forces a list of ratios and hex codes into a note that is meant to be four lines. I wrote the long note.

**M4. Are "Method", "Limits" and "Next steps" topic headings?**
> Avoid: "Topic headings" / "A heading on every section, key word first. A full sentence where the voice asks for one."

The voice asks for full-sentence headings on findings only, but bans topic headings everywhere. I wrote "Method: every figure comes from your notes." to satisfy both. A plain "Method" is equally defensible.

**M5. Where the brand colour sits takes three passages to work out.**
> "one accent for buttons and links" / "It may be the brand colour: then make buttons and links a darker step of it" / "Seven named colour tokens at most, plus one data colour if the page has a chart."

With a chart, the brand green is the data colour and the accent is a darker step. Without one, the accent is presumably the brand green. That is never said in one place.

**M6. "choose a mid-strength value yourself".** Two readers pick two greens. By design, but the pages differ in colour.

**M7. Comparison grey has no contrast rule.**
> "The comparison grey in a chart is the border colour."

A border light enough to be a border makes a weak bar (1.98 to 1 on the panel after I darkened it). The guide sets contrast for text only. I relied on the word and value labels.

**M8. "Source: your notes" is addressed to the wrong person.**
> "Where the source is the person's own notes, write 'Source: your notes'."

The reader is the business partner. They are not her notes. I wrote it as instructed.

**M9. Where "period, reader" sits.** Recipe: "Title, period, reader. Two-sentence summary." Voice: "The conclusion as the title. The answer in two sentences." The person said "answer first". I put a small period-and-reader line between title and answer. Nothing says whether that line may come between them.

**M10. An unstated comparison period.**
> "A gap is never filled with a guess." / "A date with no year is not a gap when the notes are plainly about now."

"46%, up from 38%" names no months. Neighbouring lines compare October with September. The guide covers a missing year, not a missing period that context implies. I wrote "before". A reader who writes "September" on the bar has a different chart label and a claim the notes do not make. Borderline blocking.

**M11. Is a month a date?** "a next step with no owner shows `[needs: owner]`" is clear. "November" for the hire has no day. I left it unmarked and listed it under Missing. "a deadline with no date" does not settle it.

**M12. What goes in a decision section when the notes hold no options.** The guide says to give the decision a section. It does not say what the section holds in a voice with no options table, when "an option or a recommendation the person did not give is a gap". Mine is the deadline, the one piece of evidence, and a line saying cost and options are absent.

**M13. Is a "so what" wording or a fact?** "Wording, structure and an analogy are yours to write" and "Facts are not yours to write". A "so what" is an interpretation, which is neither. I kept mine to restatements, so they are thin. A reader who treats it as wording writes a causal claim about faster quotes.

**M14. Where the person's extra ask goes.** The person asked for "three ways I could tailor this". The note has "four headings" and "Every request gets all four". I added a fifth section after them.

**M15. Moving parts.** "At most three moving parts" and "Big numbers that count up" sit beside the analyst's "Quiet". No guidance on whether quiet means none. I used none.

**M16. Three numbers: equal row or one large tile?** "Tiles of different sizes, one clearly the largest" against "The same padding and the same order in every card of a row". I made revenue full width and the other two half width.

**M17. Is "$" a currency?** The gap example is "a total with no currency". The notes say "$" with no country. I treated it as given and wrote "Unit: dollars".

Blocking: 3. Minor: 17.
