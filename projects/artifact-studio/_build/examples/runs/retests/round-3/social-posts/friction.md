# Friction with the guide

Task: three LinkedIn post drafts shown as feed posts, side by side, text cut where the feed cuts it.

## BLOCKING

**1. The cut depends on a width and a type size the guide never gives.**
> "a feed post (name, the text cut after three lines at the frame's width, a blank line counting as a line, with a control to read the rest"

"The frame's width" is not defined anywhere, and neither is the type size inside the frame. The cut point is a product of both. I used a 358 pixel frame and 16 pixel type. A reader who used a 320 pixel frame, a 550 pixel desktop-feed frame, or 14 pixel type cuts every draft at a different word. The person asked for the text "cut where the feed would cut it", so this is the one thing on the page they will judge, and the guide leaves it to chance. It also does not say whether the frame may change width between phone and wide screen (mine goes from 358 to 361 pixels on purpose, to keep the cut steady).

**2. The phone layout for "side by side" is offered two ways, and one of them breaks the next sentence.**
> "'Side by side' means side by side on a wide screen and a row to swipe, or a stack, on a phone. Nothing scrolls sideways except a table inside its own container."

A row to swipe scrolls sideways. The second sentence forbids what the first allows. Even if the swipe row is meant as an exception, there is no rule for choosing between it and a stack. I chose the stack because it cannot break the second sentence. Another careful reader builds a swipe row, and at phone width the two pages look nothing alike. The guide also gives no width at which the layout switches (I picked 1000 pixels).

**3. No rule on picture shape when the attached pictures have different shapes.**
> "Images: alt text that says what is shown, cropped to the subject at phone width"
> "The same padding and the same order in every card of a row."

The three pictures are 1.85 to 1, 1.52 to 1 and square. "Cropped to the subject" could mean leave each as it is (the subject already fills it) or crop all three to one shape so the frames match. One shape for all cuts into the lightbulb or loses a figure from the cartoon. I kept the natural shapes, so my three frames end at three different heights on a wide screen. A reader who cropped to one ratio gets three matching frames and different pictures. Nothing in the post preview paragraph settles it.

## MINOR

**4. Which actions, and whether "plain words" may be controls.**
> "the actions as plain words"
> "Never draw a platform's logo or copy its interface"
> "Every button does something, and it works by keyboard"

The guide does not name the actions. Writing the platform's own four action words in the platform's own order is close to copying its interface. Making them buttons breaks the checklist. I wrote Like, Comment, Repost, Send as plain list text. That was a guess.

**5. The read-the-rest control is unspecified.**
> "with a control to read the rest"

No label, no position (on the third line, as a feed does it, or under the text), and nothing on whether it closes again. A control placed on the third line takes room from the text and moves the cut. The guide gives a scripts-off pattern for tabs ("Build tabs from radio buttons and labels") and none for this, yet the checklist demands it work with scripts off. I used a checkbox and label, which gives a slightly odd checkbox to a screen reader.

**6. How the cut is marked is not stated.**
> "the text cut after three lines"

When three lines end exactly on a full stop, is there a mark? The browser's own clamp adds three dots after the full stop, so two of my drafts show four dots. The guide does not say whether to mark the cut, or how.

**7. Step 4 has no answer for a post, and the answer sits near the end of the guide.**
> "4. Look. In order: the person's design spec; colours taken from a picture, only when the person asks for its colours or it is the page's main picture; the voice's default look."
> "For a post, an email or a blog, the voice is the person's own."
> "Frames are plain: a white frame on a pale grey page with dark text, whatever the voice."

The person's own voice has no row in the voice table and no default look. Step 4 dead-ends until you reach the post preview paragraph. Step 4 should point there.

**8. Does "say the colours are yours" apply when the guide fixed the scheme?**
> "With no design spec, no brand colour and no picture, use the voice's default below. Say under Assumed that the colours are yours."

Here there were pictures (as illustrations) and the guide fixed white, pale grey and dark. The exact shades were still mine. I listed them and their contrast ratios to be safe. The contrast-list rule is written only for the one-brand-colour case, so I could not tell if it was wanted.

**9. Step 5 gives no order for a post.**
> "5. Outline. The voice gives the order."

The person's own voice has no order. The only structure rule for a post is "each with a different way in", with no examples of a way in. I chose the problem, the analogy and the first step. Another reader picks three others. Draft wording will always differ, so I count this minor, but a short list of ways in would help.

**10. The picture rule has a loose "several" and is circular.**
> "one picture for each draft when there are several, given out in the order the person attached them unless one plainly fits a draft's way in"

"Several" could mean several drafts or several pictures. Nothing covers two pictures for three drafts, or four. And since the assistant also picks the ways in, it can pick them to fit the pictures, so "plainly fits" tests nothing. Here the order and the fit agreed, so it did not change my page.

**11. The note length rule fights the content the note must hold.**
> "Anything in between | Treat it as a one-sentence request and follow whatever it names"
> "After a one-sentence request, one line under each is enough."
> "go by how they describe their voice and say so in the note"

This request was in between. One line under Assumed cannot hold the name line, the cut width, the wording I added, the action words and the colours. I wrote more than one line under each heading.

**12. "Your name" against the `[needs: ...]` rule.**
> "The name line shows the person's name if they gave it, and the words 'Your name' if they did not."
> "A part-missing number, name, date or price is marked `[needs: ...]` on the page."

A missing name on a post the person will publish under their name looks like a `[needs: ...]` case. The post rule is more specific, so I used "Your name" and listed the gap under Missing. The guide should say the post rule wins. The notes also held a handle (bar181) inside the link. I did not treat it as a name. The guide does not say.

**13. Two audiences, one "reader".**
> "Find the content, any media, the audience, the one job of the page, and where it will be seen."

A post preview has the person choosing a draft and the feed readers the post is for. The guide does not say which one "the audience" is at intake. I took the page's reader to be the person and the drafts' reader to be the feed.

**14. Nothing on getting the text out, or on the link.**
> "never hand back a list of text"

The person has to copy a draft to post it. The guide forbids a text list and says nothing about a copy control or whether the full text may also go in the note. I left it at "Read the rest" and select. It is also silent on whether a link inside a previewed post is a live link. I made it plain text.

Blocking: 3. Minor: 11.
