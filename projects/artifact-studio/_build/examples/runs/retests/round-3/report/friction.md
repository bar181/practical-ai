# Friction with the design guide

Run: one-page report for a landscaping company, analyst voice, green.

## BLOCKING

### 1. When a wholly missing owner gets a `[needs: ...]` mark

> "Next steps with owner and date."

> "Mark a gap on the page as `[needs: ...]` in one case only: the page shows a number, a name, a date or a price the reader will act on, and part of it is missing (a total with no currency, a deadline with no date). A whole section or beat the content cannot fill is left out, not marked."

The notes give two next steps and no owner for either. That fits neither sentence. The beat can be filled, so it is not left out. But the page "shows" no name with a part missing: the name is absent entirely. "A deadline with no date" suggests a next step with no owner should be marked, so I wrote `[needs: owner]` twice. A second reader drops the owner line and lists it under Missing only. The pages differ visibly.

The same sentence, read strictly, also catches "15 November" and "October": each is a date the reader acts on with its year missing. I did not mark the year. A stricter reader would put `[needs: year]` in the header.

### 2. A finding with nothing to chart, and how many charts the page has

> "Up to three findings, each with its own full-sentence heading, its own chart and its own 'so what'."

> "One trend chart with a full-sentence heading."

> "Where the two disagree on a count or a heading, the voice wins."

> "Never pad a section"

Two problems in one place.

First, the recipe says one chart and the voice says one per finding. I treated that as a disagreement "on a count", so the voice won and the page has two charts. A reader who does not see "one chart" against "a chart each" as a count draws one chart.

Second, the van finding (off the road four days, no jobs moved) has no comparison to draw. The guide does not say whether a finding without a chart is still a finding, gets a token chart, or moves somewhere else such as the recipe's "What changed". I kept it as a finding with no chart. Between these readings the page has one, two or three charts and two or three findings.

### 3. Where the decision section goes

> "asked for a report that also has to support a decision, make the report and give the decision a section."

> "put in each section the content can fill, where it fits that order"

The analyst order has no decision beat, so "where it fits that order" gives no answer. I put it after the findings, because finding 3 is its only evidence, and named it in the two-sentence answer at the top. A second reader puts it first, because the partner "wants the answer first" and the decision is what she acts on. A third folds it into Next steps. All three follow the guide and the pages differ.

## MINOR

### 4. Which three numbers

> "Three numbers." (analyst) against "Three or four numbers with their change." (recipe)

The count rule settles three. The notes hold four candidates: revenue, jobs, quotes that became jobs, and days to send a quote. The guide gives no way to choose which one loses its tile. I dropped quote turnaround into finding 2.

### 5. A brand colour by word is not in the look order

> "In order: the person's design spec; colours taken from a picture, only when the person asks for its colours or it is the page's main picture; the voice's default look."

"Our colour is green" is none of the three. The Colour section handles it later ("If they name the colour by word only"), and "Nothing to go on" mentions "no brand colour", but step 4 does not. I treated the word as a thin design spec.

### 6. Brand colour as data colour, accent, or both

> "A chart's data colour is its own token. It may be the brand colour: then make buttons and links a darker step of it"

> "one accent for buttons and links"

"May" leaves it open whether the bars are the brand green. And this page has no buttons and no links, so the accent has no stated job. I used the accent for the decision panel, on the strength of "One accent, used for what to act on".

### 7. The comparison grey must double as the border colour

> "The comparison grey in a chart is the border colour."

A border light enough to be quiet is too faint to read as a bar. The guide gives a contrast figure for text and none for chart marks. I darkened the border token to 3.2 to 1 against the page, which makes every line on the page heavier than a quiet look wants.

### 8. "Source: your notes" addresses the wrong person

> "Where the source is the person's own notes, write 'Source: your notes'."

The page's reader is the business partner, not the person who wrote the notes. "Your notes" is wrong for her. I followed the line as written and flagged it under Check before sharing.

### 9. The short note cannot hold what the guide requires in the note

> "After a one-sentence request, one line under each is enough."

> "List the contrast ratio of each text and background pair in your note."

> "give its hex code under Assumed"

> "Every gap is listed in the note"

Seven tokens, six contrast pairs and ten gaps do not fit in one line under each heading. I read "enough" as a floor and wrote a longer note.

### 10. "Topic headings" against the analyst's own beat names

> "Avoid: Topic headings" beside "Method. Limits. Next steps with owner and date."

> "A heading on every section, key word first. A full sentence where the voice asks for one"

The analyst asks for full-sentence headings on findings only, and its closing beats are named with topic words. I wrote "Method: ..." as a key word plus a sentence. A reader could as fairly write a bare "Method".

### 11. How far "never show one number twice" reaches

> "never show one number twice to satisfy both lists. A tile and a chart of the same measure is not showing it twice"

The rule is scoped to the two lists. It does not say whether the two-sentence answer may repeat a figure from the tiles, or whether a deadline may sit in the answer, the decision section and the next steps. "Derived numbers show their working" also forces the Method section to restate every figure. I kept numbers out of the title and the answer, apart from the deadline.

### 12. Inference against guess

> "A gap is never filled with a guess."

The notes say "46%, up from 38%" without months. Any reader would take those as October and September, but the guide draws no line between an inference like that and a guess. I labelled the bars "Before" and "Now", which sits awkwardly beside "September" and "October" on the revenue chart.

### 13. What counts as "a section headed Preferences in your instructions"

> "a file named `preferences.md`, or a section headed Preferences in your instructions"

A chat assistant often carries stored user preferences about writing style that have nothing to do with pages. The line sweeps those in, and then requires "Say in the delivery note which preferences you applied". It is not clear whether general style preferences count or only page defaults.

### 14. Where the rest of the person's request goes

> "The page, then a note with four headings: Chose, Assumed, Missing, Check before sharing."

The person also asked for three ways to tailor the result. The guide does not say whether an answer like that sits inside the note, after it, or outside it. I put it after the four headings, under a rule.

Blocking: 3. Minor: 11.
