# Friction with the guide

1. **BLOCKING. One chart or three.** The analyst voice says "Three findings: full-sentence heading, one chart, a "so what"." That reads as either one chart per finding or one chart for all three. The recipe says "One trend chart with a full-sentence heading". The tie-break "Where the two disagree on a count or a heading, the voice wins" does not help, because I cannot tell what the voice's count is. I built one chart. The other reading gives three.

2. **BLOCKING. When a gap gets a `[needs: ...]` marker.** "If the page would mislead without it, a number, a name, a date or a price the reader will act on, mark it on the page as `[needs: ...]`." The grammar allows two readings: mark only gaps that mislead, or mark any missing number, name, date or price the reader acts on. The analyst voice demands "Next steps with owner and date" and the notes give no owner and no van cost. Under the first reading the page stays clean. Under the second it gets three markers. I marked them. Step 5 also says "leave out any it cannot fill", which points the other way for the owner field.

3. **BLOCKING. "Green" with no value.** "If the person has one brand colour and nothing else, keep its hue and build seven colours from it". A colour word is not a hue. The guide does not say whether to ask, pick one, or fall back to the voice default. I picked `#2e8b47`. Every colour on the page follows from that guess.

4. **BLOCKING. Does the "one number twice" rule cover a chart.** "never show one number twice to satisfy both lists". The recipe wants "Three or four numbers with their change" and "One trend chart". The obvious chart is revenue, which is also the obvious first tile. I could not tell whether a tile plus a chart of the same figure counts as showing it twice. I read it strictly and charted quote time (six days, two days), so the page's main number has no chart. A looser reading gives a revenue chart.

5. **BLOCKING. Fewer findings than the voice's count.** The voice says "Three findings". Step 5 says "leave out any it cannot fill" and "Never pad a section", but those are written about the recipe's list, and "the voice wins" on a count. The notes fill two findings once the three numbers are in tiles. I showed two and labelled them "1 of 2" and "2 of 2". The guide never says what to do when the content falls short of the voice's own count.

6. **BLOCKING. Two jobs, and a named recipe pointing the other way.** "If the request names two jobs, the page does the one its reader will act on and the other becomes a section." The reader will act on the van decision, which makes the page "A decision brief or a memo: No recipe. The executive brief voice is the whole page". But the person asked for "a one-page report" and step 2 says "If named, use it". I kept the report and made the decision a section. The other route gives a different page, and it would be mostly `[needs: ...]` because the notes hold no options.

7. **MINOR. "Wants the answer first" and voice choice.** "If named, use it. Otherwise take the recipe's first voice." The guide does not say whether an audience wish counts as naming a voice. Both of the report's voices put the answer near the top, so the default (analyst) happened to fit. With a different wish the rule would give the wrong voice.

8. **MINOR. A brand colour is missing from the look order.** Step 4 lists "the person's design spec; colours taken from a picture...; the voice's default look". A lone brand colour appears only later, under "Nothing to go on" and under Colour. I had to infer that it ranks above the voice default.

9. **MINOR. "Source: your notes" addresses the wrong person.** "Where the source is the person's own notes, write "Source: your notes"." The reader here is the business partner, not the person who wrote the notes. The rest of the page says "we" and "our". I used the guide's wording as written and flagged it in the note.

10. **MINOR. The comparison bar is the border colour.** "The comparison grey in a chart is the border colour." A border colour is pale by design. Against the panel it measures 1.8 to 1, so the "before" bar is faint. The contrast rules cover text only, so nothing stops this. The value is labelled in words, so no meaning is lost.

11. **MINOR. "Avoid: Topic headings" against the voice's own section names.** The analyst order names "Method. Limits. Next steps", which are topic headings, and the Layout table says "A heading on every section, key word first. A full sentence where the voice asks for one". I wrote key word, colon, sentence ("Limits: two months make a comparison, not a trend."). The guide does not say this is what it wants.

12. **MINOR. Length of the note.** "Anything in between: Treat it as a one-sentence request" gives "one line under each of the four headings". Elsewhere: "List the contrast ratio of each text and background pair in your note", "Every gap is listed in the note" and "Say under Assumed". Nine contrast pairs and seven gaps do not fit in one line each. I wrote the full list.

13. **MINOR. Derived numbers.** "You never add facts" and "Only numbers from the content" sit beside "derived numbers show their working". I took simple differences as allowed and showed the sums. The guide does not say whether a percentage change or revenue per job is also allowed, so I left both out.

14. **MINOR. Where a person's extra question goes.** The person asked for "three ways I could tailor this". Step 9 fixes the note at "four headings: Chose, Assumed, Missing, Check before sharing" and says nothing about answering anything else. I put the answer under a rule after the four headings.

15. **MINOR. "Show the page as a rendered page if you can".** The guide does not say what to do when the only output is a file, beyond the warning that "A bare HTML file opens as code on many phones". That matters here because the reader is on a phone. I repeated the warning in the note.

16. **MINOR. "Title, period, reader" with no year, company name or reader's name.** The recipe's first section assumes all three exist. The guide does not say whether "business partner" is enough for the reader, or whether a missing year is a gap that misleads. I used what the notes hold and listed the rest under Missing.

Blocking: 6. Minor: 10.
