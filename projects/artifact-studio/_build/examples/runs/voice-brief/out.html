The decision

Do we keep drafting a first reply to every enquiry after the trial ends on 31 May?

During the trial our median first reply fell from 26 hours to 38 minutes, and site visits booked rose from 18 a month to 31. It costs about $1,800 a year, and jobs won are not counted yet, so the trial does not show whether it brings in more work.

Background

  1. We are a home renovation company. Enquiries arrive by web form, email and telephone. Before the trial, 12% were answered within the hour.
  2. What changed: a reply is drafted the moment a message arrives. A member of staff reads it, fixes it and sends it.
  3. Our rules: a person approves every reply. The first reply confirms and offers a visit. It never states a price.
  4. Setting it up took about an hour. We collected three replies we are proud of, wrote our rules on half a page, saved both in a chat project, tested it on five old enquiries, then used it on the next real one.
  • Median first reply 38 minutes Was 26 hours.
  • Answered within the hour 81% Was 12%. Up 69 points (81 − 12).
  • Office manager's time on replies 2.5 hours a week Was 6. Frees 3.5 hours a week (6 − 2.5).

Options

Option Cost a year What we know
A. Keep drafting first replies Recommended About $1,800 ($150 a month × 12) The trial results: median first reply 38 minutes, 81% answered within the hour, 2.5 hours a week of the office manager's time.
B. Hire a part-time coordinator About $26,000, which is about $24,200 more than A (26,000 − 1,800) Not tried. We have a cost for it and no results.
C. Do nothing: the trial ends on 31 May Weak point: the slowest replies No tool cost How things were before the trial: median first reply 26 hours, 12% answered within the hour, 6 hours a week of the office manager's time.

Recommendation

Choose option A. Keep drafting first replies after 31 May, keep the three rules as they are, and start counting jobs won. In the trial the median first reply went from 26 hours to 38 minutes, the office manager got back 3.5 hours a week, and the cost is about $1,800 a year against about $26,000 for a coordinator.

Risks and controls

  • A drafted reply goes out wrong. Control, in place: a person approves every reply, and the first reply never states a price.
  • The rise in visits may not come from faster replies. This is one company, there was no comparison group, and spring is the busy season. Control, proposed: keep recording site visits booked each month after spring and compare.
  • We do not know the effect on jobs won. They are not counted yet. Control, proposed: count jobs won from now on.

Next steps

  • By 26 May: the owners choose A, B or C.
  • 31 May: the trial ends. Under A, drafting carries on with the same three rules. Under C, it stops.
  • If A is chosen: the owners name who counts jobs won.
