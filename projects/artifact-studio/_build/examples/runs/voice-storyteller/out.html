Beat 3 of 8, the problem at three depths

By Wednesday night, none had answered.

  1. First depth: what she saw Three messages out. A day later, nothing back from any of the three.
  2. Second depth: how ordinary it is At one home renovation company, the median first reply took 26 hours. Counted from a Tuesday evening, 26 hours ends on Wednesday night. A median means half of the first replies took at least that long.
  3. Third depth: how rare a quick answer was At the same company, 12% of enquiries were answered within the hour. The other 88% were not (100 less 12).

Beat 4 of 8, the guide: empathy, then proof

That company was us.

Those were our numbers before the trial. We know the wait Priya had, because people who wrote to us had it too. Their enquiries reached us by web form, email and telephone, and the median first reply took 26 hours.

So we ran a trial. It covered February to April: 90 days and all 214 enquiries.

In the trial, 81% of enquiries were answered within the hour. A person approved every reply.

Beat 5 of 8, the plan in three steps

Three steps for every enquiry.

  1. A reply is drafted the moment a message arrives.
  2. A member of staff reads it and fixes it.
  3. That member of staff sends it.

Our rules

  • A person approves every reply.
  • The first reply confirms and offers a visit.
  • It never states a price.

Three questions

How long did it take to set up?

About an hour. We collected three replies we are proud of, wrote our rules on half a page, saved both in a chat project, tested it on five old enquiries, then used it on the next real one.

Do more visits mean more jobs?

About one visit in four becomes a job. Jobs won are not counted yet, so this page claims none.

Which enquiries did the trial count?

All 214 from the 90 days. Enquiries arrive by web form, email and telephone.
