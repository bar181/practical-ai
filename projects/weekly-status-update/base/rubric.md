# Rubric: weekly status update

A rubric for judging a status update, written on 23 November 2026 before any update was scored with
it. Use it to check your own update, or paste it into a fresh chat with the update and ask for a
score with the exact words that justify each line. (The [Rubric reviewer](../../rubric-reviewer/) does
this.) Draft, written by AI; not yet
used on real updates.

Adjust the lines before you score, then keep them fixed week to week, so the scores can be
compared.

```
Rubric: weekly status update
Written: 23 November 2026, before any update was read
For: the reader who decides, in about two minutes, whether the work needs their help
Pass mark: none set

| Line | Weight | Full marks looks like | Evidence to look for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Status up front | 25 | The first line after the subject says on track, at risk or off track, with one reason, and the status matches the facts below it | The first line, and the risks section |
| Decisions needed are clear | 20 | Any decision the reader must make is named, with options and a date; or it says plainly that none is needed | A "Decision needed" line |
| Changes shown against last week | 20 | Every date or figure that moved shows the old and new value | "What changed since last week" |
| Nothing from last week went missing | 15 | Every risk, date and promise from last week's update is settled, moved or listed as carried over | "Carried over", compared with last week's update |
| Risks say what they affect | 10 | Each risk names the date or figure it threatens and what would settle it, where known; nothing about either is guessed | "Risks and open questions" |
| Short and plain | 10 | Under 200 words; short sentences; no unexplained jargon | Length, any paragraph |

Gates (pass or fail, no points):
- No date, figure, name or promise that is not in the notes or last week's update.
- No blame on a named person.
- Nothing the reader was told last week was hidden at someone's request.

A reviewer also needs: this week's notes and last week's update, to check the gates and the
"nothing went missing" line.
```

## Why these lines

- **Status up front** carries the most weight because it is the line the reader acts on first.
  "Matches the facts below it" catches the common failure: "good week overall" above a problem
  that threatens a date.
- **Nothing from last week went missing** targets the mistake this tool is built to catch: a risk
  or promise that quietly stops being mentioned.
