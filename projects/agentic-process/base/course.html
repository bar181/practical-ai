Build the right AI tool

Learn to turn a job you repeat into a small AI tool that someone else can use, and to prove it works before anyone relies on it.

Who it is forBusiness owners, employees and students who use a chatbot at work. No code is needed. Two optional lessons in Module 5 are for people who build with a coding agent.
What you need
  • A chatbot you already use, such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot
  • One job you do on your own every week that starts from a blank page
  • About 4 and a half hours, in one day or over five weeks, plus the capstone
How it runsEach module has a lecture deck, step-by-step lessons, a three-question check and an assignment. It ends with a capstone: your own tool through steps 1 to 8, with step 9 planned.

By the end of the course you can

  1. Say what a tool does in one sentence: one user, one input, one result.
  2. Check what already exists, and decide whether to stop, wrap it or build.
  3. Write a one-page plan and the first screen a user will see, before anything is built.
  4. Test your tool with a stranger, a fresh chat that has only your instructions, and read what it tripped on.
  5. Plan how the tool grows, one addition at a time, and know which level of the process fits your work.

The fresh-chat test

Module 4 · 3 / 8

Instructor notes. 3 min. A new chat that remembers nothing gets only your instructions and one real input. It does the job and lists every place it had to guess. You do not help it. You read the list before the result.

Blocking or minor

Blocking

  • A different guess would change the result
  • Fix it

Minor

  • The result would be the same
  • Note it; fix it if cheap

Module 4 · 4 / 8

Instructor notes. 2 min. Every point in the log is marked one or the other. This stops people rewriting everything because of one complaint. Example: 'It did not say what tone to use' is blocking for an email tool.

Fix in this order

  • First: every point that appears in two or more logs
  • Then: every blocking point
  • Then run a second round

Module 4 · 5 / 8

Instructor notes. 2 min. One log can be noise from that request or that session. The same point twice means the instructions are wrong.

The author is the worst reader of their own instructions

A typical find: one step says stop if the answer is yes, while every other step says go on if yes. The author reads past it every time. A stranger trips on it at once.

Module 4 · 6 / 8

Instructor notes. 2 min. Ask: what would a stranger trip on in your instructions? Take two guesses, then say: stop guessing and run the test.

Show the answer

Module 2: Decide: is it worth building? · Lesson 2.1

Scope

This lesson: about 10 minutes · Step 1 of nine · In the coach chat

You will: Decide who the tool is for and the one job it does, before anything is built. A tool that does one job gets finished. A tool that does five gets rebuilt.

Do this

  1. Read what this lesson is for (above) and what to expect (below).
  2. In the coach chat from lesson 1.3, paste the prompt. Fill in the brackets. If you closed that chat, open a new one and paste your plan first.
  3. Check what comes back against "Proof you are done". Tick each one you have.
  4. Answer the gate question. If the answer is no, fix the step before you go on.
  5. Add the result to your plan.

What to expect

The step itself takes about 5 minutes. Expect to drop something you wanted: the first answer is usually two or three jobs.

Who it helps

  • Beginner As someone with a job I repeat every week, I want to say the job in one sentence, so that the AI builds that and nothing else.
  • Advanced As a builder working with a coding agent, I want the scope written into the plan, so that the agent has something to refuse extra work against.
  • Expert As a team lead whose agents work unattended, I want one user and one job agreed before any agent starts, so that I am not reviewing five half-built features.

Ask me up to two questions, one at a time. Then write:
1. The one user.
2. The one job, in one sentence: what goes in and what comes out.
3. Three things the first version will not do.
4. The one thing that would make someone stop and look.

This lesson: about 15 minutes · Step 2 of nine · In the coach chat

You will: Find out whether something you already have, or something free, does the job. The cheapest tool is the one you do not build.

Do this

  1. Read what this lesson is for (above) and what to expect (below).
  2. In the coach chat from lesson 1.3, paste the prompt. Fill in the brackets. If you closed that chat, open a new one and paste your plan first.
  3. Check what comes back against "Proof you are done". Tick each one you have.
  4. Answer the gate question. If the answer is no, fix the step before you go on.
  5. Add the result to your plan.

What to expect

The step itself takes about 10 minutes with web search, longer without. Expect to find something that does part of the job. Often the result is that you build less than you planned.

Who it helps

  • Beginner As someone with a job I repeat every week, I want to know whether my chatbot already does this, so that I do not spend a week rebuilding a built-in feature.
  • Advanced As a builder working with a coding agent, I want links and dates for what exists, so that I can wrap an existing tool and not start from nothing.
  • Expert As a team lead whose agents work unattended, I want the reuse check written down, so that nobody on the team builds what we already pay for.

Prompt to paste

Before I build anything, check what already exists for this job: [the one-sentence job from step 1].

Look in this order: features already built into the assistant I use ([name it]), free and open-source tools, and how people who do this well usually do it. Search the web if you can. If you cannot, say so and list what I should look up.

Give me a table: the option, what it covers, what it costs, its limits, a link, and today's date. Do not invent a product or a price.

Then tell me plainly: does something existing do the whole job? If yes, tell me to stop, and write me a short guide to it. If no, say in one line what is left to build.

This lesson: about 10 minutes · Steps 3 and 4 of nine · In the coach chat

Do this

  1. Read what this lesson is for (above) and what to expect (below).

  5. Add the result to your plan.

What to expect

The step itself takes about 5 minutes. For most beginner tools the answer is that there is nothing to buy. Write that down and go on. The step itself takes about 5 minutes. Expect the first version to cost nothing new. If it needs something paid, that usually belongs in a later level.

Plan and front door

This lesson: about 15 minutes · Step 6 of nine · In the coach chat

You will: Write the one-page plan and the first thing a user will see, before the tool exists. If the front door cannot be written, the tool is not understood yet.

Do this

  5. Add the result to your plan.

What to expect

The step itself takes about 10 minutes. Expect to cut. If a section of the plan needs more than five lines, the scope has grown.

Who it helps

  • Beginner As someone with a job I repeat every week, I want one page I can read in two minutes, so that I know what I am about to build and how I will test it.
  • Advanced As a builder working with a coding agent, I want the first screen of the README written first, so that the agent builds toward what a user will actually try.
  • Expert As a team lead whose agents work unattended, I want one worked example for each kind of request, so that a reviewer can compare a real result against a known good one.

Prompt to paste

Write the one-page plan for this tool from what we have decided so far. Then write the front door, the first thing a new user reads: one sentence that would make a stranger stop, two sentences on who it is for and what they get, and one thing they can try in five minutes.

Then write one worked example for each kind of request the tool handles differently (most first versions need one): a realistic input and the result I should expect. Say that each example is invented.

Plan the next addition

This lesson: about 10 minutes · Teaches step 9 of nine

You will: Plan how your tool will grow, so that the next change comes from real use and not from ideas.

Do this

  3. Put a date in your calendar two weeks from now. Use the tool until then, and build nothing new.
