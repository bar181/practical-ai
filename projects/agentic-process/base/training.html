By the end

You will have a one-page plan for an AI tool of your own.

And the one test that finds its faults before anyone else does.

Promise one thing they will leave with. Ask: who has asked an AI to build something, and got something close but wrong? Hands up. That is today.

The problem

Your AI can build almost anything. That is the problem.

It builds fast, with confidence, and it does not ask whether it is the right thing.

Pause on this one. The point is that speed is not the risk. Building the wrong thing well is the risk.

Think of it as

A building site with an inspector.

You agree the drawing before you pour concrete. You frame before you choose curtains. Each stage is signed off before the next.

Give the picture before the steps. Come back to it whenever someone is lost. The puppy is the inspector: friendly, brief, nothing gets past.

Turn to your neighbour

Say one job you repeat, in one sentence.

One user. One thing goes in. One thing comes out.

One minute each. Listen for people describing three jobs. Ask them which one they would miss most on Monday.

Nine steps, four stages

Decide. Design. Build and test. Grow.

  1. Decide: scope, reuse check, outside help, cost.
  2. Design: simplest design, plan and front door, review.
  3. Build and test: the smallest version, tested by a stranger.
  4. Grow: one addition at a time.
Read each stage once. Do not explain each step: the guide does that. Ask them to note which one they usually skip. Most say the reuse check or the test.

Every step has

A purpose. A proof. A prompt. A gate.

  • Purpose: why the step exists.
  • Proof: what you are holding when it is done.
  • Prompt: what to paste into your chatbot.
  • Gate: the question you answer before you go on.
If you have the guide open on a second screen, open step 1 and show the four parts. The gate for step 1 is: can you say the job in one sentence?

Stop talking. Start a ten-minute timer. Walk the room. The full nine steps take about an hour, which is why the class uses the short path. If a chatbot says it cannot open the link, the slide shows the file to copy instead.

A stranger reads it

Open a new chat. Paste this, then your one-page plan.

“You have never seen this project. Read the plan below. Explain it back in five sentences, then list every place where you had to guess, quoting the line. Do not improve the plan. Only report.”

Read where it had to guess. That list is your homework.

This is step 7 in miniature. The new chat must have no history: a new conversation, not the same one. Ask one person to read out the first thing it guessed.

Check yourself

Three questions.

  • The reuse check finds a free tool that does the whole job. What now?
  • Why fix the folder layout before anything is built?
  • A fresh session finishes the job and lists twelve problems. Did the test pass?
Answers: stop and write a guide to the free tool. Because every file points at other files, and moving them breaks links. Yes: it finished, and the twelve problems are the next hour's work.

Keep this

One job. Check what exists. Fix the layout. Let a stranger test it.

The guide, the coach and every prompt: github.com/bar181/practical-ai, in projects/agentic-process
