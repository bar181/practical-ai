# Worked example: A Monday email from sales notes

**Level:** Beginner. **Made on** 16 November 2026 by a new AI session that had only the
[build coach](../AI-INSTRUCTIONS.md) and the message below. It could not ask questions and did
not search the web, so it decided where the coach would ask, and marked prices and existing tools
"not checked". Nothing below the line has been edited. Nobody has yet used what it made. The
person in the message is invented: a kitchen-fitting company owner who is not technical.

**The message it was given**

> Read the build coach and follow it.
>
> My idea: every Monday I turn last week's sales notes into an email for my team. I want an AI to draft it. I run a small kitchen-fitting company with six staff. I am not technical. I use ChatGPT on my phone and laptop. The notes are a few lines I type into my phone during the week.

**Where it tripped** is in
[`friction.md`](../../level-3-files/fresh-run/runs/round-2/monday-email/friction.md). Read that too: it
is the honest half of the example.

---

## The plan

**The job.** Turn the few lines of sales notes you typed last week into a draft Monday email to
your team.

**The user.** You. You type the notes, run the tool, check the draft and send it. Your team
reads the email. They never touch the tool.

**What is out.** Sending the email for you. Collecting the notes for you. A separate email for
each person. Sales figures, charts or forecasts. Anything that is not in your notes. Any app
beyond ChatGPT, your notes and your email.

**The wow.** Proposed, yours to change: you paste a few scrappy lines and get back an email
that sounds like you, says nothing you did not write, and tells you what it was unsure of.

**What already exists.** Not checked. I did not search. ChatGPT on its own does not count here,
because the job needs written instructions. What to look up is in the note. No paid options are
listed, for the same reason.

**Cost.** The first version adds nothing new to pay for. Prices and limits of what you already
use were not checked.

| Needs | Price | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ChatGPT, the account you use now | not checked | not checked |
| The notes app on your phone | not checked | not checked |
| Your email | not checked | not checked |

**The design.** ChatGPT does the drafting. Two notes on your phone hold everything:
"Monday email: instructions" and "Sales notes: this week". If your notes app has folders, both
go in one folder called "Monday email". Typing notes, copying, checking and sending stay manual.

    your notes -> ChatGPT -> draft -> you check -> your email -> you send

**The steps.**
1. Once: save the instructions as a note and put your first name in.
2. In the week: one line per sales note in "Sales notes: this week".
3. Monday: copy the instructions, start a new ChatGPT chat, paste, send.
4. The first thing you see: "Paste last week's sales notes and I will draft the Monday email."
5. Paste your notes, send.
6. Read the draft and the list under "Check before sending". Fix what is wrong.
7. Copy the email into your email, add your team, send it yourself.
8. Clear the sales notes for the new week.

**The test.** Input: the five invented notes in the worked example. A good result is yes to all
six:
1. Each of the four clear notes is in the email.
2. No number, name, date or reason appears that is not in the notes.
3. The unclear "worktop supplier" note is not guessed at. It is under "Check before sending".
4. There is a subject line, a greeting and your name at the end.
5. The email is under 150 words.
6. It does not offer to send anything.

Three weak points, from reading the plan as a stranger:
- A few thin lines may tempt the AI to pad or guess. The instructions forbid it. Untested.
- Copying between apps on a phone is fiddly, and nothing warns you if you copied only part.
- Your notes may hold customer names and prices. Pasting them into ChatGPT was not checked.

**Next three additions.** Smallest first, one at a time, and only once the first version is in
use.
1. Add two or three of your own past Monday emails to the instructions, so it sounds more like you.
2. Keep the instructions inside ChatGPT so you stop pasting them. Whether your ChatGPT can do
   this is not checked.
3. Speak your notes in place of typing them. Same job, a second way in.

## The instructions

Save everything in the box as a note called "Monday email: instructions". Change
[YOUR FIRST NAME] to your first name, once.

```
You are helping me write my Monday email to my team. I run a small kitchen-fitting company.
During the week I type a few lines of sales notes into my phone. Your job is to turn those
notes into one draft email from me to the whole team.

If I have not pasted any notes yet, do not write an email. Reply with only this line:
"Paste last week's sales notes and I will draft the Monday email."
When I paste the notes, write the draft.

Rules

1. Use only what is in my notes. Do not add any number, name, date, price, customer, reason,
   praise or promise that is not there. If the notes are short, the email is short.
2. My notes are shorthand. Turn each one into a plain full sentence. If you had to work out
   what a short form means, say so under "Check before sending".
3. If a note is unclear or looks unfinished, do not guess. Leave it out of the email and list
   it under "Check before sending".
4. Write the way a boss talks to a small crew: plain words, friendly, straight to the point.
   No sales talk, no exclamation marks, no emoji.
5. Keep the email under 150 words.
6. Use this layout:
   Subject: Sales update: last week
   Morning all,
   Here is where sales got to last week.
   Then a short list, one line for each note, in the order I wrote them.
   Then sign off with: [YOUR FIRST NAME]
7. After the email, add the heading "Check before sending". List every note you left out,
   everything you were unsure of, and every short form you had to work out. If there is
   nothing, write "Nothing to check."
8. This is a draft. I will read it, change it and send it myself. Do not offer to send it.
```

## One worked example

This example is invented. The streets, names and jobs are made up. The email below was written
by the coach to show what good looks like. It is not a result from ChatGPT.

The notes that go in:

```
- Marsh Rd kitchen signed, deposit in
- 2 quotes out: Okafor, the flat on Bell St
- Showroom visit Sat, couple want quote for utility room
- Lost the Penrose job, went with cheaper
- worktop supplier - lead time??
```

A good result:

```
Subject: Sales update: last week

Morning all,

Here is where sales got to last week.

- The Marsh Rd kitchen is signed and the deposit is in.
- Two quotes went out: Okafor, and the flat on Bell St.
- A couple visited the showroom on Saturday and want a quote for a utility room.
- We lost the Penrose job. They went with a cheaper quote.

[YOUR FIRST NAME]

Check before sending
- "worktop supplier - lead time??" I left this out. I could not tell if it is a question for
  the team or a reminder for you.
- "went with cheaper": I wrote "a cheaper quote". Change it if you meant something else.
- "Sat": I wrote "Saturday".
```

## Not checked

I did not search the web. Nothing below was looked up. Please look these up yourself.

- What already exists: whether your ChatGPT can keep a set of instructions so you do not paste
  them every week. Whether your email app or your notes app has its own "draft this for me"
  feature. If you record sales in any other software, whether it can write a weekly summary.
- Paid options: what the paid ChatGPT plans add for this job and how they charge. Whether there
  are paid tools made for weekly team updates, what each covers and how each charges.
- Cost: which ChatGPT plan you are on, its price and its limit on messages. Whether your notes
  app and your email cost you anything.
- Privacy: what ChatGPT does with customer names and prices you paste in, and whether you are
  content to paste them.
- Fresh chat: whether a new chat in your ChatGPT carries anything over from earlier chats.
- The test: not run by anyone. No result exists yet.
