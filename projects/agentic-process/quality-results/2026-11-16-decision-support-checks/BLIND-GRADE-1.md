## Which would a busy owner rather receive?

A busy owner would probably rather receive A. It reads like advice from someone who knows bakeries: a concrete menu, a budget table, team guardrails and a paragraph they can forward to the partner as-is. B is the more trustworthy and auditable document, but its scoring mechanics, repeated SWOT and process sections make the owner dig for the brief in section 5.

## Same decision, or materially different?

It is the same decision. Both say no to a full launch, test local demand cheaply first, then run a capped pilot using only existing products with the head baker's hours protected. Both spend roughly $3,000 before a go/no-go gate.

They differ in scale after the gate. A runs all three shops for 90 days at $8,500 to $15,000 and projects $75,000 to $85,000 a year. B runs one shop for 8 weeks capped at $3,000 and projects about $21,600 a year.

B's sizing is better calibrated. A's $15 per head is roughly double the local continental-breakfast benchmark B found ($7.20). B also adds the Public Health, insurance and HST checks A leaves out. That makes B the slightly sounder version of the same decision, not a different or materially better one.
