The instructions left several things to interpret. They say to score "against each goal", but two of the user's three goals (raising afternoon sales and avoiding burnout) are partly limits as well, and "the partner has a say" is neither a goal nor a pass-or-fail limit. I treated the partner as a "who should see this" item rather than a test. The scoring rule (any score that rests on a not-found fact is 0) made every demand-dependent score 0, so the result is that do nothing "leads" with the small test recommended as the check. In practice that reads as "test it", but the wording "doing nothing leads" while recommending option C may confuse readers. The order "do the steps in order, but write the brief first" means the SWOT belongs in both step 4 and the brief. To avoid repeating it, I put the labelled table in the brief and cross-referenced it from step 4. It was unclear whether "every fact gets a source and date" means inline citations, so I gave a sourced fact table in the working and a source list in the brief. I also couldn't use numbered citation tags because the fetched pages had no sentence indices, so I used markdown links. The "about 400 words" length was easy to overshoot once the labels were included; the brief came out at about 390 words excluding the SWOT and sources. "Never contact anyone" applies to me, so the demand check is framed as calls the owner makes. Finally, budget, head-baker and risk tests all needed assumed readings, which I marked as such.
