## Which would a busy owner rather receive?

A busy owner would rather receive B: it leads with "yes, but as a pilot", reads like a plan they could start Monday (menu, days, caps, a catering lead, budget, scorecard), and hands them a paragraph they can forward to their partner as-is. A is the more trustworthy document (every number checks out, every limit is tested, every claim is labelled), but its useful brief is wrapped in a restated profile and a long method section that a busy owner will not read. The cost of choosing B is that its numbers are less verified, its budget contradicts its own "under half" claim, and it skips the insurance, HST and Public Health checks that A names.

## Same decision, or is one materially better?

It is the same decision: do not launch full catering; run a small, capped pilot using only existing baked goods, protect the head baker's hours, test local demand first, and decide against agreed thresholds. The differences are in scale and test design, not direction. A's pilot is smaller and cheaper (one shop, 8 weeks, $3,000 cap, 10 office-manager conversations before any spend), which fits the medium risk comfort and the burnout goal better. B's pilot is bigger (three shops, 90 days, $8,500 to $15,000) but its demand test asks for paid first orders, which is a stronger signal than A's conversations, and it is more fully specified operationally. Neither is materially better as a decision; a sensible owner could take A's scale with B's paid-order test and B's operating guardrails.
