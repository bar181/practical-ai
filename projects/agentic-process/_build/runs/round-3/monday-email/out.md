# Monday sales email: hand-over

Short path used (small job: one person, one kind of input, one result, inside a chat).
Steps 1, 6 and 8 in full; steps 2 to 5 in one line each; step 7 skipped; step 9 left open.
Nobody has tried this yet. It is built, not used.

---

## 1. The plan (one page)

**The job.** Every Monday, turn last week's sales notes (a few lines typed on your phone) into a draft email for your team, ready for you to read and send.

**The user.** You, the owner of the kitchen-fitting company. You do the job today and you will do it with the tool. The six staff read the email; they do not use the tool.

**What is out.**
- Sending the email. You read it and send it yourself from your own email app.
- Collecting or tidying the notes during the week. You keep typing them as you do now.
- Any other email or report (for example a monthly roundup). That would be a second job: see Next three additions.
- Anything that needs code, files, other software or a new subscription.

**The wow (proposed, not tested).** You paste rough phone notes and get, in under a minute, an email you would send with only small edits. Nothing is made up: every fact in the email comes from your notes, and anything unclear comes back as a question for you, not a guess.

**What already exists.** Not checked (no web search was done). You use ChatGPT already, and that is what the first version runs in. Before using the prompt, try one plain sentence in ChatGPT first (see Test this next). If that alone gives an email you would send unchanged, stop: you need no tool.

**Cost.** Nothing new to buy. The first version uses only the ChatGPT you already have, plus a note on your phone. Your ChatGPT plan, its price and its limits: not checked. The cost gate passes only because the first version buys nothing new.

**The design.**
- Assistant: ChatGPT, on your phone or laptop, as you use it now.
- Where it lives: one note on your phone called "Monday email prompt". You copy from it each Monday.
- What stays manual: typing the notes, pasting them in, reading the draft, answering its questions, sending the email.

**The steps (what you do each Monday).**
1. During the week, keep typing sales notes on your phone, as you do now.
2. On Monday, open the note "Monday email prompt" and copy all of it.
3. Open a new ChatGPT chat and paste it.
4. Under "My notes:", paste last week's notes. Send.
5. Read the draft. If there are questions under it, answer them in the chat or fix the email by hand.
6. Copy the email into your email app and send it to the team yourself.

**The test.** The worked example below: the invented notes go in, and the result is checked against the "good result" list. Not yet run.

**Next three additions (smallest first, one at a time).**
1. Save the prompt inside ChatGPT so it is one tap away, if your plan allows saving instructions (not checked).
2. A simple way of typing the notes during the week (for example, start each line with Sold, Quote, Lost or Note) so drafts come out cleaner.
3. A second job: a monthly roundup built from the four Monday emails. Only after the Monday email is in use.

## 2. The first version

### The prompt (keep this in a phone note called "Monday email prompt")

Replace [your name] once, the first time you save the note.

```
You help me write my Monday email to my team. I run a small kitchen-fitting company with six staff. Below are my sales notes from last week, typed quickly on my phone. Turn them into an email to the team.

Rules:
1. Use only what is in my notes. Do not add any number, name, job, price or date that is not in my notes.
2. If a note is unclear, do not guess. Leave it out of the email and list it as a question for me under the email, headed "Questions for you before sending".
3. Start with a subject line.
4. Open with one sentence that gives the main news of the week.
5. Then two short lists: "Last week" (what happened) and "This week" (anything the team needs to do or know). Leave a list out if my notes have nothing for it.
6. Plain, friendly words. No jargon. Under 200 words.
7. End with "Thanks, all." and my name: [your name].
8. Keep my spelling of names and places exactly as in my notes.
9. Do not send anything. I will read it and send it myself.

My notes:
```

### The worked example (invented data: these notes, names and dates are made up for testing)

Keep this separate from the prompt. You paste it under "My notes:" only when testing.

**Input (invented):**

```
signed Patel kitchen, fit starts 2 Dec
3 quotes out - Lee, Okafor, Brown. chase Fri
lost Green job, went cheaper elsewhere
Sam measure Harris?? maybe weds
```

**A good result (invented):**

```
Subject: Last week's sales: Patel kitchen signed, 3 quotes out

Hi all,

Good news first: we signed the Patel kitchen, and the fit starts on 2 Dec.

Last week
- Patel kitchen signed. Fit starts 2 Dec.
- Three quotes went out: Lee, Okafor and Brown.
- We lost the Green job. They went somewhere cheaper.

This week
- Friday: chase the Lee, Okafor and Brown quotes.

Thanks, all.
[your name]

Questions for you before sending
- Is Sam measuring up the Harris job, and is it on Wednesday?
```

**What makes it good (the checklist for the test):**
- It has a subject line.
- The first sentence gives the main news.
- Every note appears, and nothing is added: no prices, names or dates that are not in the notes.
- The unclear Harris line is a question under the email, not a fact inside it.
- Under 200 words, in plain words.
- You would send it with no more than small edits.
