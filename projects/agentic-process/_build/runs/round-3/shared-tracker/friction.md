# Friction with COACH.md

1. **BLOCKING.** "The user is whoever will do the job with the tool. The person talking to you may
   be the builder and not the user." Here the AI reads the notes, five reps write them, and the
   team lead approves. Nobody is named as the one who runs it on Friday. I chose the team lead.
   If a rep runs it instead, the front door and the Friday steps change.

2. **BLOCKING.** "Step 5: Say which assistant, where files live, and what stays manual." The
   message never says where the call notes are kept or in what form, and the coach forbids
   deciding "a fact about the world". I designed around pasting notes into the chat, which works
   wherever they are, but that is a guess that shapes the whole design.

3. **BLOCKING.** "Say which assistant" plus "Never invent a price, a product or a fact." The person
   says "Microsoft Copilot at work", which can mean versions that do or do not open files. Paste
   versus point-at-the-file is a different design. I could not check, so I chose paste.

4. MINOR. Step 2 gate: "You cannot know that unseen: go on" versus the general rule "A gate you
   cannot answer stays open: say so and go on." It is unclear whether gate 2 counts as passed,
   assumed yes, or open. I marked it open and went on.

5. MINOR. Step 3: "list paid options with what each covers and how it charges" versus "Never
   invent a price, a product" and, with no search, "go on as if nothing was found". Step 3 can
   only list kinds of help to look up, so the step's own task cannot be done.

6. MINOR. "You may decide a choice ... You may not decide a fact about the world: ... what people
   said". Whether the team lead agrees to approve every Friday is something a person would have
   to say. I recorded it as assumed and put "ask the team lead" under Test this next.

7. MINOR. "Propose a folder layout and fix it" sits awkwardly with the beginner row ("The first
   version is a prompt they can paste. No code"). I gave a three-file shared folder.

8. MINOR. "Keep it beside the prompt, not inside it" and step 8 "a fresh chat that has only
   those instructions". It is not said whether the fresh chat may receive the example's input. I
   had the tester paste the example input but not the good result.

9. MINOR. "A person approves anything that posts, sends, pays or publishes." Editing a shared
   spreadsheet is not on the list. I treated it as needing approval, and kept all sheet edits
   manual.

10. MINOR. "A second result is a second job." Whether "notes that match no row" and "deals with
    no notes" are part of the result or second results is not clear. I kept unmatched notes in
    (so nothing is silently dropped) and moved deals-with-no-notes to Next.

11. MINOR. Step 1: "Ask what would make people stop and look." It is not said whose attention
    (the lead, the reps, or someone above). I proposed a wow aimed at the lead.

12. MINOR. Step 4: "List every dependency of the first version with its price and its limit."
    With nothing checked, almost every price and limit cell reads "not checked"; the gate rule
    covers this, but the table adds little.

13. MINOR. Step 7: "Ask who will read the plan." With no way to ask, I decided nobody, which
    forces the self-review path every time.

14. MINOR. Example data: the worked example needs spreadsheet columns, which the message does
    not give. "Examples use invented data and say so" allowed me to invent them, but the prompt
    then has to be written for unknown columns.

15. MINOR. "What you hand over at the end" lists three items; it is not said whether the
    step-by-step session belongs in the result. I included a short session log before the
    hand-over.

Blocking: 3. Minor: 12.
