Chapter 1 of 5

What it is, and why it exists

See the problem this project solves, what you get from it, and what has been measured so far.

About 6 minutes

Think of it as

A building site with an inspector.

You agree the drawing before you pour concrete, and nothing moves to the next stage until the current one is signed off. Here the stages are nine steps, the sign-off is a plain question called a gate, and the inspector at the end is an AI chat that has never seen your work.

The North Star

A person, or an agent working alone, can take an idea or a spec to a tool that someone else can use, without rebuilding it three times on the way.

Three mistakes it stops

The wrong job

Ask for five things and get a tool that does none of them well.

Caught at step 1

It already existed

A built-in feature or a free tool already did the job.

Caught at step 2

Only you can use it

It works in your chat, with everything you said earlier, and breaks for anyone else.

Caught at step 8

What you leave with

A one-page plan

Fifteen headings, five lines each at most. Seven on the short path.

Instructions anyone can paste

With one worked example kept beside them.

A test you can rerun

A fresh chat, your instructions, one input, and its list of guesses.

What has been measured

Every test so far gave a fresh AI session only this project's files and one request, and counted the points that tripped it. They are AI sessions in Claude, not people, and nobody has been timed.

Chapter 2 of 5

The nine steps, and why each one exists

Scroll. The diagram stays at the top and walks with you: the stage, the gate, and the time so far.

About 10 minutes

Scroll to walk the nine steps.

9

Nine steps, four stages, a gate after each

Decide (steps 1 to 4): is it worth building at all? Design (5 to 7): the plan, before anything exists. Build and test (8): a stranger tests it. Grow (9): one addition at a time.

A gate is a question worded so that yes means go on. No means fix the step. Stopping at step 2, because something already does the job, counts as a win.

Decide

1

Scope

One user, one job

Why it exists
Decide who the tool is for and the one job it does, before anything is built. A tool that does one job gets finished. A tool that does five gets rebuilt.
What you write
One user, one job, what is out, and the one thing that will make people stop and look
Time
About 5 minutes. Running total: about 5 of 60 (estimates).
Done when
  • One sentence: who it is for, what goes in and what comes out
  • Three things the first version will not do
  • One test: an invented input that looks like the real thing, and what a good result looks like

The gate. Can you say the job in one sentence?

Decide

2

Reuse check

Does it already exist?

Why it exists
Find out whether something you already have, or something free, does the job. The cheapest tool is the one you do not build.
What you write
What already does this: built in, free, common practice
Time
About 10 minutes. Running total: about 15 of 60 (estimates).
Done when
  • A short list of what exists, each with a link and the date you looked
  • One line saying what is still left to build
  • If nothing is left: a short guide to the existing tool, and you stop here

The gate. After what already exists, is there still something to build? If not, stop and write a guide to that thing.

Decide

3

Outside help

Build it or buy it

Why it exists
For whatever is left, list the services you could pay for, so that the choice between building and buying is made on purpose.
What you write
Paid services for whatever is left. Do not pick a provider. Decide only build, buy or by hand for each part
Time
About 5 minutes. Running total: about 20 of 60 (estimates).
Done when
  • A list of paid options for what is left, or the words "nothing to buy"
  • For each part of the tool: build it, buy it, or leave it as something done by hand

The gate. Do you know what you would build and what you would buy?

Decide

4

Cost

What it costs to run

Why it exists
Put a price and a limit on everything the first version depends on. A tool people cannot afford to run is not finished.
What you write
Every dependency of the first version, with its price and its limit
Time
About 5 minutes. Running total: about 25 of 60 (estimates).
Done when
  • A table: each thing the first version depends on, its price, its limit, and who pays
  • One line: the first version costs nothing new, or it costs this much

The gate. Does the first version cost nothing, or an amount you accept?

Design

5

Simplest design

Fewest parts, fixed layout

Why it exists
Choose the fewest moving parts that do the job, and fix where every file lives before any file is written.
What you write
Which assistant, where files live, what stays manual. The folder layout, fixed now
Time
About 5 minutes. Running total: about 30 of 60 (estimates).
Done when
  • Three lines: which assistant, where the files live, and what stays manual
  • A short list a beginner could copy out: what goes in, what the AI does, what comes out, who checks it
  • For a project folder (Advanced only): the layout check passes

The gate. Could a beginner draw it?

Design

6

Plan and front door

One page, and the first screen

Why it exists
Write the one-page plan and the first thing a user will see, before the tool exists. If the front door cannot be written, the tool is not understood yet.
What you write
The steps a person follows, the first screen they see, one worked example for each kind of request it handles
Time
About 10 minutes. Running total: about 40 of 60 (estimates).
Done when
  • The plan template, filled in, on one page
  • The first screen: a hook, and one thing a person can try in five minutes
  • One worked example: an input, and the result you would be happy with

The gate. Is the beginner version fully described?

Design

7

Review

Someone else explains it back

Why it exists
Have someone who is not the author read the plan and explain it back. You cannot see your own gaps, because you know what you meant.
What you write
Someone who is not the author reads the plan
Time
About 5 minutes. Running total: about 45 of 60 (estimates).
Done when
  • The reviewer's summary, in their own words
  • A list of what was unclear, and what you changed
  • Who reviewed it and when: a name, or "a fresh AI session"

The gate. Can someone else explain it back to you?

Build and test

8

Build and test

A stranger tests it

Why it exists
Build the smallest version that does the job, then hand it to a stranger: a new AI session that has only your instructions. It finds what you cannot.
What you write
The beginner version. Then a test by a fresh AI session that has only your instructions
Time
About 15 minutes. Running total: about 60 of 60 (estimates).
Done when
  • The first version: instructions someone can paste, or a folder someone can open
  • The result the fresh session produced, unedited
  • Its list of where it had to guess, and a note of what you fixed
  • One more run with a different input of the same kind, after the fixes

The gate. Did a fresh session finish the job, and have you read what it tripped on?

Grow

9

Grow

One addition at a time

Why it exists
Add one thing at a time, and only after the level below is in use. Small additions get reviewed. Big ones get waved through.
What you write
One addition, one change request. What you learned
Time
Repeats after the tool is in use: one addition at a time, each a change you can review in five minutes.
Done when
  • A list of the next three additions, smallest first
  • For each addition made: what changed, who approved it, and a new test run
  • Evidence the current level is in use: who used it, for what, and when

The gate. Is the level below in use before you build the next?

That is the whole process

About 60 minutes for steps 1 to 8 on a small job: an estimate. The short path, for one person turning one input into one result in a chat, uses seven of the plan's headings and skips nothing that matters.

The same nine steps run at every level. What changes is who runs them and what checks them: chapters 3 and 4.

Chapter 3 of 5

Run it: three ways in

Pick the path that fits you and follow it step by step. Each one runs the same nine steps.

About 5 minutes

Beginner

In any chatbot

A chatbot you already use. Nothing to install.

123456789

  1. Copy the coach prompt below into your chatbot. Change the last line to your job.

    Read the build coach at https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bar181/practical-ai/main/projects/agentic-process/base/AI-INSTRUCTIONS.md and follow it. If you cannot open it, say so and stop: do not make up a coach of your own.

My idea: [one job you do on your own, in one sentence]

  2. Say yes to the short path if one person turns one kind of input into one result. Otherwise take all nine steps.

  3. Answer its questions. It stops at every gate and waits for you.

  4. At the plan, ask it: What did you assume? Fix what is wrong.

  5. For the test, open a Temporary Chat in ChatGPT or Gemini, an incognito chat in Claude, or any chat with memory and custom instructions turned off. Paste the test prompt the coach gives you, your instructions and one invented input.

  6. Read its list of guesses before the result. Fix repeats first, then blocking points. Test once more.

Tested: six runs by fresh AI sessions, in Claude. Not yet run in ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot.

Advanced

With a coding agent

A coding agent such as Claude Code, and a git repository.

123456789

  1. Copy the skill folder into your agent's skills, from the repository root:

    mkdir -p .claude/skills && cp -r projects/agentic-process/level-2-files/agentic-process .claude/skills/

  2. Tell the agent:

    Use the agentic-process skill. Start at step 1. My job: [one sentence]

  3. At step 5 it makes the project folder:

    python3 projects/agentic-process/level-2-files/agentic-process/new_project.py <slug> "<Title>"

  4. Run the gates whenever you like. filled fails until step 6 and built until step 8, by design:

    python3 projects/<slug>/_build/checks/gates.py projects/<slug>

  5. For step 8, start the fresh-run tests yourself, one new session for each kind of request.

Tested: two runs by a second agent, one of them unattended in an empty repository.

Advanced

From a spec you already have

A plan in workshop/plans/ or a draft in workshop/drafts/, and a coding agent.

123456789

Filled: the steps this path runs. Outlined: steps 1 to 6, checked against your spec instead of asked.

  1. Make the project folder for the plan:

    python3 projects/agentic-process/level-2-files/agentic-process/new_project.py <slug> "<Title>"

  2. Tell the agent:

    Use the agentic-process skill. The plan is in workshop/plans/<slug>/README.md. Check it against the gates for steps 1 to 6 and list what is missing. Then do steps 7 and 8, Beginner level only. Stop at every gate and wait for me.

  3. Run the two fresh-chat tests yourself. Fix only what blocks.

  4. Keep one note: where Agentic Process tripped you. That note is the next fix list for this project.

  5. Tell us in two minutes with the form at the end of this course.

Not tried yet. This is how the project tests itself on its own next tool.

Automation and the advanced levels

See what checks the work at each level, what can run without you, and what has actually been tried.

About 4 minutes

Run them all with one command at any time. A gate that cannot be answered without a person is open, not failed.

The levels

LevelWho runs itWhat checks itStatus
BaseYou, in any chatbotThe coach asks each gate question; a fresh chat tests the resultTested by AI sessions in Claude
