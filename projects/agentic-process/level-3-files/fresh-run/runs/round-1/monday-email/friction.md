# Friction: where COACH.md left me guessing

1. **BLOCKING.** Step 2's gate runs the opposite way to the general gate rule, and the stop branch is underspecified.
   - General rule: "Go on only when the answer is yes. If it is no, stay on the step."
   - Step 2: "\"Does something existing do the whole job?\" If yes, stop: write them a short guide to that thing"
   - Read literally, "no" means stay on step 2 for ever and "yes" means end the session. I read step 2 as the exception (no = go on).
   - The answer itself was a coin toss. The person already uses ChatGPT, and the first version is a prompt for ChatGPT, so "yes, ChatGPT does the whole job, here is a short guide" was a fair reading. That would have produced a guide and no plan. I answered "no" and carried on. The coach gives no test for "the whole job", and does not say whether the plan and note are still handed over after a stop.

2. **BLOCKING.** Step 8's gate cannot be passed in a one-go session, and the coach does not say whether to hand over with it open.
   - "\"Did the fresh chat finish the job, and what did it trip on?\""
   - "Go on only when the answer is yes."
   - "If you cannot ask questions at all, decide, and list every decision under \"Assumed\" at the end."
   - "Never invent a price, a product or a fact."
   - A test result is a fact, not a decision, so "decide" would mean inventing it. I left the gate open and handed over anyway. The other reading (no hand-over until the test passes) gives no result at all.

3. **BLOCKING.** Step 9 is excluded for this kind of person, but its output is a required heading.
   - "An idea in a sentence, and they are new to this | Steps 1 to 8 in plain words."
   - "The plan, on one page, with these headings: ... Next three additions."
   - "Take them from the idea to a first version someone else can try, in nine steps."
   - I skipped step 9 and its gate but wrote the three additions. Either choice changes the result.

4. **BLOCKING.** "Decide" collides with "never invent" for things only the person knows.
   - "Ask what would make people stop and look."
   - "If you cannot ask questions at all, decide"
   - "Never invent a price, a product or a fact."
   - "The wow" is a required plan heading. What would make this team stop and look is a fact about them. I wrote a guess and labelled it assumed. The same applies to the tone and length of the email, which shape the prompt itself. The coach does not say where deciding ends and inventing begins.

5. **MINOR.** Steps 2 to 4 without search produce only look-up lists, yet their gates need the answers.
   - "If you cannot, say so and list what the person should look up."
   - "list paid options with what each covers and how it charges"
   - "List every dependency of the first version with its price and its limit"
   - "\"Does the first version cost nothing, or an amount you accept?\""
   - With no prices checked, gate 4 can only be answered by assuming. It is also unclear whether naming a product or feature from memory counts as "invent ... a product". I gave names as search terms only, marked not checked.

6. **MINOR.** Step 5 assumes files and folders.
   - "Say which assistant, where files live, and what stays manual. Propose a folder layout and fix it"
   - For a person with a phone and a pasted prompt there are no files. I turned it into three notes in the phone's notes app. "fix it" could mean freeze it or repair it. I read freeze.

7. **MINOR.** "the first thing they see" has no object.
   - "Write the steps a user follows, the first thing they see, and one worked example"
   - First thing they see in the plan, in the saved instructions, or in the assistant's first reply? I chose the top line of the saved instructions note.

8. **MINOR.** The test, the worked example and the step 8 example may be one thing or three.
   - Step 1: "Write one test: a specific input and what a good result looks like"
   - Step 6: "one worked example"
   - Step 8: "plus one worked example"
   - Plan heading: "The test."
   - I used one set of invented notes for all of them.

9. **MINOR.** Step 7's gate always passes when the coach reviews its own plan.
   - "If nobody can, review it yourself as a stranger and list three weak points"
   - "\"Can a reviewer explain it back?\""
   - The coach wrote the plan, so it can always explain it back. The coach also does not say whether the three weak points must be fixed before going on. I fixed one and carried two to the note.

10. **MINOR.** "One user" does not say whether the user is the person running the tool or the person reading its output.
    - "Find one user and one job."
    - I chose the owner, not the team.

11. **MINOR.** The step count and step names clash with "plain words".
    - "Say which step you are on: \"Step 3 of 9: outside help\"."
    - "Steps 1 to 8 in plain words."
    - Is it "of 9" or "of 8" for this person? "Reuse check", "front door" and "gate" are not plain words for someone who is not technical. I kept the names and "of 9".

12. **MINOR.** The hand-over does not say whether the step-by-step record is part of it.
    - "End every step by writing down what was decided, in two or three lines"
    - "What you hand over at the end: 1. The plan ... 2. The first version ... 3. A note"
    - I put a short step record ahead of the three items. The same decisions then appear three times: in the step record, in the plan and under "Decided".

13. **MINOR.** "Decided" and "Assumed" blur when the person is absent.
    - "A note with four headings: Decided. Assumed. Not checked. Test this next."
    - "You do the drafting. They make the decisions."
    - With nobody answering, every decision is an assumption. I put design choices under Decided and everything I would have asked under Assumed.

14. **MINOR.** "on one page" is not measurable.
    - "The plan, on one page, with these headings"
    - Ten headings including the full steps and the test is tight. I kept each to a few lines and did not count a page.

15. **MINOR.** "a fresh chat that has only those instructions" may not be possible in the person's assistant.
    - "tell the person how to test it in a fresh chat that has only those instructions"
    - An assistant that carries memory or saved settings across chats is not fresh. The coach gives no fallback. I told the person to look it up.
