## 1. The plan

**The job.** Turn last week's sales notes into a draft of the Monday email to the team.

**The user.** You, the owner of a kitchen-fitting company with six staff. Not technical. ChatGPT on phone and laptop.

**What is out.** Sending the email. Gathering the notes. Anything that is not in the notes. Any other email. Running on a timer.

**The wow.** Assumed, not asked: you paste a few rough lines and get back a ready-to-read email with nothing made up in it, plus a short list of anything it could not read.

**What already exists.** Not checked. You already have ChatGPT. Whether it can store the instructions for you, and whether your email app drafts with AI, are both to look up (see the note).

**Cost.** Nothing new, assuming you keep using what you have. Prices and limits of your ChatGPT plan: not checked.

**The design.** ChatGPT does the drafting. One folder in your phone's notes app, "Monday email", with three notes: "1 Instructions", "2 This week's notes", "3 Example". You type, paste, check and send.

**The steps.**

Once:
1. Make the folder and the three notes.
2. Paste the instructions from part 2 into "1 Instructions". Type your first name after "which is:".
3. Paste the worked example from part 2 into "3 Example".

During the week:
4. Type your sales notes into "2 This week's notes".

On Monday:
5. Copy "1 Instructions" and paste it into a new ChatGPT chat. Do not send yet.
6. Copy "2 This week's notes", paste it at the bottom, and send.
7. Read the draft against your notes, line by line. If there are questions under "Check before sending", answer them in the chat and it will give you the email again.
8. Copy the email into your email app and send it yourself.
9. Empty "2 This week's notes" for the new week.

**The test.** Open a new ChatGPT chat with nothing else in it. Paste the instructions, then the invented notes from the worked example, and send. A good result:
1. The reply is a subject line, then the email.
2. The four clear lines are all in the email: the Hartleys saying yes and the fit starting on the 30th; Mrs Okafor, galley kitchen, 9.2k; 2 new enquiries from the leaflet; the Mill Rd job lost for being too dear.
3. Nothing is in the email that is not in the notes. No month added to "30th", no currency sign added to "9.2k", no praise or comment.
4. "BT chasing worktop sample" is not in the email. It is under "Check before sending" with a question.
5. You can read it on your phone in about a minute.

Then write down whether it finished the job and what it tripped on. ChatGPT may carry things over from your other chats. Whether it does on your account, and how to start a chat without that, is not checked. Look up "ChatGPT temporary chat" and "ChatGPT memory settings".

**Next three additions**, smallest first, one at a time:
1. Add one of your own past Monday emails to the instructions as a sample of how you write.
2. Store the instructions inside ChatGPT so you stop pasting them each week, if the look-up shows your plan can.
3. Keep each email you send, and paste last week's in with the new notes so the draft can pick up where the last one left off.

## 2. The first version

Who has tried it: nobody. It is built, not used.

### The instructions to paste

Copy everything between the two lines into the note "1 Instructions". Type your first name after "which is:". That is the only change to make.

---

I run a small kitchen-fitting company with six staff. Every Monday I send the team an email about last week's sales. Please draft that email from my notes, which are at the bottom of this message.

About the notes: I type them into my phone during the week, a few lines at a time, so they are short and rough. Expect half sentences, shorthand, and sometimes only a name and a number. They are the only source for the email. My team will take what the email says as fact, so a name, price or date that I did not type would cause real trouble. Where the notes say nothing, the email says nothing.

What I would like back:

1. A subject line, then the email, written so I can copy it straight into my email app. I read every draft and send it myself.
2. Plain, friendly wording, the way the owner of a six-person firm talks to people they work beside every day. Ordinary sentences, with no headings, no sales talk, and no praise or comment that is not in my notes.
3. Short enough to read on a phone in about a minute.
4. Every clear line of my notes should appear somewhere. Put related lines together, and put first anything the team has to do or get ready for.
5. Names, prices and dates stay exactly as I typed them. If I typed "30th", write "30th" and leave the month out.

Some lines will be unclear to you: shorthand you cannot read with confidence, initials that could be more than one person, a number with nothing to say what it is. Leave those lines out of the email. Under the email, add a short list headed "Check before sending" with each unclear line as I typed it and one question about it. When I answer, give me the full email again with those lines worked in. If every line was clear, write "Nothing to check" there.

Sign the email off with my first name, which is:
(If nothing is written after that colon, end the email without a sign-off.)

If there are no notes below the next line, reply with only "Paste last week's notes" and wait for them.

My notes from last week:

### One worked example

Everything in this example is invented: the names, the jobs, the price, the dates and the owner's first name "Sam". I wrote the result by hand to show what good looks like. It did not come out of ChatGPT.

The notes that go in:

```
Tue: quoted Mrs Okafor, galley kitchen, 9.2k
Wed: Hartleys said yes! fit starts 30th
Thu: 2 new enquiries from the leaflet
Fri: lost Mill Rd job, too dear they said
BT chasing worktop sample
```

A good result:

```
Subject: Last week's sales

Hi all,

Here is how last week went.

The Hartleys said yes on Wednesday, and their fit starts on the 30th.

On Tuesday I quoted Mrs Okafor 9.2k for a galley kitchen.

On Thursday we had 2 new enquiries from the leaflet.

On Friday we lost the Mill Rd job. They said we were too dear.

Sam

Check before sending
- "BT chasing worktop sample": who is BT, and what should the team do about the sample?
```
