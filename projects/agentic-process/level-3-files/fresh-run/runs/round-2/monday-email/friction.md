# Friction

Places where COACH.md was unclear, contradicted itself or left me guessing.

1. **BLOCKING.** "Every gate is worded so that yes means go on. If it is no, stay on the step"
   and "nothing moves to the next stage until the current one passes", against "If you cannot
   ask questions at all, decide". The coach never says whether I may answer a gate for the
   person. A gate answer is neither clearly "a choice" nor clearly "a fact about the world".
   Gate 4 ("an amount you accept") is the person's own consent. I answered all nine gates yes
   and listed each under Assumed. If that is not allowed, the session stops at step 1 and
   there is no hand-over.

2. **BLOCKING.** "The assistant they already use counts only if it does the job with no
   instructions written", with "go on as if nothing was found". Two guesses in one place.
   First, is a single typed line such as "turn these notes into a team email" an instruction
   written? If it is not, ChatGPT already does this job and the session should stop at step 2
   with a short guide, not a plan. I read any typed request as written instructions and
   carried on. Second, with no search the answer to gate 2 is forced to yes, so the reuse
   check cannot stop anything in a no-search session.

3. **BLOCKING.** Step 4: "List every dependency of the first version with its price and its
   limit" and gate "Does the first version cost nothing, or an amount you accept?", against
   "Never invent a price, a product or a fact. Say 'not checked' where you did not check."
   With no search every price is "not checked", so the gate asks the person to accept an
   amount nobody has stated. I passed it on the reading "no new spending is added". The coach
   does not say whether a cost table made only of "not checked" can pass the gate.

4. **BLOCKING.** Step 1 "Write one test: a specific input and what a good result looks like",
   step 6 "one worked example", step 8 "plus one worked example", hand-over "the prompt or
   instructions, and one worked example". The coach does not say whether these are one thing
   or three, or whether the worked example goes inside the prompt or beside it. Inside, the
   AI may copy invented names into a real email. Beside, the prompt has no example to learn
   from. I used one invented input for all of them and kept it outside the prompt. The other
   reading gives a different first version.

5. MINOR. "list every decision under 'Assumed' at the end" against the note heading
   "Decided". In a session with no questions every decision is an assumption, so "Decided" is
   left with nothing of its own. I put what the person's message and the fixed rules settled
   under Decided and every choice of mine under Assumed.

6. MINOR. The plan headings ("The job. The user. What is out. The wow. What already exists.
   Cost. The design. The steps. The test. Next three additions.") have no place for step 3
   (outside help) or step 7 (the three weak points). I put paid options under "What already
   exists" and the weak points under "The test".

7. MINOR. "Say which step you are on" and "End every step by writing down what was decided,
   in two or three lines", against "What you hand over at the end", which lists three items
   and no record of the steps. In a single pass it is not said whether the step-by-step record
   is part of the output. I folded it into the note under Assumed, one entry per step.

8. MINOR. "Propose a folder layout and fix it." "Fix" could mean settle or repair. For a
   person who is "new to this" and gets "a prompt they can paste. No code", a folder layout
   barely applies. I read it as "settle where the two notes live".

9. MINOR. "Ask what would make people stop and look." Which people: the team reading the
   email, or anyone shown the tool? The coach allows "a proposed wow", so I proposed one aimed
   at the owner.

10. MINOR. "The user is whoever will do the job with the tool." Here the owner is builder and
    user. The six staff read the result and the coach has no word for them. "Shape the first
    version for the user" says nothing about shaping the result for its readers, and nothing
    is known about them.

11. MINOR. Step 7: "If nobody can, review it yourself as a stranger and list three weak
    points", then gate "Can a reviewer explain it back?". When the coach is the reviewer the
    coach passes its own gate. It is not said whether the weak points must be fixed before
    going on or only listed. I listed them and went on.

12. MINOR. "The plan, on one page". A page of what is not said. Ten headings that include the
    steps, the cost table and the test make one page tight. I kept each heading to a few lines.

13. MINOR. Step 8: "test it in a fresh chat that has only those instructions". The coach does
    not say what fresh means for someone using an account on a phone, and "Never invent ... a
    product" stopped me naming any setting that might carry things over. I wrote "start a new
    chat, type nothing else" and put the doubt under Not checked.

14. MINOR. Between the two instruction files, not inside the coach: RULES.md says "Use only
    facts from the person's message. Invent nothing." COACH.md says "Examples use invented
    data and say so" and requires a worked example. I followed the coach for the example and
    labelled it invented.

15. MINOR. "If you can search the web, search in steps 2 to 4 and give links with the date."
    Steps 2 to 4 are three steps whose whole content is search results. With no search they
    produce three lists of "not checked" and three assumed gates, a third of the session. The
    coach gives no shorter path for that case.

Blocking: 4. Minor: 11.
