# Friction

1. **BLOCKING.** "Leading option. The highest total. Break a tie by the goals in the order the profile lists them, then by the smaller, easier option." All three options totalled 0, including do nothing. The instructions do not say whether do nothing is a candidate in a tie, or whether an option must score above 0 to beat doing nothing (the scores are defined "than doing nothing", so a total of 0 can be read as "no better than doing nothing"). I read the tie-break literally: goal 1 removes do nothing, then the smaller option (the pilot) wins. The other reading ("a 0 total does not beat the baseline", or "do nothing is the smallest, easiest option") makes do nothing the recommendation. The Judgement calls section only asks for readings of "a limit, a score or an option's shape", so this guess is not one I was told to disclose.

2. **MINOR.** "tell them to save it as a file in this project (in a plain chat, to paste it into their next question)". I could not tell which setting I was in, so I gave both in one line.

3. **MINOR.** "Write each limit in the profile as a one-line pass-or-fail test". "The head baker already works six days" is a fact, not a stated rule. The instructions cover this ("If the limit is a worry rather than a rule ... mark the test 'assumed'"), and I disclosed it.

4. **MINOR.** "Up to 25,000 dollars to invest": the instructions give no guidance on whether a budget limit covers one-time spending only or also running costs such as wages. Disclosed as a judgement call; it does not change the leading option.

5. **MINOR.** "Score each remaining option ... as better (+1), same (0) or worse (-1)". The three-point scale gives a $2,000 capped pilot and a full three-shop service the same scores, so the decision falls entirely to the tie-break. Scoring the pilot 0 on burnout instead of -1 is defensible; disclosed as a judgement call, no change.

6. **MINOR.** Goal 2, "raise weekday afternoon sales": unclear whether catering orders count by when they are eaten, delivered or prepared. Disclosed as a judgement call; affects both catering options equally.

7. **MINOR.** "a fact that would change the leading option is 'not found'". Whether local office demand counts as such a fact took judgement (it would change C versus do nothing, not C versus B). I added the check, which is visible in the answer.

8. **MINOR.** "A fact you could not find is written 'not found'". The rule does not cover facts I chose not to search for to keep research proportionate (the full setup cost). I wrote "not found (not researched)".

9. **MINOR.** "Every fact gets a source and its publication date, or the date you read it if the page has none." The convention-centre menu has a "valid until" date rather than a publication date. I gave both the validity date and the read date.

10. **MINOR.** Step 4 asks for a SWOT with each point's origin, and step 5 asks for "SWOT: a two-by-two table" in the brief, while saying "keep steps 1 to 4 above it brief". It is unclear whether the SWOT should appear twice. I wrote it in step 4 and a shorter version in the brief.

11. **MINOR.** "No legal or tax advice: say when a question needs a professional, and who." The brief format has no slot for this. I added one line after the sources and put the calls in Next steps.

12. **MINOR.** "a smaller version that tests the riskiest part of the idea cheaply. Design it to learn, not to win." Then the tie-break favours "the smaller, easier option", so the test option is structurally advantaged in ties. I did not shape the pilot to score better (it has the same offer as B, only smaller), but the rule makes the pilot hard to lose with.

Blocking: 1. Minor: 11.
