# Friction

1. **BLOCKING.** "If the limit is a worry rather than a rule, or you had to interpret the person's words, mark the test 'assumed'" together with "If you cannot tell whether it passes, keep it and mark it 'unknown'". The profile's limits are facts ("the head baker already works six days"), not tests. Turning that into "no hours added" is my reading; another reader could make it "no seventh day", which a pilot passes easily. The instructions also give no way to mark a test both "assumed" and "unknown", and no rule for whether an "unknown" result counts against an option's score. A different reading changes which options get "check first" and how strong the full service looks.

2. **BLOCKING.** "the person's idea; and, where one exists, a smaller version of the idea." I design the smaller version myself, and its design decides its scores. By aiming my pilot at afternoons, I gave it +1 on goal 2 and it won. A pilot designed around lunch would tie with the full service. Nothing tells me how to choose the smaller version's shape, or warns against designing it to fit the goals.

3. **BLOCKING.** "Score each remaining option against each goal ... as better (+1), same (0) or worse (-1) than doing nothing". Goal 1 is a target ("grow sales 10 percent"). It is unclear whether +1 means "moves toward the target" or "meets it". The pilot will not reach 10% alone; I scored +1 for moving toward it. Scoring by "meets it" gives 0 for both and changes the totals.

4. **MINOR.** "tell them to save it as a file in this project (in a plain chat, to paste it into their next question)". I could not tell whether the person is in a project or a plain chat, so I gave both instructions.

5. **MINOR.** "Show the work for each step under its own heading" and step 5's brief "In this order: ... SWOT: a two-by-two table." Step 4 already produces the SWOT, so it appears twice. I wrote it as a labelled list in step 4 and as the table in the brief. Not stated whether that duplication is intended.

6. **MINOR.** "Every fact gets a source and its publication date, or the date you read it if the page has none." The Altus page shows only "Updated" and the Canada.ca page shows two different dates (visible 2025-04-01, metadata 2026-10-07). I used the updated date and the visible date. No rule for these cases.

7. **MINOR.** "Find the facts that would change the decision: demand, costs, prices, competitors, rules that apply." Local demand, competitors and costs were all "not found". No rule says what to do when most decision-changing facts are missing: whether to still recommend, or to only list checks. I followed the "check first" path.

8. **MINOR.** "A widely known fact such as a tax rate still needs a source" and "No legal or tax advice: say when a question needs a professional, and who." Citing the 13% HST rate is a fact, but whether catered bakery goods are taxable is tax advice. The line between the two is mine to draw; I cited the rate and sent the rest to an accountant.

9. **MINOR.** "about 400 words, not counting the SWOT table and the sources" with "Why: three bullets" and "What would change this recommendation" also required in steps 3 and 5. The same material is written three times (step 3, SWOT, brief). "Keep steps 1 to 4 above it brief" gives no length for them.

10. **MINOR.** "Break a tie by the goals in the order the profile lists them". Not needed here, but unclear whether that means "highest score on goal 1 wins" or something else when options tie on goal 1 too.

Blocking: 3. Minor: 7.
