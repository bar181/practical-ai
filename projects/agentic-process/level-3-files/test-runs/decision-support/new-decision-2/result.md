## Step 5. The brief

**Decision:** For the second pickup needed April to November, should Greenline Yard Care keep renting, buy a used truck, or lease a new one?

**Recommendation:** Lease a new base-model pickup, but first check this year's rental invoices against two written lease quotes that state the km allowance.

**Why**
- Leasing is the cheapest option over the next twelve months: about $8,700 to $9,200 including insurance, against about $20,900 to $22,000 to rent for the season (estimates from a dealer offer of $148 a week and local rental rates of $2,394 to $2,520 per 4 weeks). That frees roughly $12,000 for the line of credit.
- It needs no down payment, and it lowers April spending, so it fits tight March and April cash flow better than buying.
- A new truck should lose less time to breakdowns than a used one (assumed), and it is there all winter for snow work.

**SWOT**

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|
| About $12,000 cheaper than renting in year one (estimate) | 48-month commitment |
| $0 down | About $641 a month even when idle in winter (estimate) |
| Available all year | Nothing owned at the end |

| Opportunities | Threats |
|---|---|
| Winter snow contracts | Km limits and overage charges not yet known |
| Base trim is cheapest | Insurance costs vary widely |
| Used truck stays a fallback | CVOR rules if truck plus trailer exceed 4,500 kg |

**Risks and how to reduce them**
1. **Km overage charges.** Get the allowance and per-km charge in writing; compare with the km on this year's rental agreements.
2. **Idle winter payments.** Price snow contracts the truck could serve, or choose the cheapest trim.
3. **A truck that cannot tow your trailer.** Give the dealer your trailer's loaded weight and ask for the tow package in the quote.

**Next steps (next 30 days)**
1. Total this year's rental invoices: cost, insurance, km driven.
2. Get two written lease quotes for a base pickup with towing, km allowance and early-end terms.
3. Get an insurance quote from your broker, and book an hour with your accountant on lease versus buy and HST.

**What would change this recommendation:** real rental cost under about $9,200 a season; a km allowance below what you drive; or keeping a truck four years or more without using the line of credit, which favours buying used.

**Assumptions:** "Up to $60,000" means the price or full lease commitment; you already run two trucks in season; a new truck breaks down less than a used one; the rental price does not include insurance; the dealer offer is still available; all figures are before 13% HST.

This is not tax or legal advice. Ask your accountant how a lease and a purchase are each treated for income tax and HST, and check with the Ministry of Transportation whether you need a CVOR certificate.
