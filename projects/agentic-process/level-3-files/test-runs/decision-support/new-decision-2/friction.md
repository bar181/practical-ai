# Friction

1. **BLOCKING.** "Score each remaining option against each goal in the profile as better (+1), same (0) or worse (-1) than doing nothing, over the next twelve months" together with "If a longer view would change a score, say so." This is a 48-month lease against a truck you own. On the twelve-month view leasing wins (+1 against -2). On a four-year view, buying used costs about the same and leaves a truck, so goal 3 could flip. The instructions say to mention the longer view but not whether it can affect the leading option. A person with low risk might well want the longer view to count.

2. **BLOCKING.** "the person's idea; and, where one exists, a smaller version of the idea." The person named two ideas (buy used, lease new), and it is not said how to define an option they named only in a few words. I took "buy used" as a cash purchase in year one. A financed used truck could have low yearly payments and score +1 on the line of credit, which could change the leading option. The instructions give no rule for choosing between versions of an option the person named loosely.

3. **MINOR.** "List two to four options: always 'do nothing' ...; the person's idea; and, where one exists, a smaller version of the idea." With two ideas from the person, it is unclear whether a smaller version is still expected, or of which idea. I listed three options and said why there is no smaller version.

4. **MINOR.** "Write each limit in the profile as a one-line pass-or-fail test." "We can spend up to 60,000 dollars" could mean up-front cash, spending per year, or the total commitment. All options pass under each reading here, so the result does not change, but the test had to be marked "assumed".

5. **MINOR.** "If the limit is a worry rather than a rule ... mark the test 'assumed' ... Drop an option that fails a test." It does not say whether an option failing an "assumed" test is dropped or kept until the person confirms the test. No option failed here, so it did not matter.

6. **MINOR.** "Find the facts that would change the decision: demand, costs, prices..." The most important figure, what the business pays now for the rental, is in their own records, not on the web. The instructions only allow a question at framing ("If the question is too vague to frame, ask one question and stop"), so there is no step for asking for a missing figure from the person. I used market rental rates and wrote the real figure as "not found".

7. **MINOR.** "Show the work for each step under its own heading" and step 4 "SWOT for the recommended option", then step 5 "SWOT: a two-by-two table" inside the brief, which "stands on its own". The SWOT therefore appears twice. I kept a sourced version in step 4 and a short version in the brief.

8. **MINOR.** "A widely known fact such as a tax rate still needs a source" next to "No legal or tax advice". HST applies to every option, but whether a business recovers it changes the real cost. The instructions do not say whether costs should be compared with or without tax. I compared before HST and sent the tax question to the accountant.

9. **MINOR.** "tell them to save it as a file in this project (in a plain chat, to paste it into their next question)." The chatbot cannot tell whether it is in a project or a plain chat, so I gave both instructions.

10. **MINOR.** "First, check ... when any of these is true: risk in the profile is low; a fact that would change the leading option is 'not found'; or the option is marked 'unknown' on a limit." With low risk set, every recommendation for this business gets a check first, and it is not said whether one check should cover several triggers. I used one check covering both "not found" facts.

Blocking: 2. Minor: 8.
