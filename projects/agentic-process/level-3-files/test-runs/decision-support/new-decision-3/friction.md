# Friction

1. **BLOCKING.** "Score each remaining option against each goal in the profile as better (+1), same (0) or worse (-1) than doing nothing ... with one line of reason." There is no rule for scoring when the fact behind a score is "not found" or rests on a doubtful source. The only lease figure found ($175 a week, Carpages) does not say whether it is a lease or a loan. I scored the lease +1 on goal 3 using that figure as an estimate. Scoring it 0 ("cannot tell") would tie lease with renting, and the tie-break would make keep renting the leading option. The "Judgement calls" rule covers how something was "read", not how missing data is scored, so the instructions did not require me to disclose this (I disclosed it anyway).

2. **MINOR.** "where one exists, a smaller version that tests the riskiest part of the idea cheaply." With two named ideas (buy used, lease new) it is unclear whether a smaller version is wanted for each, for one, or at all, and what counts as one. I added none and listed it as a judgement call ("an option's shape").

3. **MINOR.** "If the limit is a worry rather than a rule, or you had to interpret the person's words, mark the test 'assumed'." "We can spend up to 60,000 dollars" could mean up front, per year or total (a 48-month lease versus an 84-month loan matters). I read it as total and marked it assumed; it did not change the result.

4. **MINOR.** "cash flow is tight in March and April" has no pass-or-fail form, and the instructions let me make option-shape proposals (purchase date, lease start) that could make an option pass by construction. I marked both B and C "unknown" and kept them; disclosed as assumed.

5. **MINOR.** "Where a limit, a score or an option's shape depended on how you read something, take the plainer reading." "Plainer" is not defined. "Pay down our line of credit" as the line-of-credit balance versus all debt flips the result; it is disclosed as a judgement call, so it stays minor.

6. **MINOR.** "Strengths and weaknesses come from the profile or the option itself; opportunities and threats come from the research." The next sentence allows a point to come from "an estimate, a proposal, or 'assumed'", which contradicts "come from the research". I included one assumed opportunity (snow clearing) and labelled it.

7. **MINOR.** "Show the work for each step under its own heading" and "keep steps 1 to 4 above it brief" and the brief "stands on its own, so it repeats the key points". The SWOT is required both as step 4 and inside the brief. I put it once, in the brief, and pointed step 4 to it; another reader might print it twice.

8. **MINOR.** "Every fact gets a source and its publication date, or the date you read it if the page has none." The AutoTrader page reports Q3 2026 but its metadata says 14 July 2026, before the quarter ended. I gave the report period and the date read.

9. **MINOR.** "No legal or tax advice" alongside "Find the facts that would change the decision: ... rules that apply" and "A widely known fact such as a tax rate still needs a source." Tax treatment of lease versus purchase (and HST on a purchase) could change the decision, but the instructions say not to advise. I wrote it as "not found" and referred it to an accountant; the $60,000 cap for used was framed as all-in to avoid needing a tax rate.

10. **MINOR.** "If it came in the message, write it up ... tell them to save it as a file in this project (in a plain chat, to paste it into their next question)." The chatbot cannot tell which surface it is on. I gave both instructions.

11. **MINOR.** "about 400 words, not counting the SWOT table and the sources" while nine required sections (including Assumptions and "What would change") must fit. Mine runs about 430 words; the target and the required content pull against each other.

Blocking: 1. Minor: 10.
