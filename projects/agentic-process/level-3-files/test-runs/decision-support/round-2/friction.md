# Friction with the instructions

1. **BLOCKING.** "If a fact that would decide between the options is "not found", recommend the smallest step that would find it out, such as a trial or a few calls."
   The smallest step is "a few calls" to office managers. Calls cost nothing, but they measure stated interest, not real orders or workload. A trial measures both, but it is bigger. Reading the line strictly, the recommendation would be "make 10 calls", not "run a trial". I chose the trial and made the calls its first next step. A different reader would recommend something different.

2. **BLOCKING.** "The limits in the profile are pass or fail."
   One of the person's "limits" is a fact, not a rule: "the head baker already works six days." The instructions do not say how to turn that kind of limit into pass or fail. I read it as "no option may add to the head baker's days". On that reading the full launch fails and the trial passes. A stricter reading, "the team cannot take on any extra production", would fail the trial too and make "not now" the answer.

3. **MINOR.** "Score each option against each criterion as better, same or worse than doing nothing."
   The criteria include limits, and limits are pass or fail, not better, same or worse. Step 1 puts goals and limits together as criteria; step 3 needs two different scales. I used both in one table.

4. **MINOR.** "recommend the one that is better on the most goals"
   This counts only "better". A "worse" score never counts against an option. Both B and C were worse on burnout, and the rule ignored it. "Do nothing" also scores zero by definition, so it can only win if every other option breaks a limit. The result did not change here, but the rule cannot weigh a cost against a gain.

5. **MINOR.** "taking the goals in the order the profile lists them to break a tie. If still tied, take the smaller, easier step."
   With only three marks (better, same, worse), a full launch and a trial score the same on every goal. The scale cannot show size. Size was decided only by the last tie-break.

6. **MINOR.** "5. How much risk you are comfortable with: low, medium or high."
   The instructions collect risk appetite but never use it in the decision rule. I could not tell whether "medium" should change anything, so it changed nothing.

7. **MINOR.** "List two to four options"
   The person named one idea, "a weekday catering service", without a scope. The content of each option, and so whether it passes a limit, was entirely my proposal. The instructions do not say whether options should come from the person's words or be designed.

8. **MINOR.** "Each point says where it comes from: the profile, a source, a proposal, or "assumed"."
   That list has no "estimate". Step 2 asks for estimates to be labelled, but the SWOT has no label for them. I kept estimates out of the SWOT.

9. **MINOR.** "Find the facts that would change the decision: demand, costs, prices, competitors, rules that apply." versus "No legal or tax advice."
   It is unclear how far to go on "rules that apply". Quoting the Ontario food regulation is research. Saying whether it applies to the bakery's items is close to legal advice. I quoted the rule and referred the question to Hamilton Public Health Services.

10. **MINOR.** "Every fact gets a source and its publication date"
    There is no rule for sources from another country. Most demand data found was US data from ezCater, in an unstated currency. I labelled it "US data" and kept its dollar figures out of my own estimates, except as context.

11. **MINOR.** "Short enough to read in two minutes: about 400 words plus the SWOT table and the sources. It stands on its own"
    The brief must hold nine sections and repeat the key points, and that leaves little room. It is also unclear whether the person receives only the brief or the whole answer with steps 1 to 4 shown. "Show the work for each step under its own heading" makes the full answer several pages long, against "a one-page brief". I sent everything, with the brief as step 5.

12. **MINOR.** "Look for a business profile in this project's files"
    In this test I could not open project files. The profile was in the message, so it did not matter.

13. **MINOR.** "write it up under the heading "Business profile" at the top of your answer and suggest they save it in this project."
    In the no-profile case the instruction is to "tell the person to save it as a file in this project". Here it only says "suggest". The wording differs between the two cases, and it is unclear whether that is on purpose.

Blocking: 2. Minor: 11.
