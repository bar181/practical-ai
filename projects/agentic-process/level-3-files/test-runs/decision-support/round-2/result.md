# Step 5. The brief

**Decision:** Should Harbour Lane Bakery start a weekday catering service for offices near its three Hamilton shops?

**Recommendation:** Not a full service yet. Run a capped eight-week catering trial from one shop, then decide with your partner.

**Why**

- A full launch would likely add production to a head baker who already works six days, which breaks one of your limits (assumed).
- The trial aims straight at weekday afternoon sales (an afternoon treat tray and afternoon delivery slot), costs at most $3,000 (proposal), and also adds some sales.
- The deciding fact, whether offices near you will actually order, could not be found. Catering would need about $560 of orders every weekday to deliver your whole 10% goal alone (estimate). A trial finds out whether that is realistic.

**SWOT**

| | Helps | Hurts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Inside** | Three Hamilton shops and an existing range (profile). Existing products only, $3,000 cap (proposal). | Head baker at six days, burnout risk (profile). No delivery routine or vehicle (assumed). |
| **Outside** | 78.8% of Canadian workers fully on site [1]. Office vacancy falling [2]. Mid-afternoon treat habit, US data [4]. | Short-notice orders, 30% in US data [5]. At least 20 Hamilton caterers [7]. Food temperature rules in transport [8]. |

**Risks and how to reduce them**

1. **Burnout.** Cap orders at 10 a week, require 24 hours' notice, and keep the head baker out of the trial (proposal).
2. **Food safety and licensing.** Keep the trial to baked goods and coffee. Ask Hamilton Public Health Services which items count as potentially hazardous before the first order.
3. **Hidden costs.** Ask your accountant how HST applies to catering and delivery, and your insurance broker about delivering in a staff car. Keep spending under $3,000 (proposal).

**Next steps** (all within 30 days)

1. List the offices within a short walk of each shop. Pick the shop with the most, and call or visit 10 office managers to ask what they order now, when, and for how many.
2. Cost the three trial items using your own ingredient and labour costs. Set prices, using the convention centre's $19.95 a person breakfast as one reference point.
3. Agree the order cap with the head baker and your partner, and make the three calls in Risk 2 and Risk 3. Then start the trial.

**What would change this recommendation:** if the trial shop reaches about $190 a weekday with no added overtime (proposal threshold), plan a full launch costed under $25,000. If it averages under $50 a weekday, or overtime rises, stop.

**Assumptions**

- "Head baker already works six days" means no option may add to their days.
- The trial can be produced by staff other than the head baker.
- Shop kitchens have room for the extra work.
- You have no delivery vehicle.
- There are about 250 weekdays a year.
- The US survey figures are a fair guide for Hamilton offices. They may not be.
