# Friction

1. **BLOCKING.** "When the question is about starting something new, also include a short, capped trial of it". Is switching from renting to buying or leasing "something new"? I said yes and added a trial. Saying no would leave three options, and the lease (C) would win outright.

2. **BLOCKING.** "recommend a short, capped trial that would show whether people actually buy or use it". This is written for a product or service that customers buy. It does not fit an equipment decision, where there is nothing to trial and no one "buys" anything. I made the trial "get quotes, lease only if they pass", which is a gated decision rather than a real trial.

3. **BLOCKING.** "Recommend the option with the highest total." versus "Risk sets the size of the step: low, start with a short trial". It does not say which rule wins when the highest scorer is a full commitment and risk is low. Here a tie hid the conflict. Without the tie, one reader would recommend the lease and another the trial.

4. **BLOCKING.** "score each remaining option against each goal as better (+1), same (0) or worse (-1) than doing nothing". There is no time horizon. I used the profile's twelve months, which counts a used truck as -1 on the line of credit. Over three seasons, buying used is likely cheapest (estimate E5), so a longer horizon would flip that score.

5. **BLOCKING.** "write each limit in the profile as a one-line pass-or-fail test". "Cash flow is tight in March and April" is a worry, not a rule. I read it as "no extra cash out in March and April combined", and the lease passes. A strict reading ("no new payment in March") would drop the lease, and the lease trial with it.

6. **MINOR.** "always include 'do nothing' or 'not now'". The person's own option "keep renting" is the do-nothing option. The instructions do not say whether to merge the two, as I did, or list them separately.

7. **MINOR.** "we can spend up to 60,000 dollars" (the profile) has to become a test, but the instructions give no help on whether it is per year, per decision or a total. All the options I scored pass under any reading. Only a new-truck purchase, which nobody asked about, would fail.

8. **MINOR.** "Drop any option that fails a limit". This does not cover an option that passes only on a condition. The used truck passes the March and April test only if it is bought before 1 March. I kept it and stated the condition.

9. **MINOR.** "tell them to save it as a file in this project (in a plain chat, to paste it into their next question)". I cannot tell whether I am in a project or a plain chat, so I gave both instructions.

10. **MINOR.** "Strengths and weaknesses come from the profile or the option itself" versus "Each point says where it comes from: the profile, a source, an estimate, a proposal, or 'assumed'". "The option itself" is not one of the allowed labels, so I labelled those points "assumed".

11. **MINOR.** "Show the work for each step under its own heading" versus the brief, which "stands on its own, so it repeats the key points". Steps 4 and 5 both hold the SWOT. I put the SWOT only in the brief and pointed to it from Step 4, to avoid printing it twice. The whole answer is still well over two minutes to read.

12. **MINOR.** "Every fact gets a source and its publication date". It is unclear whether common facts, such as the Ontario HST rate, need a source too. I left tax out of the arithmetic rather than state an unsourced figure, so every estimate is "before tax".

13. **MINOR.** It does not say how to score a gated or trial option whose result depends on a check not yet made. I scored the trial the same as the option it leads to (+1). Scoring it 0 would hand the win to the lease, if risk did not override.

Blocking: 5. Minor: 8.
