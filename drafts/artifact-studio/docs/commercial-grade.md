# Commercial grade: what a commercial build adds

This folder explains. It never holds a project.

The levels in this repository get one person or a small team to a good page. An organisation that
publishes pages every day, under a brand that other people depend on, needs more than a page that
looks right.

**Think of it as** the difference between a good home kitchen and a restaurant kitchen. The food
can be the same. The restaurant adds inspection, consistency across cooks, and a record of what
was served.

## What changes

| Area | In this repository | In a commercial build |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brand | One design spec, kept by one person | A design system with tokens, approved components and an owner |
| Voices | Seven cards anyone can edit | Approved voices per audience, with examples and a review step |
| Content | The person checks facts by reading | Facts traced to a source of record, with a log of who approved what |
| Accessibility | A checklist and standard controls | A full audit against a published standard, on real assistive technology |
| Review | The author looks once | A named reviewer signs off before publishing |
| Publishing | The person decides, by hand | A release step with roles, a rollback and an audit trail |
| Measurement | None | What was opened, what was acted on, and which version |
| Scale | One page at a time | Many pages from one data source, kept in step when it changes |
| Legal | Examples are synthetic | Licences for fonts, images and libraries are tracked. Claims are reviewed |

## What does not change

- No invented facts.
- A person approves anything that goes out.
- The recipe, voice and look are still chosen in that order.

## Signs you have outgrown the levels here

- Two people made pages for the same customer and they did not match.
- Someone published a page with a wrong number, and nobody can say who checked it.
- Pages need to be updated when the underlying data changes.
- A customer or a regulator asks how a page was produced.
