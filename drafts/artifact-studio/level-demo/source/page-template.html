One photo, twelve components

Every effect on this page is built from a single lake photo. Each one comes with the sentence that asks for it.

Pick a component, copy its sentence, attach your own photo and paste both into Claude or ChatGPT. That is the whole method.

Nothing here loads a library. It is one file of HTML, CSS and a little JavaScript, so each effect is small enough for an assistant to write in one reply.

Pan and zoom

A still photo that moves slowly reads as film. Use it to open a page or to hold a quote.

Ask for it like this

Fill the frame with my photo and zoom in very slowly toward the island, like a documentary shot. Loop it back out.
Effort
Easy. One sentence.
Made here with
One CSS animation
Grow into

Seen in

Scrolling photo tour

The photo stays put while the text scrolls. Each paragraph moves the camera to the part it talks about.

1 of 7

  1. Start wide

    One frame, 2016 by 1512 pixels, taken from the shore under full cloud.

  2. The sky does most of the work

    Cloud fills the top 57% of the picture. That much even tone is why text sits on it so easily.

  3. The far shore

    A band of evergreens crosses the frame just past halfway down. It is the darkest strip in the photo.

  4. The island

    To the right of the trunk a small wooded island stands taller than the shoreline behind it.

  5. The detail you would miss

    A single bare shrub stands in open water. Zooming is how a page says: look here.

  6. The tree

    The trunk runs the full height of the frame and splits it into a wide part and a narrow part.

  7. The reeds

    Dry cattails and fallen leaves fill the bottom third. They are the only warm colours in the picture.

Ask for it like this

Pin my photo while I scroll. I will give you seven short paragraphs. For each one, zoom the photo to the part it describes.
Effort
Moderate. You say where to look; expect one round of nudging the zooms.
Made here with
Sticky positioning and an IntersectionObserver
Grow into
Scrollama, the step-trigger library used in newsrooms
Seen in
The Pudding, and the New York Times feature Snow Fall

Self-drawing line art

A sketch that draws itself as you scroll, then gives way to the real photo. The treeline, island and trunk were traced from the photo's own pixels.

Keep scrolling. Far shore first, then the tree, then the reeds, then the photo.

Ask for it like this

Trace the main shapes in my photo as a simple line drawing. Draw the lines as I scroll, far things first, then fade the real photo in underneath.
Effort
Harder. The assistant has to analyse the photo. Ask for a looser sketch if the first one is off.
Made here with
SVG paths and stroke-dashoffset, tied to scroll
Grow into
GSAP DrawSVG or Anime.js; Lottie or Rive for animation drawn by a designer
Seen in
Explainer intros and Codrops demos

Chart from the photo

A chart is a component too. These numbers are measured from the photo, so the chart and the picture tell the same story.

Ask for it like this

Measure how bright my photo is from top to bottom and chart it as a line with a soft fill. Put a strip of the real colours under the axis. Label only the highest and lowest points, and let me touch the chart to read the rest.
Effort
Moderate. The chart is easy; ask the assistant to show how it measured.
Made here with
Hand-written SVG, sized to your screen
Grow into
Observable Plot for quick charts, D3 for custom ones, ECharts for dashboards
Seen in
The Pudding's data stories

Swipe gallery

Cards in a row that snap into place one at a time. On a phone it is the most natural way to browse a set.

The island

Evergreens on a low rise, right of the trunk.

The shrub

Alone in open water, easy to miss at full size.

The bark

The trunk and a few bare branches against cloud.

The cattails

Dry stalks and seed heads along the near shore.

The far shore

A thin dark line of trees between sky and water.

The cloud

Soft bands of grey and blue, most of the picture.

Ask for it like this

Free tools to grow into

  • GSAPThe standard for scroll and timeline animation. Free with every plugin, under its own no-charge licence.
  • MotionAnimation for React, Vue and plain JavaScript. MIT licence.
  • Anime.jsSmall and friendly, strong on SVG. MIT licence.
  • ScrollamaStep triggers for scrollytelling. MIT licence.
  • LenisSmooth scrolling that other effects sit on top of.
  • Built into browsersScroll-driven animations and view transitions now work without any library.
  • Observable PlotA chart in a few lines. ISC licence.
  • D3Total control for custom charts. ISC licence.
  • Apache EChartsA large catalogue of interactive charts. Apache 2.0 licence.
  • shadcn/uiAccessible components you copy into your project.
  • Magic UIAnimated components for landing pages. MIT licence.
  • Aceternity UIDramatic, dark, cinematic components.
  • React BitsAnimated text, backgrounds and cards with open source.
  • Three.js, Lottie and RiveFor 3D scenes and for animation drawn by a designer.
