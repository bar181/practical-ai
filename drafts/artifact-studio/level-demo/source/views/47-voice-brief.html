Decision brief: a one-hour standard for first replies

To
The owners
From
Office manager
Date
12 May
Decision needed by
26 May
Reading time
Three minutes

Decision required

Approve option C: every enquiry receives a first reply within one working hour, drafted automatically and sent by a member of staff. Cost: about $150 a month.

Background

1.Enquiries reach us by web form, email and telephone. Before the trial our median first reply took 26 hours, and 12% of enquiries were answered within the hour.

2.From February to April we trialled drafted replies. Median reply time fell to 38 minutes, 81% of enquiries were answered within the hour, and booked site visits rose from 18 to 31 a month.

3.The trial ends on 31 May. Without a decision we return to the previous arrangement.

Options

OptionCost a yearExpected resultMain risk
A. Return to the previous arrangementNoneReplies in about a day. Visits return to about 18 a month.Lost work we never hear about
B. Hire a part-time coordinatorAbout $26,000Replies within the hour in office hoursCover for holidays and illness
C. Drafted replies, sent by staff (recommended)About $1,800As measured in the trialA wrong draft sent unread

Recommendation

4.Option C. It delivers the result of option B at about one fifteenth of the cost, and it has already been tested here for 90 days.

Risks and controls

  • A draft is sent without being read. Control: nothing sends without a named member of staff pressing send. Reviewed monthly.
  • The first reply promises too much. Control: first replies confirm and offer a visit. They never state a price.
  • The result was seasonal. Control: measure again in November and report to the owners.

Next steps if approved

5.Standard takes effect 1 June. Office manager reports reply times and visits on the first Monday of each month.

Example brief. The company, the dates and every figure are invented for this demo.

Behind this page

The executive brief voice exists to get a decision from busy people. It is the plainest voice here on purpose. It follows the convention of a board or cabinet decision memo. The company and figures are invented for the demo.

How it is built

  1. A header: to, from, date, and when the decision is needed
  2. The decision, stated before anything else
  3. Background in numbered paragraphs, facts only
  4. Options in one table, including "do nothing"
  5. The recommendation and the reason in two sentences
  6. Risks, each with its control
  7. What happens next if the answer is yes

Design rules

  • It looks like a document: one column, serif text, a paper page
  • No images and no animation. Movement would cost trust.
  • Numbered paragraphs, so people can say "see paragraph 2"
  • One table, with the recommended row shaded
  • One accent colour, used for rules and headings
  • Short enough to read in three minutes, and it says so
