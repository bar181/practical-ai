Where people go wrong

  • Letting it send by itself. A person approves every reply. No exceptions.
  • Putting a price in the first reply. Confirm, offer a time, stop.
  • Skipping the test on old enquiries. Five tests find most of the problems.

Behind this page

The practical instructor voice gets someone doing the thing within the hour. A best guess at Bradley Ross's teaching style: core concept, one analogy, steps for today. The company and figures are invented for the demo.

How it is built

  1. The core idea in one sentence
  2. One analogy, boxed so it can be found again
  3. Three numbers that say why it matters
  4. Numbered steps with a time on each, and boxes to tick
  5. The same job at three levels
  6. The common mistakes
  7. One next step, small enough to do now

Design rules

  • It should feel like a field guide or a workbook, not a brochure
  • Short sentences that start with a verb
  • Everything the reader does is a control they can touch
  • Numbers only on steps that happen in order
  • Two colours: one for structure, one that means "act"
  • Tables for comparing levels, never paragraphs
