Colour

Most pages need fewer colours than people expect: a background, a text colour, a few quiet greys and one colour that means "act here". These five ideas are enough to choose them well.

  • Mostly quiet, a little loud. About 60 parts background, 30 parts text and structure, 10 parts accent.
  • One accent. It marks what to act on. If it is also used for decoration, it stops meaning anything.
  • Greys with a trace of your colour. Pure grey looks dead beside a brand colour. A slight tint makes them belong together.
  • Check every pair of text and background. 4.5 to 1 for ordinary text, 3 to 1 for large text, 7 to 1 if you can.
  • Never colour alone. If red means late, the word "late" is there too.

60, 30, 10

A rule borrowed from decorating a room. The largest share is calm, the next gives structure, and the smallest draws the eye. Judge it by eye: it is about weight, not a count of pixels. On a dense screen such as a dashboard the calm share is nearer 90.

Contrast you can read

Contrast is a ratio between the text and what is behind it, from 1 to 1 (invisible) up to 21 to 1 (black on white). Ordinary text needs 4.5. Two of these four pairs pass. The other two use colours that look fine side by side and still fail as text.

16.0 to 1. Passes.

7.5 to 1. Passes.

3.3 to 1. Fails for ordinary text.

1.4 to 1. Fails for ordinary text.
