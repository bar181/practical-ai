Seven social formats

The same message cut to the shapes people post: a square, a portrait, a story, a three-slide carousel and a link preview. Each is a drawing you can download as a picture. The company is invented.

  • Square post 1080 by 1080

    A claim or a quote. Works in every feed. Keep it to one sentence.

  • Portrait stat 1080 by 1350

    One number and what it means. Portrait takes more of the screen than a square.

  • Story 1080 by 1920

    Full screen and upright. Keep words out of the top and bottom sixth, where apps put their own buttons.

  • Carousel, slide 1 1080 by 1350

    A swipe-through post. Slide one is the hook and must stand alone.

  • Carousel, slide 2 1080 by 1350

    The middle slides carry one idea each. Number them.

  • Carousel, slide 3 1080 by 1350

    The last slide is the ask. One action only.

  • Link preview 1200 by 630

    The picture shown when your page is shared as a link. Wide, with the title large enough to read small.

Behind these formats

A post is read in about a second, on a phone, among other posts. So each format carries one idea in very large type, and the brand is a small line at the bottom. The sizes are the common ones. Platforms change their preferred sizes, so check the platform before a campaign.

How they are built

  1. Each format is one SVG drawing at its real pixel size
  2. Text is set line by line, so nothing reflows by surprise
  3. The download button writes the fonts into the file, then saves a PNG at full size
  4. The story reads its photograph from the page, so swapping the page photo swaps the story

Design rules

  • One idea per picture. If it needs two, make a carousel
  • The first carousel slide must make sense alone
  • Seven words or fewer in the headline
  • Keep words away from the edges, and out of the top and bottom sixth of a story
  • Strong contrast: most people see it at thumbnail size first
  • The last slide asks for one action
