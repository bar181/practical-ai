# Example: a one-page report

A worked example: what was asked, what came with it, what the studio chose and what came back. The business and every figure are invented.

## The request

> Turn my October notes into one page I can send to my business partner. She reads on her phone and wants the answer first. Our colour is green.

## The notes that came with it

- We are a landscaping company with two crews
- October revenue $84,200. September was $71,500
- Jobs completed: 23 in October, 19 in September
- Quotes that became jobs: 46%, up from 38%
- We started sending quotes within two days of the visit. It used to take six
- One van was off the road for four days. Crews shared. No jobs moved
- November: hire one seasonal worker for leaf clearance
- Need to decide about a second van by 15 November

## What the studio picked

For "say how something went", "a busy decision-maker" and "a web page", the picker chose **One-page report in the analyst voice** (strong fit, 30 of 30). One-page report: a strong fit for "say how something went", a strong fit for "a busy decision-maker". Analyst voice: a strong fit for "say how something went", a strong fit for "a busy decision-maker".

## How it was made

The assistant built on the skill's page skeleton. The colours come from the palette rule with a green hue of 140, which gives seven colours and checks each text and background pair. The three figures are stat cards and the two next steps are action cards, both from the design ideas.

## The output

- [`output.html`](output.html): the page. One file, no script, reads on a phone

## The note that came back with it

- **Chose:** the one-page report in the executive brief voice, because she wants the answer first. Colours built from your green.
- **Assumed:** that faster quotes are the main cause of the rise. Your notes put the two side by side but do not say so.
- **Missing:** profit. The page says so in its last section.
- **Check before sharing:** the sentence that says why revenue rose. It is the one claim on the page that is an inference.

## What a good result must have

1. The headline is the answer, not a title such as "October report"
2. The three figures match the notes, and each says what it was before
3. Change is written in words, not shown by colour alone
4. Profit is marked as missing, because the notes gave no costs
5. On a phone nothing scrolls sideways and all text is readable
6. The page shows everything with scripts turned off
