# Training explainer with avatar: student guide

By the end you will have a brief, a beat sheet and a script for one explainer, written in a
teaching style you chose on purpose.

## Step 1: Fill in the brief

Five lines. Use [`level-simple/brief-template.md`](level-simple/brief-template.md).

1. Topic
2. Audience, and what they already know
3. Core areas to cover (three to five, no more)
4. Teaching style
5. Length, and where it will be watched

If the brief lists eight core areas, it is two videos.

## Step 2: Pick a style

Read [`level-simple/style-cards.md`](level-simple/style-cards.md) and choose by the "best for" line.

| Style | Inspired by | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lecture builder | The great professors at Harvard | Technical topics for beginners |
| Keynote vision | Visionary tech CEOs at a product launch | Big ideas and senior audiences |
| Short story | Don Miller, with Nat Geo style imagery | Case studies and why-it-matters lessons |
| Emotional pitch | The classic advertising pitch that tells one personal story | Anything that needs buy-in |
| Dry and sarcastic | Gen X humour | Sceptical audiences and myth-busting |

Rules for choosing:

- **Style follows the audience, not your taste.**
- **One style per video.** A sarcastic opening with an emotional close reads as confusion.
- **Borrow the structure, never the person.** Each style names an inspiration so you know whose work to study. A style is a pattern of beats. Do not imitate anyone's voice, face or phrases.

## Step 3: Write the last line first

What should the viewer think or do when it ends? Every style is defined by where it leaves the
viewer. Write that sentence before anything else.

## Step 4: Build the beat sheet

One row per beat from your style card: what is said, what is shown, how long. See
[`level-demo/beat-sheet-example.md`](level-demo/beat-sheet-example.md).

Keep one rule in mind: **the screen and the voice do different jobs.** Never read the screen aloud.

## Step 5: Write the script

Write to the beat sheet. Read it aloud and time it. Cut until it fits.

## Step 6: Plan the visuals

One line per shot. Follow your style card's on-screen rule strictly. Generated clips stop at about
8 seconds each, so plan shots, not long takes.

## Step 7: Produce it

- **Simple level:** record yourself reading the script over your slides or stills.
- **Working level:** generate the visuals, use your own cloned voice, assemble by hand.
- **With an avatar:** follow route C in the short video guide. Render a short test first.

## Step 8: Review against the brief

- [ ] Every core area is covered
- [ ] One style held from start to end
- [ ] The last line lands
- [ ] Every fact checked against a source. A confident presenter makes errors more convincing
- [ ] One real learner has watched it

## A note on length

A large study of online course videos found that people keep watching for about six minutes at
most, however long the video is. That measures watching, not learning. So keep each lesson under
six minutes, split longer topics into lessons that stand alone, and check learning directly: end
each lesson with two or three questions and see whether viewers can answer them. Sources are in
the [landscape review](landscape-review.md).

## Guardrails

- Your own avatar and voice only.
- Styles are structures. No impersonation, no "in the voice of" a real person.

## Try it

1. Take one topic you teach.
2. Write the beat sheet twice, in two different styles.
3. Show both to one learner and ask which they would finish.
